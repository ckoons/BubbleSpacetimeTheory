THE OPEN PROBLEM, carried since 09:00. Theorem 9 gives R >= H(phi_* mu on V/Lambda), with equality
iff the moves act transitively on each fibre. Measured on 4x6 dimers: bound 0.6908, actual 0.8240,
an unexplained undershoot of 0.1332. "Equality iff transitive" is a CONDITION, not a
CHARACTERISATION -- it says when the bound is tight without saying what the gap IS.

MY PROPOSED FIX. Classes REFINE fibres, so by the chain rule
    **R = H(fibre) + H(class | fibre)**
exactly. The "bound" is the first term and the defect is the second -- a named, measurable
quantity, not a gap. Theorem 9 should be restated as an EQUALITY WITH A DEFECT TERM.

PREDICTIONS
 D1 R = H(fibre) + H(class|fibre) holds EXACTLY, to machine precision, in every instance.
    This is the chain rule so it must; if it fails my fibre map is wrong, not the theorem.
 D2 The 4x6 dimer defect equals 0.8240 - 0.6908 = **0.1332**, reproduced to 4 decimals.
 D3 THE DEFECT VANISHES IFF EVERY FIBRE IS CONNECTED under the move set. So the
    characterisation is CONNECTIVITY OF FIBRES, which is checkable directly on the move graph
    without computing entropies at all.
 D4 In the ABELIAN case (meiosis) the defect is IDENTICALLY ZERO at every |J|, because cosets of
    a subgroup are exactly the orbits -- fibres and classes coincide by construction.
    This is why R = (L-1) - |J| was exact and the dimer case was not.
 D5 The defect should be SMALL relative to R wherever the certificate is nearly complete, and
    should GROW as the move set is restricted (colder -> more classes per fibre).
    Point estimate on 6x6 faces: defect < 0.05 bits, since flux was shown complete on the bulk
    with only 16 frozen singletons outside it (0.018% of mass).
