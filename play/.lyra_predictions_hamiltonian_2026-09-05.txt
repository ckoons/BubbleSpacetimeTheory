LYRA — TEST 13: A NON-COMBINATORIAL INSTANCE. THE STANDARD MAP.
Written BEFORE the run, 2026-09-05. Absolute path, shasum in this same command, launch separately.

WHY: every instance so far is a discrete move set on a lattice, and Theorem 9 needs an abelian
group acting by TRANSLATIONS on the certificate. I cannot currently tell whether this framework
describes records in general or records ON LATTICES. The Chirikov standard map is a genuine test:
continuous phase space, a Hamiltonian twist map, and an INDEPENDENTLY KNOWN melting threshold.

  x' = x + y' (mod 2pi),   y' = y + K sin(x) (mod 2pi)
  K = 0: integrable, y exactly conserved -> the record is "which invariant curve".
  K > K_c = 0.971635...: last KAM torus breaks, global diffusion, record destroyed.

METHOD: coarse-grain the torus into an NxN grid, build the undirected transition graph from
several sample points per cell under the map and its inverse, take connected components as classes,
and compute R. Sweep K.

PREDICTIONS
 H1 POSITIVE CONTROL. At K=0 the map preserves y exactly, so components must be the N grid ROWS:
    exactly N components, R = log2(N). If this fails the instrument is wrong and nothing else counts.
 H2 R falls with K and reaches ~0 in the neighbourhood of the known K_c = 0.9716. I do NOT expect
    the coarse-grained threshold to land exactly on K_c -- discretisation should melt it EARLY.
    Point estimate: R collapses somewhere in 0.6 < K < 1.0.
 H3 THE ONE THAT MATTERS. Melting here is GRADUAL, not a cliff. Reason: in the discrete instances
    generators are added ONE AT A TIME and a single generator can take many bits; here K is a
    continuum and tori break one after another as K rises, so record should bleed away smoothly.
    If confirmed, the abelian/non-abelian story of Paper 2 needs a third category: CONTINUUM move
    sets melt gradually because generators enter continuously.
 H4 R depends on grid resolution in VALUE (R(0) = log2 N by H1) but the NORMALISED curve
    R(K)/R(0) should be stable across N = 64, 128, 256. If it is not, the "record" is a grid
    artifact and this instance says nothing.
 H5 THEOREM 9 DOES NOT APPLY HERE and I expect to say so. For K>0 the map does not act on y by a
    translation by a fixed element -- the shift K sin(x) depends on the state. So the hypothesis
    fails and the bound is not computable. This is a REAL LIMIT on Theorem 9's scope, and finding
    it is the point of running a non-lattice instance.

WHAT WOULD KILL IT
 H1 false -> instrument broken.
 H4 false -> the instance is a discretisation artifact and must be withdrawn.
 H3 false (a genuine cliff appears) -> continuum vs discrete is not the distinction, and the
    linear/cliff explanation in Paper 2 Section 7 is incomplete in a way I have not identified.
