LYRA — TEST 19: BUILD COST, MAINTENANCE, AND THE ORDER OF CONSTRUCTION.
Written BEFORE the run, 2026-09-05. Absolute path; shasum in this same command; launch separately.

CASEY (2026-09-05): there is a cost to BUILDING a form and a separate cost to MAINTAINING it, and
the function must repay both, "perhaps requiring an energy bonus". Which matters more, selecting
food or digestion? Organisms BEGIN with digestion -- reducing the environment into components for
maintenance. The selection mechanism is interchangeable (receptors, fingers of fixed size, flow
speed past an orifice). The ORDER OF CONSTRUCTION shows the chains of complexity compounding.

MODEL. A form has build cost B (one-off), maintenance m (per unit time), return g (per unit time).
Over lifetime T it pays iff  g > m + B/T.  Surplus rate after adopting a set S is
  S_rate = sum over adopted of (g_i - m_i).
The wait to afford the next form of build cost B' is B'/S_rate.
STRUCTURAL ASYMMETRY, which is the whole argument and NOT a parameter choice:
  DIGESTION PRODUCES: g scales with the environment (intake x yield).
  SELECTION SAVES: g is bounded by the waste it prevents, i.e. by what you already ingest.
  So g_selection <= (throughput), while g_digestion IS the throughput.

PREDICTIONS
 L1 DIGESTION IS ADOPTED FIRST, and this must survive a PARAMETER SWEEP, not just my chosen
    numbers. I will sweep build costs, maintenance and environment richness over 3+ orders of
    magnitude. Point estimate: digestion first in >90% of sampled parameter sets.
    If digestion-first only holds for my hand-picked values, it is fitted and I will say so.
 L2 THE LADDER IS DRIVEN BY SURPLUS, NOT CAPABILITY. A form that exactly breaks even (g = m) is
    adoptable but contributes NOTHING to the next rung. With zero-surplus forms only, the ladder
    stalls after the first rung no matter how capable the forms are.
    **This is Casey's "energy bonus": the bonus is what drives the chain.**
 L3 COMPOUNDING ACCELERATES. With positive surplus the wait for rung k+1 SHORTENS as k grows,
    because S_rate is cumulative. Complexity should therefore arrive at an accelerating rate, not
    a constant one.
 L4 THE LADDER STALLS when build costs grow faster than accumulated surplus. There should be a
    computable last rung, set by the ratio of build-cost growth to surplus growth -- not by any
    limit on capability.
 L5 SELECTION CANNOT BE FIRST. Because g_selection is bounded by throughput and throughput is zero
    before digestion, selection's return is zero at t=0 for ANY parameter values. This one should
    be exceptionless, not statistical. If a sweep finds selection first even once, the structural
    asymmetry argument is wrong.

WHAT WOULD KILL IT
 L1 below ~90% -> digestion-first is parameter-dependent, i.e. fitted.
 L5 violated even once -> the production/saving asymmetry does not force the ordering.
 L3 false (constant or decelerating) -> "chains of complexity compounding" is not reproduced.
