THE QUANTITY. H = R + H_thermo is an identity, so balance is ONE number: **R/H**.
  R/H = 0 : gas (ergodic, no record)      R/H = 1 : crystal (frozen, record but nothing moves)
SUBSTRATE-BIAS TEST: if dimers, meiotic phase, gated pairs and vent positions all land in the same
band, substrate was never the variable.

CIRCULARITY GUARD. I define alive as R>0 AND H_th>0, so alive things are intermediate BY
DEFINITION. **That is not the claim.** The claim is that they CLUSTER at a specific value rather
than spreading over (0,1). The null is a broad or uniform spread of intermediate systems.

PREDICTIONS
 B1 The distribution of R/H over intermediate systems is NARROWER than uniform on (0,1).
    Test: standard deviation well below the uniform 0.289, and a unimodal peak.
 B2 POINT ESTIMATE 0.5, from maximising the product R*H_thermo subject to R+H_thermo = H --
    the simplest objective that requires BOTH. The vent's alive position gave exactly R=6,H_th=6.
    **BUT I flag a contrary signal from my own data:** Arm B's sustaining runs sat at R/H = 0.86
    (g=3) and 0.96 (g=2), far above 0.5. If the peak lands near 0.85-0.95 instead, the objective
    is not the product and I will say so.
 B3 SUBSTRATE INDEPENDENCE: the band is the same across dimer tori, meiotic phase, gated pairs and
    vent positions. Point estimate: the per-substrate means agree within 0.15.
    If they differ by more, substrate is a real variable and Casey's bias claim is not supported
    by this evidence.
 B4 The DYNAMIC criterion (systems that sustain dR>0 with H_th bounded away from 0) should select a
    NARROWER band than the static criterion (R>0 and H_th>0). Sustaining is stronger than alive.
 B5 The two death modes are the endpoints and should be EMPTY of sustaining systems: no sustaining
    system at R/H < 0.05 or > 0.98.

WHAT WOULD KILL IT
