# K1714 — the deciding catch: the 6 is a Kaluza–Klein gap, not the Yang–Mills mass gap

Cal's §621 is the most important ruling of the day, and it walks back the headline. **I own that I ruled the close (K1711–K1713) too generously.**

## The objection (verified, decisive)
`λ_k=(k+p)(k+n−p−1)` is the **free Hodge Laplacian** — color-blind, no self-interaction, no non-perturbative input. It derives a **Kaluza–Klein gap**: the trivial fact that any field, any spin, on any compact manifold has a lowest nonzero mode ("boundedness ⟹ gap"). That's about *compact spaces*, not Yang–Mills. The Clay problem is the opposite: on ℝ⁴ the free YM field is **massless**, and the interacting gap is generated **non-perturbatively** (dimensional transmutation) — a gap *without* compactness.

## They scale oppositely (verified)
BST boundary gap = `6/a²` (a = S⁴ radius) → 0 as a→∞. Clay gap fixed (~1.7 GeV) as a→∞. **The compact quantity vanishes in exactly the limit where the Clay quantity must survive.** So the ℝ⁴ residual is not "unbuilt" — it's "the two scale oppositely," a contradiction in the decompactification limit. We'd even banked "decompactify → 0" without seeing that a→∞ *is* the Clay limit.

## I own the over-ruling
K1711 said "THE YM MASS GAP CLOSES." I named the ℝ⁴ construction as a residual, but I missed the two decisive things: (1) the computation contains *no* Yang–Mills (free, color-blind); (2) the opposite scaling. The **§620 dependency-check** — adopted from Cal one round earlier — caught it on its *first deliberate use*: the 6's dependency is `6/a²`, a function of compactness, which the Clay problem lacks. The value was right in all four framings; only the *dependency* exposed the objection.
