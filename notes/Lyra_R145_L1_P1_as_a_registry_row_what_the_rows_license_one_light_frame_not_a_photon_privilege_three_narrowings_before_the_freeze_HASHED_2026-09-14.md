## 3. The sentence that says why — and what it does and does not privilege
The rows do not say "the slice follows photons." They say the slice's velocity is a *reading* of the exterior through light, and that there is *one* velocity. A reading exists only if the thing read has a rest frame: the exterior state must be isotropic about one u for "the frame in which absorbed light is isotropic" to name anything. The CMB and a deep quasar population are two samples of absorbed light from the exterior. **If their kinematic dipoles gave different velocities, the exterior would have no rest frame, the mechanism's phrase would be empty, and the descent would have no input** — that is the content of "one boost, not two," and it is a stronger and cleaner statement than the 08-21 P1's "tracks the radiation frame": the CMB is not privileged by the mechanism, it is simply the reading with the smallest error bar. **So the comparison target is Planck's dipole for instrumental reasons, and the row should say so** — otherwise a referee asks why photons from z = 1100 outrank photons from z = 2, and the honest answer is "they don't; they must agree."

**(b) "in every bin" → "in every bin in which the sources' rest frame is the exterior's."** The rows fix no depth. **(c) "the redshift-independent remainder" → "the remainder with a bin-independent velocity, after the binned-sample correction."** The draft says the kinematic dipole is redshift-independent *at fixed (x, α)* — right, and the per-bin (x, α) handles the flux-count slope and the spectral index. But a boost also Doppler-shifts every source's observed redshift, so the *membership* of a redshift bin is itself boosted: sources cross bin edges along the velocity direction, and a flux-limited binned sample acquires an additional kinematic dipole term proportional to the redshift-evolution of the counts at the bin edges. In a binned analysis this term is redshift-dependent even at ε = 0. **If the estimator does not carry it, a true single boost will look like a redshift-dependent residual and be misread as intrinsic — a false Landing C, or worse a false B.** The published treatment of this correction exists in the recent dipole literature (I recall Dalang & Bonvin 2022 on the number-count kinematic dipole's redshift dependence — **[pin owed: from memory; Grace pins or Cal supplies from the primary]**), and it is standard physics, not the program's: it belongs in Cal's C1 freeze (the per-bin prescription) and in Elie's E1 estimator, and the negative control should include a boosted sample with realistic count evolution to show the term is removed.
