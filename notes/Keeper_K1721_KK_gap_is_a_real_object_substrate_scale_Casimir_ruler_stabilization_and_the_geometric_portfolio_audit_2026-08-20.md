BST's compact object is the Shilov boundary `(S⁴×S¹)/ℤ₂`, radius `a = the ruler`, so `6/a²` is the mass² of the first excitation **of the substrate boundary itself** — Planckian (m_KK ~ 1.2×10¹⁹ GeV), the "graininess" scale of substrate discreteness. Three live readings, **none** the Yang–Mills gap:
1. **Substrate excitation scale** — the mass gap of the boundary/substrate, the scale of its discreteness (ties to the Koons tick).
2. **★ Casimir energy → Λ + ruler-stabilization** — the vacuum energy of the discrete KK spectrum. The literature's principal *use* of such a gap: (a) it sources dark energy / feeds Λ (BST's a₀ rung, #97); (b) it **stabilizes the compactification size** — which in BST *is the ruler a*. So the KK Casimir energy is a **candidate mechanism that fixes the one input** (the ruler stabilized, not merely posited). Genuine, deep, computable.
3. **Modulus stabilization** of the constant-holonomy zero mode (Cal's flagged modulus, K1713).
