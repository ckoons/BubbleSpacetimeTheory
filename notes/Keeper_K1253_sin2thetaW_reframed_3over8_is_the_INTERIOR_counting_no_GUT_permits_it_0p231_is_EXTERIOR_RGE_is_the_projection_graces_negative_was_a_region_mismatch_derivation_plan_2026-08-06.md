## Grace's result (as far as it goes)

## The reframe (Casey's insight, ruled correct)
**The 3/8 is a *counting fact* — a trace over the fermion content — not a GUT-specific result.** SU(5) is *one* story for why the content gives 3/8 (its embedding fixes the hypercharge normalization); but the trace itself is just the isospin/charge content of one generation, and **BST derives the generation content.** So BST supplies 3/8 *directly*, without SU(5). No-GUT preserved.

And it's exactly your interior/exterior frame: **3/8 = the discrete-interior value** (a counting, exact); **0.231 = the exterior-continuum measurement**; **the RGE running = the interior→exterior projection** (measured RGE, an external input like G in GR). "Modern physics sees only the exterior continuum; with BST we see the discrete interior" — so BST sees 3/8 where the SM sees only 0.231. **The "3/8 vs 0.231 conflict" is not a conflict — it's one object at two regions.** Grace's negative was a **region-mismatch**: she scored the interior 3/8 against the exterior 0.231 same-region — the exact error your "make sure we're same-region" directive forbids.

## The load-bearing crux (honest — don't over-swing)
The 3/8 needs a **fixed hypercharge normalization** (Tr(Q²) depends on it). In SU(5) the embedding fixes it — *that's what the GUT supplies*. Without a GUT, the U(1) normalization is a priori free. So the one concrete question: **does BST fix the hypercharge normalization geometrically** (from the domain's U(1)/SO(2) charge), giving 3/8 in the interior without SU(5)? **If yes → the interior 3/8 is forced, and sin²θ_W = 3/8-run-down is a genuine derivation** (interior forced + measured RGE). If no → it borrows the GUT normalization (forbidden), stays Identified. Promising, because BST claims to derive the gauge structure — the normalization plausibly comes from the SO(2)/U(1) in K = SO(5)×SO(2).

Does running 3/8 down from μ_geo give 0.231? **This is the same μ_geo problem under the up-Yukawa and the masses** — Grace's insight: "which scale the geometry speaks at is *one* problem, not several."
