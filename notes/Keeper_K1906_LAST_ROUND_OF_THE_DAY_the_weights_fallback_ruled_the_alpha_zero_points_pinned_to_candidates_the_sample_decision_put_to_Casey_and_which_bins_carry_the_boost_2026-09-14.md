On the unweighted G < 20.5 table at the α = 1 placeholder: f ≈ 2 + 2x + B → **bin 1: 2 + 1.64 + 4.86 ≈ 8.5**; bin 2 ≈ 6.1; bin 3 ≈ 4.9; bin 4 ≈ 2.7; **bin 5 ≈ −3.2.** So N f²: bin 1 ≈ 1.0×10⁷, bin 2 ≈ 8.1×10⁶, bin 3 ≈ 5.8×10⁶, bin 4 ≈ 1.4×10⁶, bin 5 ≈ 1.3×10⁶ — **the boost is carried by the low-redshift bins, and in bin 1 the boundary term B₁ ≈ 4.9 is larger than the Ellis–Baldwin terms 2 + x(1+α) ≈ 3.6 combined.** In Lyra's form: the clock window's edge term (edge density × edge speed) exceeds the Rac terms wherever n(z) is steep at an edge — rising into bin 1's upper edge, falling out of bin 5's lower edge — and the sign follows the sign of the edge density difference. The kinematic moment flips sign exactly where the window's lower-edge density exceeds the upper-edge density by more than (2 + x(1+α))/(1+z_edge) — a one-line criterion Lyra can state on the clock factor alone. Under observed-redshift binning the boost is read mostly off the *edges of the clock windows*, not off the Rac threshold — which is why (K1900 §4) the redshift channel windowed on flux, where no clock edge moves, is the cleaner reading, and why the joint fit needs both.
