Oppenheim's hybrid theory keeps the metric classical and couples it to quantum matter through a completely-positive classical–quantum (CQ) master equation; consistency then *forces* stochasticity in the classical sector — the metric diffuses — and the theory's signature is the trade-off between the decoherence rate of quantum matter and the diffusion of the classical field: for a measured classical quantity (the field at a point, or a test mass's weight read repeatedly) the CQ dynamics adds noise that is, in the simplest realisations, **Gaussian, unbounded, with a variance growing linearly in time** — a random walk with a diffusion constant whose lower bound is set by the decoherence rate. That is what "randomness" means there: an intrinsic stochastic increment, accumulating without limit, on a continuous classical variable. In this program the same phenomenon would be read as a **resolution limit**: a measurement is a write to the boundary (Lecture 3, T2630), a record is an idempotent that reads the same twice (T2543), and the boundary has a finite capacity — N_max = 137 independent channels (T186; Lecture 8's "computed invariant, a capacity") — with writes paced by the clock unit τ₀ = a₀/c (T1136, a definition). Three consequences follow from that reading before any number is computed, and each is the opposite of the CQ shape: **(i) bounded** — a record can carry at most a capacity's worth of distinguishable outcomes, so the fluctuation of a *re-read* field about its record is bounded by the resolution of one write, not by a growing walk; **(ii) scale-fixed** — the fluctuation's scale is set by the write's quantum (the tick τ₀ and the channel count, invariants the geometry names) and not by a diffusion constant that is a free parameter of the hybrid theory; **(iii) non-accumulating** — because the record is idempotent, repeated reads do not random-walk away from it: the variance of the reading sequence saturates at the write's resolution instead of growing linearly, and the one fluctuation statement the register already carries points the same way — the commitment count is Fermi–Dirac, sub-Poisson, with a Fano factor below one (T2546), and Λ is Pauli-frozen rather than everpresent (T2571). So the contrast a Section-D marker would state is: *CQ predicts a Gaussian, linearly growing, parameter-set variance on a classical field; the record-space reading predicts a bounded, saturating, sub-Poisson variance with a scale fixed by N_max and τ₀ — the "randomness" is the resolution of the write, not a diffusion of the metric.* What is not yet there, and why this is a marker and not a row: no observable is named (which field, read how, at what cadence relative to τ₀); no number is computed (the saturation variance in laboratory units needs a map from the record space to a measured quantity, which is exactly the map T2624 says the dictionary lacks — "the record-space dictionary has no kinematics"); and the CQ theory's diffusion constant is itself bounded only from below, so the two shapes are distinguishable by the *time dependence* of the variance (linear growth vs saturation), not by its size — which is the honest form of the falsifier if one is ever written. ## The mechanism, in Casey's words, and its statistics (2026-09-22, Lyra; Keeper's round-4 prompt; consistent with Elie's prereg 5776)
Casey (2026-09-22), verbatim: **"the continuum boundary is smooth but not perfectly smooth; the variance is the individual commitment areas closing."** That is shot noise. Closings of commitment cells are a counting process, so the variance of the count equals its mean, and a quantity carried by a region's record fluctuates about it with relative floor 1/√N, N the region's commitment-cell count (N = A/ℓ_P², Elie's rule R). Three consequences, each the opposite sign of the classical–quantum picture: (i) **bounded** — the floor is set by N, not by an accumulating walk; (ii) **shrinking with expansion** — the committed count grows like an area, d ln N/dt = 2H (K1919 Section 7), so 1/√N falls as 1/a and the floor tightens as the universe fills; (iii) **the mean never random-walks** — the mean of Λ is the ledger's identity Λ = 3H²Ω_Λ, and the noise is about it, not in it. Against this, the CQ master equation's noise is Gaussian with a variance growing linearly in the measurement time T with a free diffusion coefficient D (Oppenheim et al. 2023, above). **The discriminator is the sign of the T-slope of the variance: zero here, positive there.** This is consistent with the register's A1 variance clause — Λ is Pauli-frozen, sub-Poisson, not everpresent (T2571) — and with T2546's Fano factor below one.
