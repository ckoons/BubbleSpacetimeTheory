# K1640 — The double cover, sharpened; the precise 4π path of time; and the paper-on-time destination

## 1. The condition-form, corrected and signable (Cal §558)
"Closes at 4π ⟺ every charge ∈ (1/2)ℤ" is **not** a biconditional — integers live in (1/2)ℤ too, so it only says 4π is *a* period, not *the* period. The correct, exact form (and Cal signs it):
> **T = 4π is minimal ⟺ (every charge ∈ (1/2)ℤ) AND (at least one charge ∉ ℤ).**

## 2. It rests on the scalar — and that's the interesting version
The **Di spinor tower {2, 3, 4} is all-integer** → closes at 2π by itself. The double cover rests **entirely on the Rac scalar at 3/2.** Web-confirmed (arXiv:1409.2185, Fernando–Günaydin: SO(5,2) has exactly two singletons — the Rac *scalar* minrep and the Di *spinor* deformation) and formula-grounded (Δ_scalar=(d−2)/2=3/2, Δ_spinor=(d−1)/2=2 at d=n_C=5). Because d=5 is **odd**, the *scalar* weight is the half-integer. So the fermion story is not merely retracted — it is **inverted**: in odd dimension the **scalar (Higgs sector) forces the double cover**, and the spinor is the one sector that would *not*. Bank the odd version — *a Higgs-sector charge making time's circle a double circle* is the non-trivial, reader-worthy claim; "spinors are 4π-periodic" is a triviality and, here, false. (Single-point-of-failure de-risked by the formula; a primary-source page-ref still wanted before anything external — a second reader, not Cal.)

## 3. Casey's question: what is the precise 4π path of time?
Tiered — the solid geometry first, the evocative part held as candidate.

**Solid (standard + corpus):**
- The time-generator is the **one SO(2)/Spin(2) center** of K = SO(5)×SO(2) — a **U(1)**. So: *not* an SU(2), *not* "both SU(2)," *not* two circles. The SU(2)'s live in SO(5) and carry *space/spin*, not time.
- **Spin(2) is the double cover of SO(2), and the double cover of a circle is a circle** — "twice as long." So the path is **one circle, traversed twice.** 4π = twice around the single time-circle.
- The reason it's *twice* is that a half-integer-charged state is a **section of the Möbius bundle** over that circle (the non-trivial real line bundle over S¹ / a half-integer-weight complex line bundle). A frame carried around it flips sign at 2π and returns only at **4π.** So: **time traces a circle; the state riding time traces the edge of a Möbius strip** — one edge that wraps the circle twice.

**The distinction that keeps it honest (do not merge these):**
- The **"two faces"** of time — the real-time tick exp(−τJ) and the imaginary-time circle exp(iθJ) — are the **Wick pair** (the *complexified* time parameter, τ real ↔ iθ imaginary). That is a *different* structure from the double cover.
- The **4π / Möbius / double cover** is a property of the **imaginary (circular) face specifically.** Real-time is a **one-directional half-line** (the arrow — a semigroup, spectrum bounded below); imaginary-time is the **bi-directional circle** that closes at 4π.
- So, answering you precisely: it is **not** "two directions of time," and it is **not** "two sides of one Möbius" (a Möbius has *one* side). It is **one U(1) circle, traversed twice** (equivalently one Möbius edge), with a *separate* real/imaginary Wick duality that gives the two faces. The half-twist that demands the second loop is sourced by the **scalar's** half-integer weight, i.e. by **n_C=5 being odd.**

**Guard (this is the re-inflation risk):** the "two faces = two sides of one strip" merger is exactly the kind of beautiful picture we retracted one round ago. Bank only the solid geometry above; the *interpretation* (what the Möbius means physically, whether the two faces and the double cover are one deeper object) is the lane's open work, held as candidate.
