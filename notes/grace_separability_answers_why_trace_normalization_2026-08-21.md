## The question (K1359, honest open)
The spectral-action/NCG route computes sin²θ_W from tr(Y²)=10/3, tr(T₃²)=2 → **3/8**, TRACE-normalized (U(1)_Y locked to the non-abelian factors at μ_geo). But **3/8 is the SU(5) unification value**, and Five-Absence FORBIDS GUTs. So: why does BST use the trace-normalization that yields the GUT value, in a no-unification framework?

## The answer — "unification" is two different objects (subscript it)
- **Unification_group:** a simple gauge group (SU(5), SO(10)) CONTAINING SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) as a subgroup. **This is what Five-Absence forbids** (no GUT, no proton decay, no new gauge boson at a unification scale).
- **Unification_coupling:** the three couplings tied to ONE shared constant g₀ at a scale (the trace-normalization). **This is what BST uses** — and it does NOT require Unification_group.

**These are different.** The trace-normalization is Unification_coupling (a shared *constant* at μ_geo), not Unification_group (a shared *group*). Five-Absence forbids the group, not the constant. No contradiction.

## Why separability is the load-bearing input (Elie verifying)

So BST trace-normalizes (shares a constant) without unifying (merging the group).

## The 3/8 = SU(5) coincidence, explained (not embarrassing)
3/8 is the ratio of the fermion-content hypercharge traces under the shared normalization. SU(5) gives the same 3/8 **because SU(5) embeds the same fermion content** — the trace ratio is a property of the (BST-derived, 45-Weyl) FERMION CONTENT + the shared-constant normalization, NOT of a unifying group. So the coincidence is fermion-content-driven, not group-driven; it does not smuggle in a GUT.
