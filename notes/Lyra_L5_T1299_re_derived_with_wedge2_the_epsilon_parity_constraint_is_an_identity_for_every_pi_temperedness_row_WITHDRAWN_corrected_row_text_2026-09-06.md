## 2. Step D re-run: the "ε-parity constraint" is an identity, and it was an identity with Sym² too

- Maass–Selberg pairs s with w₀s, which in the L-normalisation is 1 − s, and ε-factors obey **ε(s, π, r) · ε(1 − s, π̃, r) = ω_r(−1)** for EVERY π (tempered or not) — this is the functional equation of the local ε, a theorem (Tate for GL₁, Jacquet–Langlands for GL₂), with the s-dependence q^{−a(s−½)}·q^{−a(½−s)} cancelling identically because the pairing is s ↔ 1−s, never s ↔ −s.
- Hence ε(s,std)³ε(1−s,std)³ = ω_π(−1)³ = ω_π(−1), and ε(2s, ω)ε(1−2s, ω̄) = ω(−1); the product is ω_π(−1)² = **1, for every π, identically**. - The deeper reason the constraint had to be empty: M(s)M(w₀s) = Id is Langlands' THEOREM about the intertwining operators of ANY induced representation.
