# How to Read a BST Claim: the Tier System in One Page
**Keeper, v0.1, 2026-08-24 — packet document (R2). Scope: a physicist reading only this page can correctly
predict what evidence stands behind any labeled claim in this packet. That sentence is this page's own
test: if you find a labeled claim whose support surprises you after reading this, the page has failed and
we want to know.**

---

## The five tiers — what each label promises

**DERIVED (D).** The result follows from the geometry of D_IV⁵ by an argument you can check, with **no
step chosen to reach the answer.** Where a Derived claim consumes an input, the input is named *in the
statement* — e.g., the hypercharge theorem carries its four inputs (one observational) inside its own
sentence. **Derived-conditional** means exactly that: derived, given named conditions that are themselves
stated, not hidden.

**PARTIALLY DERIVED (PD) — always in explicit-split form.** Part of the claim is forced and part is not,
and the label tells you which is which: *"magnitude derived, branch data-picked"* or *"order derived, value
input."* A PD claim never blends the halves. If you see a PD label without its split, that is a defect —
report it.

**IDENTIFIED (I).** A formula in BST's integers matches a measured value, and **we do not claim to know
why.** Identification is a candidate, not evidence: our own null tests show that expressibility in small
integers is cheap (the fine-structure constant's famous 137 sits in the *bulk* of what such forms can
reach, and we say so). An Identified claim graduates only when a mechanism is derived — and several never
will.

**CANDIDATE (C).** A mechanism or identity with a stated test it has not yet passed — or a result awaiting
its second, independent verification. Candidates carry their kill-conditions with them.

**STRUCTURAL (S).** A statement about the framework's shape (what exists, what is impossible) rather than
a number. Example: *"the geometry cannot supply a length scale"* is itself a proved theorem — an honest
openness, labeled as such.

## The three disciplines behind the labels

**1. Pre-registration.** Bars, win/loss conditions, and failure modes are frozen *before* computations
run. Both of this program's recent headline negatives — a chirality criterion failing, a mass-mechanism
dying at its own test — were scored against bars written in advance, and are reported as failures by name.
**A register of falsifiers (packet document) lists what would kill each claim, including the ones currently
passing.**

**2. Target-innocence.** An input is admissible in a derivation only if it exists independently of the
answer. Where an input is the observed answer itself (e.g., the observed charged fermion spectrum in the
hypercharge theorem), it is declared *observational* — in the theorem statement.

**3. One input, stated.** Dimensionless structure is derived; the theory takes **one measured number as its
ruler** and says so. Ratios are predictions; the absolute scale is calibration. This is the same posture as
GR (which posits G) and QM (which posits ℏ), made explicit.

## What the labels do NOT mean

- *Derived* does **not** mean "beyond question": it means the argument is exhibited and its inputs are on
  its face. Attack the argument, not our confidence.
- A count of registry tags is **not** a verification statistic; only per-claim tiers are.
- Agreement with data quoted without a σ, or against an unnamed dataset, does not count here. Experimental
  comparisons in this packet are pinned to named releases (e.g., NuFIT 6.0) with the σ computed against
  the stated band.

## The one-line test, restated

Pick any labeled claim in this packet. From the label alone you should be able to say: *what would change
my mind about this, and what did its authors already try.* If you cannot, the label — or this page — is
wrong, and either is a bug we will fix in public.

*— The tier system is maintained by the program's internal auditor. Every tier assignment in this packet
survived at least one adversarial review by a referee whose written role is to break it.*
