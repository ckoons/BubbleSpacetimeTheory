## (1) D_IV⁴ is not a valid separator; twist separator = D_IV⁷ vs D_IV⁸
The color block V₁₂ = ℝ^{n−2} carries the SO(n−2) vector rep — real-type for m≥3, but at **m=2 (n=4) SO(2) is abelian**, so its rep is **complex type**. Hence **D_IV⁴ census = {ℂ,ℍ,ℂ}**, not {ℂ,ℍ,ℝ} — not matched to D_IV⁵, so it can't be the twist separator (Elie 5402, verified). **Re-based:** twist (Inv 2 non-droppable) = **D_IV⁷ vs D_IV⁸** (both {ℂ,ℝ,ℝ}, differ only in orientability); census (Inv 1 non-droppable) = **D_IV⁵ vs D_IV⁷**. SEP-1 moves from (4,5) to (7,8) and **stands**. Amend K1743, the table, and F1067 Lane-A (4).

## (2) "Ordered triple," not "multiset" (Cal)
The banked 4.75-bit bound *is* the ordered count (27 = 3³). A genuine multiset (10 states, 3.32 bits) would make the rows "charge=ℂ, weak=ℍ, color=ℝ" unreadable — they assign types to **named** blocks. Say: **"the census is the ordered triple of reality types, multiplicity-free; the '3' in V₁₂ is a rep dimension (=N_c), a separate reading."**

## (3) Capacity 4.75 ≠ content 1.58 — and the honest version is sharper (Cal)
Across D_IV^n only three census triples occur ({ℂ,ℂ,ℝ}, {ℂ,ℍ,ℝ}, {ℂ,ℝ,ℝ}) — charge=ℂ and color=ℝ fixed, only the spinor moves. So the census's **content inside D_IV is 1.58 bits** (one trinary question — the spinor's reality type), not the 4.75-bit **capacity**. The honest statement is the *better headline*: **inside D_IV the entire internal-SM census turns on one question — is the spinor real, complex, or quaternionic — and n_C=5 is the unique value making it quaternionic (→ SU(2)_L); which is exactly why D_IV⁷ (spinor→ℝ) is the separator.** Table: state 1.58 content, mark 4.75 capacity.
