## The one honest divergence, stated plainly (banked, not hedged)
**BST does not reproduce Finster's causal action.** We settled this internally on 2026-08-14 (verdict, verbatim from our living status: *"B1 CFS lane CLOSED, clean structural answer"*), and the answer is **structural NO**:

## How BST *does* approach "causal action"
BST's causal structure is **read directly off the commitment order** of the forced domain, not produced by minimizing anything:
- The substrate **commits** events in a sequence; **that order is the causal set.** (BST has a full causal-set formulation from D_IV⁵, Aug-14.)
- The native two-point object is the **Bergman reproducing kernel**, whose reproducing property `∫ K(x,y)K(y,x) dμ(y) = K(x,x)` **is** the Hardy two-point→one-point collapse. This plays the *role* A_xy plays in CFS (a two-point causal structure) but is a **different mathematical object** — a reproducing kernel, not an eigenvalue Lagrangian.
- So BST's "causal action" is the **commitment/Bergman order** — self-standing, geometric, native to D_IV⁵ — **not borrowed from CFS.** Where CFS *derives* the causal order by minimization, BST takes the commitment order as **primitive** and reads the causal set off it.
- **Falsifiable distinction:** BST computes the **variance of the commitment count, Var(N)**, which differs from Sorkin's standard causal-set growth statistics — a concrete, computed way to tell BST's causal set from the standard one.
