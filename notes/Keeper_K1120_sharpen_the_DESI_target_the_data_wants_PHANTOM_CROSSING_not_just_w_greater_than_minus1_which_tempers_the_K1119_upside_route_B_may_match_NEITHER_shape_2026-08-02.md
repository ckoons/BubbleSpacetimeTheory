# K1120 — The data wants phantom crossing, which tempers the K1119 upside

## The precise numbers (2026-08-02 scrub)
- **DESI DR2 + CMB + Pantheon+:** w₀ = −0.838 ± 0.055, wₐ = −0.62 (+0.22/−0.19).
- **DESI DR2 + CMB alone:** w₀ = −0.42 ± 0.21, wₐ = −1.75 ± 0.58 (3.1σ).
- **ΛCDM discrepancy:** 2.5σ–5.4σ, SNe-sample-dependent (Pantheon+ lower, Union3 middle, DESY5 highest) and analysis-dependent; the high end rides the most-contested SNe systematics (DES-SN5YR low-z/high-z offset debate).

## The shape fact that matters
The preferred w₀wₐ fit is **phantom-crossing**: w > −1 **today**, w < −1 in the **past**, crossing w = −1 near **z ≈ 0.5**. The data doesn't just want "w > −1" — it wants an evolving shape that **dips below −1 in the past.**

## Why this tempers K1119
K1119 said the more-evolving outcome gives the *stronger* BST result (route B → Derived). **That holds only if route B's w(a) shape crosses the phantom divide.** A simple holographic/growing-horizon route B plausibly gives w ≈ −0.89 roughly **constant** — matching w₀ > −1 but **not** the past-dip below −1. So BST could sit in tension with the DESI *shape* on **both** horns:
- **Route A** (w = −1, no evolution): disfavored 2.5–5.4σ.
- **Route B** (constant w ≈ −0.89, no crossing): matches today's value, misses the past-dip.

**#58 has three possible outcomes, not prejudged:**
1. route B produces phantom crossing → matches DESI → strong (Derived value *and* explains an exotic feature);
2. route B is constant w ≈ −0.89 → partial match (right sign, wrong shape) → BST predicts a *distinguishable* evolving DE — its own near-future test;
3. neither BST shape matches → tension on both horns (needs a third BST behavior, or the phantom signal is a systematics artifact).

## Calibration
Phantom crossing is **exotic** (naively violates the null energy condition; hard for single-field quintessence). If BST route B produces it naturally, that's a striking success — but I must **not assume it does**. K1119 leaned optimistic; this pulls it back to **neutral**. The fork is genuinely open, BST is sharply tested either way, but "the data favors the stronger BST horn" was premature until route B's shape is computed.

## Unchanged
The K1118-tightening retraction (circular) stands; the fork stands; the cc **smallness** (Structural-Derived) and **structure** (Proved) stand; Lyra's determination stays **doubly-blind** — the geometry decides route A vs B, *then* we compare to this phantom-crossing shape.
