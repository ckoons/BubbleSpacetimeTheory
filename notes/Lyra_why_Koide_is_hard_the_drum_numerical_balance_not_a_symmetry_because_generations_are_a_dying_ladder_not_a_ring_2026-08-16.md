## The analogy (Casey's, for everyone)

**D_IV⁵ is a drum.** Strike it and it rings in modes — overtones. **The three fermion generations are its first three overtones.** Each has a "loudness" (a mass), and the Koide relation asks a single question about them:

> Take the **square-roots of the loudnesses**, make a 3-vector, and ask: does it sit at the **45° midpoint** — where the "everyone-equal" part (democratic) and the "lopsided" part (traceless) weigh exactly the same? That balance is Koide's Q = 2/3.

## Why, in most theories, you'd expect a symmetry to force it — and why it can't here

There are two ways a balance like that can come out exactly:

1. **By symmetry (forced).** If the three overtones sat at the corners of a **ring** — a 3-fold rotation, where you can spin overtone 1 → 2 → 3 → 1 and return — then the balance is *automatic*: a cyclic Z₃ splits any vector into a democratic piece and a traceless piece, and the ring's symmetry can pin them equal. **No computation needed; the symmetry does it.**
2. **By numerical coincidence of the shape (computed).** If there is *no* such ring, the balance is not protected by anything — it is just a number the drum's geometry either lands on or doesn't. You have to **compute the actual couplings** and look.

**BST is in case 2, and we can see exactly why.** The three generations are **not** a ring. Every way the geometry builds them, they are a **ladder that dies at the top**:
- In the cohomology (Z[h]/h⁶) the generations are the odd modes **h¹ → h³ → h⁵**, and the next rung, h⁷, is **zero**. The ladder *ends*; it does not *wrap back*. A ring would need it to close (h⁶ = 1); the drum gives h⁶ = 0. (Nilpotent, N³=0 — not cyclic, C³=I.)
- The one genuine 3-fold **ring** in BST is the **color** (the SU(3) center). But **Koide is a law about leptons, and leptons carry no color** — so the color ring spins the quarks and cannot reach the colorless leptons. (The same wall as "color ≠ space": leptons don't carry the thing the ring acts on.)

**So in BST the 45° balance is *not* protected by any symmetry.** It is a **numerical fact about the drum's shape** — the FK reproducing-kernel couplings between the three overtones either put the √-loudness vector at the midpoint, or they don't. There is no ring standing behind it to guarantee the answer.
