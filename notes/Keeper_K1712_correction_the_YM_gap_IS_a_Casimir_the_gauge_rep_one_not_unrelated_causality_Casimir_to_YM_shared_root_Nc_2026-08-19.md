# K1712 — the YM gap IS a Casimir (the gauge-rep one); causality Casimir→YM; shared root N_c

Casey caught a real imprecision: I'd said the gap is "2N_c, unrelated to the Casimir." Too flat. The precise picture:

## The gap is a Casimir — the gauge-representation one
The Laplacian on a symmetric space **is** the Casimir of its isometry group. S⁴ is acted on by **SO(5)** = the compact isotropy factor of D_IV⁵ (SO(5,2)/[SO(5)×SO(2)]). So the transverse-gauge-field (co-exact 1-form) Laplacian on S⁴ **is the SO(5)-isotropy Casimir on the gauge representation**, lowest eigenvalue = 2(n_C−2) = **2N_c = 6**. It is a Casimir, the right one, part of the D_IV⁵ algebra.

## Why "C₂ = 6" is overloaded (the clean reason)
A quadratic Casimir has a **different eigenvalue on every representation**: scalar → n_C+1; gauge 1-form → 2N_c = 2n_C−4; Bergman → n_C+1. Same operator, different reps → different functions of n, coinciding at n_C=5 (Condition 5). K1711 correctly refuses identifying the **gauge**-rep Casimir (2N_c) with the **scalar**-rep Casimir (n_C+1) — *that* is Condition 5. But the gap itself is a Casimir.

## Causality (Casey's 3rd question)
**Casimir → YM, not the reverse.** The Casimir is prior — an algebra property, its eigenvalues exist before any physics. YM *reads* it on the gauge rep. So the Casimir does not draw identity from YM; YM draws its gap from the Casimir. **But both share one root: N_c = n_C−2** (short-root multiplicity) — it defines SU(N_c) *and* sits in 2N_c. Geometry → N_c → (gauge group, Casimir eigenvalue).
