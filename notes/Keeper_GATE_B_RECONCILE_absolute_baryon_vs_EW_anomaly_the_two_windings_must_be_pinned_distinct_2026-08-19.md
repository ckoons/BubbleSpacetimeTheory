# GATE-B-RECONCILE — absolute-B vs the EW anomaly (the two windings must be pinned distinct)

Casey's absolute-proton ruling (K1699) owes the electroweak-anomaly reconciliation. The **easy answer has a trap:** "baryon number is topological, so the anomaly can't touch it" — but **sphalerons are themselves topological transitions** (they change the `SU(2)_L` Chern–Simons winding, and via the anomaly that changes `B`). So "it's topological" is the *start* of the question, not the answer.

**The sharp question (the whole reconciliation):** is BST's baryon-number winding (the substrate trefoil / `π₁` of the time-circle / Hopf-`Z₃` submanifold-closure — T296/T1945) the **same** topological object as the SM sphaleron's `SU(2)_L` Chern–Simons winding, or a **different** substrate charge?
- **Different** → sphalerons change the EW winding but not the proton's substrate winding → **absolute-B holds**, and BST makes a clean stated departure: the physical proton is protected by a winding the electroweak sphaleron cannot reach (B broken only at Planck-scale topology change — T296's own text). *(Corpus leans here: T296/AC-Theorems L6521 list B's breaking as GUT/Planck-scale, not electroweak; the `‖B−L‖²→3/13` route is refuted, K738, so `B−L` isn't load-bearing.)*
- **Same** → sphalerons change it → absolute-B is in tension and needs another reason for a zero rate.

## Acceptance (all required)
1. **Pin the two windings as distinct** — substrate trefoil/Hopf vs EW Chern–Simons, on different spaces/invariants, *exhibited*, not identified from the shared word "winding."
2. **State the departure as a prediction** — BST predicts no sphaleron-induced baryon violation (the proton's winding is sphaleron-invariant).
3. **Baryogenesis = initial data, on the ledger** — Sakharov needs B-violation; exact B ⟹ the asymmetry is initial data (consistent with BST positive-time ontology; coherent, but *said*, beside the one ruler input).
