## 1. What a test function is, and what T1448/T1451 have instead
The Selberg/Arthur trace formula on Γ\D_IV⁵ is an identity of distributions: for h an even, W(B₂)-invariant function on i𝔞* ≅ ℝ² in the Paley–Wiener class (entire of exponential type, rapidly decreasing on the axis — equivalently the spherical transform of a compactly supported bi-K-invariant kernel k on G), Σ_{discrete} h(λ_j) + (continuous terms, including the Eisenstein term E₀[h] of L8) = (identity) + (hyperbolic) + (parabolic) terms, each a functional of h or of its kernel k. The test function is the INPUT; the "contributions" are what the formula assigns to it. Nothing in the formula produces h; h is chosen, and every number on either side is a number FOR THAT h.
T1448 (April) writes the two-loop Schwinger coefficient as C₂ = I + K + E + H with I, K, E, H "identified" as identity, curvature, Eisenstein and hyperbolic terms, and names as its test function "the vertex spectral function h_V(k) = d_k^{(1)}/λ_k², d_k^{(1)} = C(k + N_c, rank²), λ_k = k(k + n_C)" — a function of the DISCRETE Bergman index k, i.e. a weighting of the holomorphic discrete spectrum. T1451 generalises to C_L = I_L + K_L + E_L + H_L + M_L for L = 1…4 and adds a "heat kernel column rule" for L = 1.

## 2. Why there is no rule from the vertex to h
- **Domain.** h must live on the continuous parameter λ ∈ i𝔞* (two real coordinates), be even and Weyl-invariant, and extend analytically to a strip. h_V lives on k ∈ ℕ, one discrete coordinate indexing the scalar holomorphic discrete series. There is no canonical continuation of a sequence to a Paley–Wiener function of two variables: infinitely many h restrict to any given sequence on the discrete set, and the Eisenstein term E₀[h] is NOT determined by h's values on the discrete spectrum (the continuous term integrates h against ∂log c over the whole axis, where h_V says nothing). So the number T1448 calls "the Eisenstein contribution" cannot be computed from h_V; it was ASSIGNED — K1869 ruled exactly this (Mode B: "−(π²/2) ln 2 from rank^{−2s}" is a normalisation; E10 shows the value requires ∫h = π⁴/2).
- **Construction.** A rule "vertex ↦ h" would have to say how a QED Feynman vertex (a local operator insertion in a field theory on ℝ^{3,1}) determines a bi-K-invariant kernel on SO₀(5,2), i.e. a specific distribution on the double coset space K\G/K. The corpus has a Bergman-kernel reading of the vertex (the reproducing kernel at coincident points, T1445/T1448 Step 2), which determines h on the HOLOMORPHIC discrete spectrum — that is what h_V is — and nothing on the tempered continuous spectrum, because the Bergman kernel is orthogonal to it. So the vertex fixes h where the Eisenstein term is blind and is silent where the Eisenstein term looks. **That is the precise sense in which there is no rule.**
- **Consequence for the tier.** T1451's C_L = I + K + E + H + M is a TEMPLATE: a partition of a known number into named pieces with integers assigned to each, with no functional h from which all five pieces are computed. Its own 13-to-15-digit matches are matches of the assignment, not outputs of the formula; K1869's Mode B ruling on the E line extends to the whole template, and T1450/T1451 are CONDITIONAL on an h that does not exist as constructed (Grace G18, this round). The honest sentence: "T1451 assigns Selberg-term NAMES to the pieces of C_L; it does not compute C_L from a test function." What survives independently: the arithmetic identities of the Bergman spectrum (H₅ = 137/60, the eigenvalue sums) are theorems about the discrete spectrum and do not need h.

## 3. One h fixed by a rule independent of the target, for E12
Pick the heat kernel: **h_t(λ) = exp(−t(⟨λ, λ⟩ + ⟨ρ, ρ⟩))**, λ ∈ i𝔞* (so ⟨λ,λ⟩ ≤ 0 on the axis and h_t is Gaussian there), with ⟨ρ, ρ⟩ = (5/2)² + (3/2)² = 17/2 and the Killing-normalised inner product of L1 (⟨e_i, e_j⟩ = δ_{ij}). It is even, W-invariant, Paley–Wiener (its kernel is the heat kernel of the Laplacian at time t, the one bi-K-invariant kernel every trace-formula paper on a locally symmetric space uses first). **The rule:** t is fixed by the geometry, not by C₂: take **t = 1** (unit Laplacian time) as the primary, and **t = 2/17 = 1/⟨ρ,ρ⟩** (the time at which the bottom of the continuous spectrum has decayed by e⁻¹) as the secondary; report both with ∫_{i𝔞*} h_t dλ = π/t · e^{−17t/2} beside each number. E12's output for h_t is then the first Eisenstein number in the corpus that is computed rather than matched: E₀[h_t] at level 1 and at level 137, with its logarithms (ln 2 from the ε normalisation, ln 2 inside ψ(½), ln π from the ξ normalisations, ln 137 from the level) as coefficients read off the computation. No comparison to −3.4205 is meaningful, because h_t is not h_V and h_V is not a test function.
