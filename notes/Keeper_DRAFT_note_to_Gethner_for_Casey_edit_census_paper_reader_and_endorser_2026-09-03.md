Subject: Your 2009 question on Kempe–Kittell switches — a census through 24 vertices, with 349 witnesses

Dear Professor Gethner,

In "How false is Kempe's proof of the Four Color Theorem? Part II" (Involve 2:3, 2009) you and your co-authors left open whether a series of Kempe–Kittell chain switches always resolves an impasse. I am writing because a small group I work with has produced an exhaustive census that sharpens that question and answers a bounded form of it, and you are its natural first reader.

The paper studies one operation on Kittell's eight chains — the four-swap commutator of two link-seeded Kempe swaps with the seeds held fixed — at every degree-five vertex of every 5-connected sphere triangulation with 12 to 24 vertices. Of 7,379,253 stuck colourings (modulo colour permutation), all but 349 reach the gate phase within one fully legal word of a frozen 186-word menu; all 349 reach it within two; none needs three. The 349 are "commutator-locked at depth one," a menu-relative notion: in the unrestricted alphabet of plain Kempe swaps, 27 of them exit in a single swap and Kempe's own two swaps insert the vertex on two. Every count was produced on two independent instruments with hashed witness lists, and every witness is on disk as (n, plantri index, vertex, colouring).

I want to be exact about what is and is not claimed. The paper proves the local structure (one context at a stuck vertex, Kittell's eight chains under an explicit seed rule, and a small stage table for the commutator) and it proves that where Kempe's argument applies, the commutator built from the same chains is the identity. It does not claim a proof of the Four Colour Theorem, any depth bound for all n, or any mechanism behind the depth-two observation. The census is not an unavoidable set and its exits are not reducibility. Your Figure-10 remark in Kittell's own paper — a colouring that every one of his single switches leaves impasse and α² exits — is, we think, the same concept in his frame, and the paper says so.

The full record is public: [Zenodo DOI, version link]. I would value your reading, and any correction. If you find it sound, I would also ask whether you would be willing to endorse it for arXiv math.CO, since under arXiv's current policy an author without an institutional address needs a personal endorsement; if not, the Zenodo record stands on its own.
