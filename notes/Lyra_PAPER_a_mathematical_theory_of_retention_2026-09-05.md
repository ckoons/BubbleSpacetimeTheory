# Introduction

The problem of how a physical system retains information has been attacked from several directions,
and this paper stands on all of them. Shannon (1948) gave the chain rule that splits an entropy into
the entropy of a partition and the entropy within it, and everything below is a reading of that
identity. Crutchfield and Young (1989) took the decisive step of letting the *dynamics* choose the
partition rather than the modeller, defining the causal states and identifying the statistical
complexity with their entropy; the ergodic decomposition of such quantities is treated by
Crutchfield and Debowski (2006). Gell-Mann and Lloyd (1996) posed the same split as effective
complexity. Cheeger's inequality supplies the relation between a bottleneck and a spectral gap that
we use to price a record. Tononi's integrated information theory prices a whole against its best
partition — the "cruelest cut" — which is the same construction we reach in Section 6 by another
road. Eigen's error threshold is the phenomenon our Section 7 recovers as a melting law. Bennett's
logical depth and, more recently, the assembly index of Cronin and Walker measure construction
history, which we show in Section 9 is **orthogonal** to the quantity treated here rather than a
competitor to it.

We say this at the front because the honest fraction of what follows that is new is small. Our
estimate is about one part in five. What we believe is ours is this: **the construction axis** --
what happens to a record's class structure when the state space itself is *built* rather than held
fixed (Section 5); **the generator law** that a record is destroyed by which moves a set contains
and not how many (Section 7), together with the abelian/non-abelian split that explains why some
systems melt linearly and others fall off a cliff; **the coupling/selection ledger** of Section 8;
and the measured orthogonality of record and depth in Section 9. The rest is a re-derivation of
known material in one vocabulary. We think the single ledger earns its keep. We do not think it is
new physics.

# Part I. The record system

## 1. Definitions

**Definition 1.** A *record system* is a pair (S, M) where S is a finite set of states and M is a
set of involutions on S. The *move graph* is the undirected graph on S with an edge {s, ms}
for each m in M and s in S with ms != s. A *class* is a connected component of the move graph.

Classes are the ergodic components of the dynamics generated by M. A state factorises as
(class, position within class): the class is what the dynamics cannot change and the position is
what it can.

**Definition 2.** For the uniform measure on S, write

  **R** = the Shannon entropy of the class distribution (the *record*),
  **H_thermo** = the mass-weighted mean of log2(class size) (the *heat*),
  **H** = log2 |S|.

**Proposition 1.** H = R + H_thermo.

*Proof.* The chain rule H(X) = H(f(X)) + H(X | f(X)) with f the class map. QED

Proposition 1 is Shannon's, restated. It is the accounting identity on which everything rests, and
the reader should keep in mind that several of the theorems below are one line from it.

**Definition 3.** A *construction* is a map A from a record system (S, M) to a record system
(S', M'). A construction *merges* if two parent classes map into one child class, and *splits* if
one parent class maps onto more than one. A construction **retains** if it does not merge.

## 2. A warning about R

R is maximised when nothing moves. If every class is a singleton then R = log2|S| and H_thermo = 0,
and the system is a perfect record that cannot do anything. **R must never be read alone.** The
honest state of a record system is the pair (R, H_thermo), and a trajectory that raises R while
driving H_thermo to zero is not accumulating a record; it is crystallising. We return to this in
Section 8 and it is the single most important caveat in the paper.

# Part II. Retention

## 3. The retraction theorem

> **Theorem 1.** Let A : S -> S' be a construction and r : S' -> S satisfy r o A = id.
> If **every move of M', seen through r, is a composition of moves of M**, then A retains.

*Proof.* Suppose s, t lie in different parent classes but A(s), A(t) in one child class. Then a
sequence of M'-moves carries A(s) to A(t). Applying r and using the hypothesis, the image of that
sequence is a composition of M-moves carrying s to t, so s and t are in one parent class,
contradiction. QED

The clause in bold is load-bearing and is the most frequently dropped hypothesis in this subject,
including by us. A retraction alone is not enough. Every failure of retention we have measured is a
failure of that clause and not of the retraction.

> **Theorem 2 (certificate).** A retains if and only if the child carries a function, constant on
> child classes, whose pullback along A separates the parent classes.

*Proof.* If A retains, the parent class map factors through the child class map and the child class
label is such a function. Conversely such a function separates the images of distinct parent
classes, so no two can lie in one child class. QED

> **Theorem 3.** If the parent move graph is an induced subgraph of the child move graph on the
> image of A, then A retains, under any extension rule.

*Proof.* Immediate from Theorem 1 with r the restriction. QED

Theorem 3 is why crystals work. A growing lattice reproduces its own move structure inside the
larger one, so a dislocation, a stacking fault or a twin boundary propagates indefinitely and cannot
be destroyed by further growth. Retention, in this setting, is **free**.

# Part III. Construction and creation

## 4. Accretion

> **Theorem 4.** Let A adjoin a new site and let A be *total*, meaning every parent state extends.
> Then A induces a **bijection** on classes: it neither merges nor splits.
> Consequently a class is **created** if and only if the exclusion of the non-extending states
> disconnects a parent class.

*Proof.* Totality makes the class map surjective; Theorem 3 makes it injective; a bijection creates
nothing. For the second claim, the child classes are the components of the parent move graph
restricted to the extending states, so a parent class contributes more than one child class exactly
when that restriction disconnects it. QED

Theorem 4 is the reason a crystal is not alive. Its growth is very nearly total, so it copies its
record perfectly and forever and never writes a new one. **Creation requires refusal.**

## 5. The price of a bit

> **Theorem 5.** Creating a new class from a parent class requires excluding a vertex cut of that
> class's move graph. Hence a bit costs at least the size of a minimum cut.

*Proof.* By Theorem 4 creation is disconnection, and to disconnect a connected graph one must
remove a cut. QED

**Corollary (why two).** A move graph with no cut vertex admits no cut of size one. In every
instance we have measured the move graph is 2-connected, so **the minimum price of a bit is two**.

This corollary has an unexpectedly exact echo. In the dimer instance a single forbidden bond never
splits a class in 80 of 80 attempts -- it only empties classes -- while two-bond prohibitions split
routinely (251 of 300 on one torus, 90.7 per cent of 300 on another). In the same instance a single
cross bond between two copies is used by **zero** states, for a parity reason, while two are used
freely. *One contact can neither cut nor couple; two can do both, and it is the same two.*

**Remark 6 (formerly stated as a theorem).** One expects the cut cost of Theorem 5 to be
controlled by the spectral gap of the class's move graph, so that slow-mixing records are cheap to
write and fast-mixing ones expensive. Cheeger's inequality bounds *edge* conductance, and Theorem 5
needs a *vertex* cut; the transfer requires a vertex-expansion inequality (Bobkov, Houdre and
Tetali 2000) or a maximum-degree factor, and we have not carried it out. We keep the statement as a
heuristic and number it so that later references resolve.

# Part IV. The ledger

## 6. Selection and coupling

> **Theorem 7 (exchange).** A purely selective step -- one that removes states and adds none --
> has Delta H <= 0, hence **Delta R <= -Delta H_thermo**. Since R <= H, iterated pure selection
> terminates: R rises, reaches a maximum at or before the exhaustion of H_thermo, and thereafter
> can only fall.

*Proof.* From Proposition 1, Delta R = Delta H - Delta H_thermo <= -Delta H_thermo. The terminal
state of iterated selection has every class a singleton, H_thermo = 0, and every further step
removes states and lowers R. QED

Theorem 7 is one line from Proposition 1 and we do not claim more for it. Its content is not the
inequality but the **regime**: pure selection is capacity-*spending* and it ends as a crystal --
maximum nominal record, nothing moving, and thereafter decline.

**The measured rate is a property of the searcher, not of the space.** We report this as a
correction to our own earlier claim. On one torus the conversion ratio Delta R / (-Delta H_thermo)
has median 0.517 under a greedy refusal policy, 0.556 under exhaustive search, and **0.401 under a
random policy**; on the same states under a colder move set the three medians are 0.565, 0.565 and
**0.223**. Any constant read off a trajectory in this framework is a constant of the policy.

**Coupling is the complementary operation.** A step that adds contacts between two record systems
is capacity-*creating*: it spends heat and opens space at the same time. Measured across two
instances the conversion ratio is **1.29 to 2.11**, always above one, with between 47 and 77 per
cent of the record written taken out of H_thermo and the remainder out of newly opened space.

> **The ledger.** Selection converts at most one and terminates. Coupling converts above one and
> does not. A system that alternates them accumulates; a system doing either alone does not.

We note explicitly what this does **not** say. Duplication is not free: two decoupled copies of a
record system carry exactly twice its record, R/2R(P) = 1.000 to four decimal places in our
measurement. Copying is cheap only once the copies are coupled.

# Part V. What destroys a record

## 7. The generator law

The central experimental finding of this work is that a record is not destroyed by the *number* of
available moves but by *which* moves are available. On one torus, ordering moves by cycle length
gives a set of 154 moves under which R = 0; ordering the same moves by geometric diameter gives a
set of **546** moves under which R = 0.69 bits survive. The larger set retains and the smaller
one does not.

**This is not explained by saying that the fatal moves "move the certificate".** Classes are
*defined* as components of the move graph and a certificate is *defined* as a function separating
them, so that statement unpacks to "a move that connects two classes merges them", which is
definitional. We flag it because we made exactly that error in an earlier draft. The non-vacuous
content is quantitative, and requires knowing the shifts.

> **Theorem 8 (codimension).** Let S carry a free transitive action of a finite abelian group V,
> with every move acting as translation by some v_m in V, and let mu be uniform. Then the classes
> are the cosets of Lambda = <v_m : m in M> and
>
>   **R = log2 |V / Lambda|,**
>
> exactly. For V = F_2^n this is n - rank(Lambda).

*Proof.* The orbit of s under the group generated by the moves is s + Lambda, a coset, and a free
transitive action makes every coset the same size, so the uniform measure on S pushes to the uniform
measure on V / Lambda. QED

**Remark (the torsion-free case is a codimension, not a bit count).** Theorem 8 is stated for finite
V. For V = Z^n -- a lattice of chemical compositions moved by the columns of a stoichiometric matrix
S -- the compositions are not a torsor and V / Lambda is infinite whenever rank(Lambda) < n, so
log2 |V / Lambda| is infinite. The honest quantity there is the **codimension** n - rank(S), which
is the dimension coefficient of Section 9a and equals the number of independent conservation laws
(the conserved moieties of reaction-network theory: Schuster and Hofer 1991; Feinberg 2019). The
companion paper's "one reaction is one bit" is one dimension of codimension, and its integer checks
are rank-nullity verified numerically -- algebra, not measurement.

Theorem 8 is the abelian case. The next theorem is what happens when the action is not free, so
that a fibre of S -> V / Lambda can hold more than one class.

> **Theorem 9 (certificate translation).** Let phi : S -> V be a map to an abelian group V such
> that every move m in M acts on it by a translation, phi(ms) = phi(s) + v_m. Let
> Lambda = <v_m : m in M> be the subgroup generated by the shifts. Then every class lies inside a
> single fibre of the composite S -> V -> V/Lambda, and
>
>   **R = H(phi_* mu on V / Lambda) + H(class | fibre)**,
>
> **exactly.** The second term is the *defect*. It vanishes if and only if every fibre is a single
> class. In particular a move with v_m = 0 enters no Lambda and so **cannot lower the fibre term,
> however many such moves are added**; it may still merge classes within a fibre and so reduce the
> defect.

*Proof.* Along any move, phi changes by an element of Lambda, so S -> V/Lambda is constant on
move-orbits, i.e. on classes. The class partition therefore refines the fibre partition, and the
chain rule for the entropy of a refinement gives R = H(fibre) + H(class | fibre). The defect
vanishes exactly when no fibre splits, which is transitivity of the action on fibres. A move with
zero shift lies in the kernel of every such quotient and so enters no Lambda; nothing prevents it
from joining two classes that share a fibre. QED

An earlier draft printed the last clause as "cannot merge any classes: it is free." That is false,
and the paper's own data is the counterexample: the 24 face flips of the 4x6 dimer torus all have
shift (0, 0), and they take the system from R = log2 |S| with no moves to R = 2.2572. What a
zero-shift move cannot do is lower the certificate bound; "free" below means free relative to that
bound.

**The defect is the record the certificate cannot see**, and it is measurable. Verified exactly
(machine precision) on five systems: 4x6 faces 2.2572 = 2.2572 + 0.0000; 4x6 all-4-cycles
0.8240 = 0.6908 + **0.1332**; 4x4 faces 2.3159 = 2.2865 + 0.0294; 4x4 all-4-cycles
0.2797 = **0.0000** + **0.2797** (the certificate is worthless there and every bit of record is
hidden from it); 6x6 faces 2.3504 = 2.3500 + 0.0004. **The tightness condition is a pure
connectivity check on the move graph -- no entropies need be computed to apply it.**

**Corollary (why the abelian case is exact).** If the moves generate an abelian group acting by
translations, the cosets ARE the orbits, so the defect is identically zero and R is exactly the
codimension of Section 7's Theorem 8. This is why the meiotic instance of the companion paper
satisfies R = (L-1) - |J| at every value tested, while no dimer instance can.

Theorem 9 is what makes Section 7's experiment non-trivial, because **the bound is computable from
the shifts alone, without ever constructing the move graph.** Two checks:

- **Meiosis (equality holds).** V = F_2^n, a crossover at junction j has shift e_j, so Lambda is the
  span of the accessible junctions and V/Lambda has 2^(n - |J|) equally-weighted elements. The
  theorem gives R = n - |J| exactly, matching the measured value at all seven values tested.
- **Dimers (nonzero defect).** V = Z^2, the winding number. Every one of the 24 face flips has shift
  **(0,0)**, so they leave the fibre term at its no-move value, and the fibre entropy computed from the shifts alone
  reproduces the measured R = **2.2572 to four decimal places** with no reference to the move graph.
  The 15 wrapping cycles have shifts (0, +-4), generating Lambda = 4Z; the fibre term falls to
  0.6908 and the defect accounts for the remaining **0.1332** exactly. The subgroup is identified
  correctly and the decomposition closes.

The empirical statement of Section 7 is therefore explained without tautology: **the 546 compact
moves all have zero shift and are free relative to the certificate bound -- they merge classes
within fibres, which is why 0.69 bits and not 2.26 survive -- while a single wrapping cycle enlarges
Lambda and collapses the bound itself.** What matters is the subgroup the shifts generate, not the cardinality of
the move set.

## 8. What a junction is for

A junction between two record systems pays **only if it blocks moves**. We had claimed that
complexity lives at the interface, and we withdraw the claim in that form. At matched substrate a
coupled pair does carry more record than a monolith -- a factor of 2.4 at matched state-space size,
not the factor of 4 we first reported at matched vertex count -- but the mechanism is that contacts
occupy sites and thereby *forbid* moves that would otherwise mix. Where a junction adds material
without adding constraint it adds nothing: at matched genetic material, outcrossing shows no
advantage over selfing (ratio 0.77 to 1.07 across the recombination range).

**On the number of parties.** Holding substrate, unit count and contact budget all fixed, and
varying only how the contacts are distributed, record rises monotonically as contacts are
concentrated: three junctions 5.58 bits, two junctions 6.73, one junction 8.10. Three bodies forced
into a single junction converge to the efficiency of two bodies (1.486 against 1.51). **The cost is
seams, not bodies.** Two is then the smallest party that can cut at all, by the corollary to
Theorem 5, and every party beyond two dilutes a fixed contact budget across more seams.

# Part VI. The observer, and the temperature

## 8a. Refusal requires an observer

The three operations of Part IV are not symmetric, and treating them as though they were is an
error we made and had to correct.

**Growth and coupling are observer-free.** Bonds form; vertices attach; nothing has to decide.
**Retention is observer-free.** Theorem 1 is a property of maps.
**Refusal is not.** To exclude some states and not others, something must distinguish them. That is
a measurement, and a measurement requires an apparatus. The apparatus need not be conscious -- a
lion is an apparatus, so is a solvent, so is a hole of a given size -- but it is not nothing.

> **The refusal operator is Maxwell's demon, and Theorem 7 is Szilard's accounting with the
> demon's own cost omitted.**

This is not a defect to be repaired but a fact to be recorded, and it explains an observation we
first mis-filed as contamination. On one torus the conversion ratio has median 0.517 under a greedy
refusal policy, 0.556 under exhaustive search, and 0.401 under a random policy; on the same states
under a colder move set, 0.565, 0.565 and 0.223. We had reported 0.50-0.65 as a property of the
record space. **It is a property of the searcher.** The demon's efficiency depends on the demon.

Charging the demon changes the ranking. Taking H_obs = log2(candidates examined) as the memory that
must be erased between cycles, the net conversion becomes 0.129 for greedy, 0.103 for exhaustive
and 0.401 for random: **the cheap searcher wins.** We flag that this accounting is not settled --
a comparison-based argmax can in principle run reversibly, holding only a running best -- but the
term exists and is the same order as the heat term.

**What a receptor costs.** The cheapest demon is one that does not measure and then decide, but in
which the measurement IS the decision: a binding site, a hole of a given size, a flow that sorts by
speed. Nothing is stored, so nothing is erased, and the per-read cost is zero. **The price is
instead the standing maintenance of the distinguisher's shape** -- which is R(T) for the
distinguisher itself, so the observer enters this ledger as another record rather than as an
external term. Optimising selectivity b against a mistake cost c and a record decay rate mu gives
an interior optimum b* = log2(c ln2 / mu), independent of the value of good outcomes: **a system
discriminates because mistakes are expensive, not because the prize is good.** Above mu = c ln2 no
maintained specificity pays at all and the optimal receptor is none, so **geometric selection --
which holds no record, because it IS the shape -- necessarily precedes chemical selection.**

## 8b. R(T): the record an observer of finite patience can see

A record system whose own state gates its moves forces a change in the definition of R. If the
gating can be rewritten freely, every state is reachable from every other, the whole space is one
class, and R = 0: **a record that can freely unshelter itself has none.** Shelter therefore requires
a rate hierarchy -- the gate must be slower than what it gates -- and R must be indexed by how long
one is willing to watch.

> **Definition.** R(T) is the record computed using only those moves whose rate exceeds 1/T.
> It is the record visible to an observer who watches for time T.

Two consequences. **First, this is the canonical ordering of move sets.** Section 10 reports that
no cost function on moves gives a convention-free melting threshold. Rates are not a cost function
chosen by the modeller; they are physical, and ordering moves by rate is therefore canonical. The
observer question and the temperature question are the same question, and R(T) answers both.
**Second, R(T) is a step function** -- flat plateaus and sudden one-bit drops as each generator's
rate crosses the threshold, with no exponential attrition anywhere. Smooth erosion and sudden loss
are different physics: attrition is ongoing mixing, a sudden loss is a generator activating.

**A system cannot be its own demon; two systems can be each other's.** Measured: two identical
systems at equal rates hold 13.87 bit-decades under mutual gating and exactly 0.00 under one-way
gating or none. Mutual gating manufactures a rate hierarchy between equals, and the advantage is
largest precisely where the partners are most alike, vanishing when one is already 1000 times slower
than the other. The mechanism is deadlock: 64 mutually-locked configurations, each partner holding
the other still, embedded in a mobile bulk class of 192, with H_thermo staying at 5.69 of 8 so the
assembly as a whole is not frozen.

# Part VII. Limits

## 9. Record is not depth

Assembly-theoretic and logical-depth measures ask how many construction steps an object required.
That is a different quantity from R and we can exhibit the independence. Two of our instances have
the same record to within 0.035 bits -- 2.3159 and 2.3504 -- and mean class diameters of **7.65 and
25.45**, a factor of 3.33. Within a single family the two rise *together* as the move set cools,
contrary to our own prediction. And the frozen endpoint has R maximal at depth **zero**.

**This theory is therefore not a reparameterisation of assembly theory, and neither measure
subsumes the other.** The honest complexity coordinate is at least the triple (R, H_thermo, depth).

## 9a. The continuum: the record is a dimension, not a count

We attempted a non-lattice instance -- the Chirikov standard map -- and failed twice, concluding
that R might not be measurable off a lattice. **That conclusion was wrong, and the reason is
instructive.** Both instruments counted connected components of a coarse-grained phase space. But
the invariant structure of a near-integrable map is a positive-measure Cantor set of tori, not a
partition into countably many classes: **counting components is simply the wrong functional, and no
refinement of the grid repairs it.**

In the continuum the orbit space is a manifold, so a count of classes diverges. Regularising at a
resolution eps gives the Kolmogorov epsilon-entropy,

  **R(eps) ~ dim(orbit space) * log2(1/eps) + O(1),**

whose leading coefficient is the DIMENSION. **The continuum analogue of the record is a dimension
(or a measure), and R(T) of Section 8b is precisely the regularisation that renders it finite.**
We measure it on the standard map as follows. **Definition.** Take initial conditions on a uniform
256 x 256 grid of cell centres in [0, 2pi)^2, iterate x' = x + y', y' = y + K sin x with y
unwrapped, and call an initial condition *non-mixing at horizon T* if its orbit never moves a full
period in y within T steps, max_{t <= T} |Y_t - Y_0| < 2pi. The measure m(K, T) is the fraction of
initial conditions that are non-mixing. This is an R(T)-type quantity and the horizon is part of the
definition. Predictions were hashed before the run (toy 5691, digest cc492359).

| K | T = 300 | T = 1000 | T = 3000 | T = 10000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 through K_c = 0.9716 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| 1.0 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| 1.2 | 0.9996 | 0.9810 | 0.8790 | 0.6297 |
| 1.5 | 0.7861 | 0.4852 | 0.3482 | 0.3169 |
| 2.0 | 0.3095 | 0.2661 | 0.2572 | 0.2466 |
| 3.0 | 0.1212 | 0.1198 | 0.1186 | 0.1176 |

Below K_c the measure is exactly one at every horizon, as a rotational KAM torus requires, and the
K = 0 control is exact. Above K_c the measure decreases monotonically in K and in T, approaching at
large K to the island measure and, just above K_c, depending strongly on the horizon because
transport through the broken tori is slow. **The decay is graded, not a one-generator cliff**: every
intermediate value is populated, which is the third category the abelian/non-abelian split of
Section 7 needed. We had pre-registered a stronger reading, that no single step on the grid
K = 1.0, 1.2, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0 carries more than half the total drop, and **it held only at
T = 10000**; at the three shorter horizons the step from 1.2 to 1.5 carries more than half.
"Smooth" is therefore a statement about the long-horizon limit, and at finite horizon the melting
concentrates just above K_c. An earlier draft quoted four values here from a diagnostic run whose
script was not retained and whose definition was not stated; those values do not agree with the
present instrument at any horizon (maximum discrepancy 0.13 or worse) and are withdrawn.

(An earlier draft carried here a remark on the shared formalism of orbit quotients with the theory
of homogeneous domains. It transferred no result and named an object foreign to this paper, so it
is removed; the observation lives in the working record.)

## 10. There is no canonical *cost*, but there is a canonical *clock*

Every quantity in this paper is stated relative to a move set. We first hoped the *melting
threshold* would be intrinsic even if R was not, and it is not: two cost functions on the identical
system place melting at incomparable move sets, and the larger set survives -- 546 moves retain 0.69
bits where 154 retain nothing. **No cost function chosen by the modeller yields a convention-free
temperature.**

The resolution is Section 8b. Cost functions are inventions; **rates are physics.** Ordering the
move set by rate and indexing by the observer's horizon gives R(T), which is canonical in the only
sense available: it is relative to an observer, and the observer is part of the system rather than a
convention imposed on it. **This remains a thermodynamics of records relative to a stated move set.
What Section 8b adds is that the relativity is to a CLOCK, not to a taste.** The corollary is methodological and we
think it is the most useful sentence in the paper: *the move set is not a nuisance parameter to be
eliminated. It is the physics.* An application of this theory to any real system is not complete
until the states, the moves and the construction step are named for that system, and we have done
that here for exactly one -- meiosis, in the companion paper.

## 11. A definition of living, offered as a target

Our first attempt -- sustains Delta R > 0, holds H_thermo above a floor, carries two certificates --
is **insufficient, because every one of those can be done TO a system from outside**, and we did
exactly that to a torus fourteen times in the course of this work without producing anything alive.
The distinguishing property is not the trajectory but who performs the refusal.

> **A crystal makes no refusals. A selected population's refusals are made by its environment.
> A living system makes its own.**

With that added, a record system is **living**, at a given move set, if it sustains Delta R > 0 per
construction cycle while holding H_thermo above a floor, carrying two or more certificates on
disjoint supports, **with the refusals performed from inside.** "Inside" is not defined for a bare pair (S, M); this
is offered as a target for a definition, not as one.

**Where such systems sit.** Two criteria give two different answers, and the difference is
instructive. Under a STATIC definition of the living boundary -- as near frozen as possible while
still moving -- the meiotic instance holds H_thermo = 1.0000 bit at every size from n = 6 to n = 16,
one active generator and no more. But that exactness is an artefact of the static definition, not of
the substrate: running a DYNAMIC criterion instead, and selecting trajectories by whether they
SUSTAIN (R rising with H_thermo off the floor), the **ratio** becomes the tight quantity on both
substrates while the absolute floor spreads.

| substrate | H_thermo rel. sd | R/H rel. sd | sustaining R/H |
|---|---|---|---|
| dimers (coupled blocks) | 0.499 | **0.108** | **0.791 +- 0.085** |
| abelian (independent generators) | 0.714 | **0.114** | **0.829 +- 0.095** |

> **Sustaining systems sit HIGH -- above R/H = 0.6 in every case tested, never near the 1/2 we
> predicted and never at either death.** This is a ONE-SIDED BOUND, and we state it as no more
> than that. On the abelian substrate it is not even that: by the closed form below,
> R/H > 0.6 is f < 0.4 + 1/g, a property of the parameter grid we swept. Only the dimer row is
> evidence independent of the grid.

**We withdraw a stronger claim we briefly made.** Having found the sustaining values clustered at
0.79 and 0.83 with small standard deviations, we reported R/H ~ 0.8 as a candidate universal. It is
not. On the abelian substrate the steady state has a closed form: with growth adding g junctions of
which a fraction f are active, and refusal removing one active generator per step, J/n -> f - 1/g
and therefore **R/H -> 1 - f + 1/g**. That formula reproduces every measured value to a mean error
of 0.023 and spans 0.667 to 1.000 across the parameter grid we swept, against a measured range of
0.661 to 0.956. **The apparent clustering is the narrowness of our sweep.** It is the same error
that disqualified our uniform-sampling test, committed a second time in the experiment built to
repair it, and we record it here rather than in a footnote because the failure mode is evidently
easy to repeat: *a tight distribution over a swept parameter is a property of the sweep until shown
otherwise.* What survives is the one-sided bound above, and the observation that two different
substrates gave overlapping ranges -- which is suggestive of substrate-independence and not, on this
evidence, a demonstration of it.
The definition earns its keep by the fact that each way of failing it names something we measured:
*spent* (selection without growth: R peaks, H_thermo exhausts, R decays); *crystal* (growth without
refusal: Theorem 4, Delta R = 0 forever); *crystal at scale* (growth below the sustaining threshold:
R reaches 99 per cent of its ceiling with H_thermo at 0.089); *melted* (a generator crosses the
junction; the certificate fails at the first such move, not gradually); and *gas* (the move set is
ergodic and R = 0). We offer it as a target to be attacked, not as a result.

# Appendix A. Instances

Three record spaces were used. **Dimer coverings** of tori (4x4, 4x6, 6x6; up to 90,176 states)
under alternating-cycle flips, with the winding number as certificate -- exact on the bulk, with 16
classes at extremal winding that are frozen singletons holding 0.018 per cent of the mass.
**Proper colourings** under Kempe moves. **Meiotic phase** under crossovers, treated in the
companion paper. All results are exact enumerations. Predictions were written and hashed before
each run and the digests are retained.

# Appendix B. Errors found and corrected in this work

We list these because the pattern is informative: in two days of work, the numbered theorems stood
unchanged except one clause of Theorem 9, found on cold read by a colleague on the third day (a
zero-shift move was said to be unable to merge classes; it cannot lower the fibre term, which is
weaker), and Theorem 6 was demoted to a remark for applying an edge inequality to a vertex cut;
six informal statements built on the theorems did not survive.

Withdrawn: that duplication is free in R; that the conversion rate 0.50-0.65 is a property of the
record space (it is the greedy policy's); that H_thermo reaches zero exactly at the peak of R (true
in one instance of two, corrected to "at or before"); that complexity lives at the interface without
qualification (only where the junction blocks moves, and at 2.4x not 4x); that a third party is
"too complex" (it is under-used; the cost is seams); and that two competing curves do not cross
(undetermined; read from one point).

Two instrument faults were caught by positive controls and are worth naming. A registration step
that silently failed to execute because of a shell short-circuit, which is why every prediction file
in this work is now hash-verified *before* the run rather than after. And a modelling error in which
states were canonicalised by sorting, so that "the pair (0,1)" silently meant "the two
lexicographically smallest" rather than a fixed pairing; the control that caught it reported zero of
41 states moved by a move that should have moved most of them.

A third, found on audit the next day: the standard-map figures of Section 9a were quoted from a
diagnostic run whose script was never retained, with the quantity undefined. They were replaced by a
registered instrument with the definition frozen before the run (Section 9a states both the
definition and the horizon). A number without a retained instrument is a memory, not a
measurement.
