A boost acts on both factors of a record — it aberrates and flux-boosts the Rac factor (where a source sits in the count) and Doppler-shifts the clock factor (what the source reads) — and a *sample* is a window on one of them. **Window on the clock and read the clock, and the first-order moment cancels:** a top hat on observed redshift z′ selects, in the boosted sky, the same population shifted along β̂ — the window moves with what it reads — so the ℓ = 1 moment of z′ inside the window is zero at first order and only the gradient of dN/dz across the window survives; that is g → 0.05–0.09 in Elie's observed-z bins, and it is the *same* mechanism as the boundary term B_i on the count side, where a window on z′ selects a boosted population and the count moment picks up (1+z)f_b|edges (Eq. (13)) or −∫ f_b d log W_b/d log(1+z) (Eq. (21)). **Window on the Rac factor (flux, counts, the full sample) and read the clock, and nothing cancels:** the window is blind to the reading, so the clock's ℓ = 1 moment carries the full Doppler dipole, g ≈ 1 + m — the same statement, dually, as "window on nothing and read the counts" giving Ellis–Baldwin's 2 + x(1+α). The rule *window one factor, read the other* is Theorem B's factorisation used as a design principle: the two factors are K-independent, so a window on one leaves the other's moment intact and a window on the same one subtracts its own first-order shift. **The flux-cut derivative is the same principle read the other way round.** The Rac factor's *own* anisotropy — the intrinsic dipole — does not know the magnitude limit to first order (it is a property of the population, not of our motion), so its ℓ = 1 moment is cut-*invariant*; the kinematic moment enters through x(1+α) and B, which change with the cut, so it is the cut-*derivative* of the count moment: D(cut₂) − D(cut₁) ≈ [Δ(x(1+α)) + ΔB]·βû isolates the boost within one catalogue with the intrinsic part cancelling, and Mittal & Lewis's simultaneous fit across disjoint flux bins (arXiv:2605.27520, pinned by Grace) is the same lever with the derivative taken by the fit rather than by subtraction. In operator terms: the kinematic ℓ = 1 moment is d(count moment)/d(cut); the intrinsic ℓ = 1 moment is its cut-invariant part; the clock moment is the cut-*and*-window-blind reading of the same boost. **On the face of the note:** first order only — the intrinsic dipole acquires a weak cut dependence through the luminosity function's evolution (the Dalang–Bonvin / Guandalin debate K1898 §3 pins), which is why Elie's S6 must inject an intrinsic dipole and show it cancels, and why this is a v1.3 check and not a v1.2 term; and the whole picture rests on the same identification as before — Rac density = counts, clock charge = observed redshift — stated as identified, not derived.
