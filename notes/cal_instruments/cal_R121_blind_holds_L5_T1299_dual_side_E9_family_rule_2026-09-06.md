## H6 — E9, the family rule for the comb (pre-registration, own convention)
Kernel q₀ = sum of k = n − 2 squares. Anisotropy at 2: k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4} yes, k ≥ 5 no (Hasse; four squares is the Hamilton norm form, ramified at {2, ∞}). But the comb is NOT "anisotropic at 2"; it is "a Jacquet–Langlands surgery at exactly the places where the kernel GROUP is a compact inner form," and that requires (i) a kernel group (k ≥ 2 — at n = 3 the kernel is a point and SO(2,1) is split everywhere: NO comb, the constant term is ξ(2λ)/ξ(2λ + 1)) and (ii) the set of anisotropic places to be finite and to include 2: k = 3 → {2, ∞} (n = 5); k = 4 → {2, ∞} (n = 6). At k = 2 (n = 4) the kernel ⟨1, 1⟩ is anisotropic at 2, ∞ AND every p ≡ 3 mod 4, and the rank-one group is SO(3,1) = PGL₂(ℚ(i)): the 2-adic (and inert-prime) structure sits INSIDE ζ_{ℚ(i)} in numerator and denominator alike — no isolated resonance comb, a Dedekind zeta instead. Predictions: **resonance comb at n ∈ {5, 6} only**; none at n = 3, 4, 7, 8. Quadratic character: it is χ_{disc} with disc = (−1)^{k/2}·det for even k — k = 2: −1 → χ₋₄ (n = 4); **k = 4: (+1)·1 = 1 → NO character at n = 6**; k = 6: −1 → χ₋₄ at n = 8. So "character for every even n" fails at n = 6; the rule is the discriminant, not the parity. Kills for H6: a comb at n = 4 or 7; a character at n = 6; no comb at n = 6.
