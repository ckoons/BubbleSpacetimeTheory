# K1654 — The singleton question resolves to constituents; applied consistently it's ordinary spin-statistics; the paper gates on an identity

The team converged, and the honest answer is the un-flashy one — which is exactly why it's right.

## The resolution: constituents (and the singleton reading was incomplete)
- **Grace (decisive):** the #Rac law *counts Rac constituents* — so the framework is internally committed to the **constituent** reading. You cannot count constituents and simultaneously call the constituent a particle. And the singleton-as-particle reading is *incomplete*: there is no vector singleton (Grace, R16), so a physical photon has no singleton to be.
- **Rac = muon: refuted** — a shared-3/2 conflation (the muon's 3/2 is its ν-address; the Rac's 3/2 is its E₀ — different objects; and the muon is a spinor, the Rac a scalar). 3/2 is an S3 hotspot: muon-address, Rac-energy, ρ₂, α^(−1/12) — four unrelated objects on one number.
- **Rac = Higgs: owes a forced map** — a boundary singleton is not a bulk scalar with a VEV, and OP-6/F603 puts the Higgs condensate at the SO(5)-*vector* (1,0), not the Rac scalar. The Higgs has no settled substrate identity in our own notes.

So **retract "only the Higgs" firmly**: not merely unproven, but *reversed* under a consistent reading.
