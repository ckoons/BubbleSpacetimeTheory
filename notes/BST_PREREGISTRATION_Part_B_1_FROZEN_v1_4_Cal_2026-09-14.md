# Part B.1 — FROZEN v1.4

## 0. What is frozen, in one paragraph
A distant-source catalogue with per-source redshifts is cut into fixed redshift bins. In each bin, the source-count dipole vector is measured by a fixed estimator with a fixed mask and fixed weights. The dipole in every bin is modelled as ONE kinematic vector, common to all bins up to the bin's own Ellis–Baldwin factor, plus ONE intrinsic (clustering) vector with a fixed decreasing redshift profile. The fit returns the common boost. **P1 says the common boost is the CMB boost — amplitude and direction — and it is stated so that a large intrinsic dipole neither rescues nor condemns it.** The three landings partition every possible outcome; the hatch is a finite list of named tests with pass/fail written here; nothing in Sections 2–6 may be adjusted after a real dipole is seen.

## 1. The claim (P1), the presupposition, and the inputs
**P1 (as narrowed by Lyra R145 L1, narrowings (a) and (b), and carried here).** Let $\mathbf{D}_i$ be the source-count dipole vector in redshift bin $i$ (Section 2), with per-bin factor $f_i = 2 + x_i(1+\alpha_i) + B_i$ (Section 3; $B_i$ is the observed-redshift boundary term of Section 3.4). Under the model of Section 4, the fitted common boost $(\beta, \hat{u})$ is **one common velocity, of which the CMB's is the best-measured instance** — operationally, it equals the CMB boost $(\beta_{\rm CMB}, \hat{u}_{\rm CMB})$ within the tolerances of Section 5 (narrowing (a): the rows license one light-defined frame, not a photon privilege; the comparison is to Planck 2018 because that is the best ruler, not because the CMB is the law). **The claim is made in every bin that the separation model of Section 4 assigns to the exterior** (narrowing (b)): P1 makes no claim about a bin whose dipole the fit assigns to the intrinsic term, and the rows fix no depth — at low redshift a population shares the local bulk flow, which is peculiar velocity, not a second frame. The CMB boost's values are **Grace's pin from Planck 2018** (the draft quotes $369.82 \pm 0.11$ km s$^{-1}$ toward $(l,b) = (264.02°, 48.25°)$; the pinned primary governs, and the frozen quantity is *whatever the pin says*, not these digits).
**Presupposition on the face of the claim:** the CMB dipole is read as purely kinematic. Support: Planck's aberration and modulation measurement (Grace pins the primary and its significance). A partly intrinsic CMB dipole is a named alternative OUTSIDE this test; if it is ever established, P1 is re-stated against the kinematic part and this freeze does not cover that case.
**Prior art, consistent (K1898 §1; found on the day of registration, not after).** Wu & Xia, "Measuring peculiar velocity and tomographic redshift dipole with DESI DR1 catalogs," arXiv:2608.30914 (submitted 31 Aug 2026, revised 2 Sep 2026; abstract read), measure the boost by the *redshift-dipole* method — the Doppler modulation of observed redshifts, tomographically over $0.1 < z < 2.1$ — and report for the high-redshift QSO sample $v = 357.95^{+55.05}_{-48.47}$ km s$^{-1}$, in agreement with the CMB-inferred $369.82 \pm 0.11$. That is P1's Landing A on a different catalogue by a different estimator, two weeks before this file. **P1 is therefore registered as a REPLICATION with an independent estimator (Ellis–Baldwin number counts in bins, intrinsic part separated) on an independent catalogue**, not as a first test; its value is that the two methods carry different systematics and different contaminants (counts: clustering; redshifts: source bulk flow). The register's A9 row carries "prior art consistent" in its current-state column (Grace pins the DOI).

**The evolution debate, and why the per-bin prescription is the answer to it (K1898 §3).** Dalang & Bonvin 2022 (MNRAS 512, 3895; corrections MNRAS 521, 2225) and Guandalin, Piat, Clarkson & Maartens 2023 (ApJ 953, 144) show that luminosity-function evolution — $x$ and $\alpha$ varying with distance — can bias the *projected* expected dipole by more than $3\sigma$; von Hausegger 2024 (MNRAS Lett 535, L49) shows the Ellis–Baldwin test is robust to such evolution when $x$ and $\alpha$ are measured on the sample as selected. This freeze measures $(x_i, \alpha_i)$ **per bin, from the bin's own sources near the selection limit** (Section 3), so the evolution the projection argument worries about is absorbed by the binning; von Hausegger & Dalang (their footnote 4) say the same of $\alpha$ evolving with cosmological redshift. Ellis & Baldwin's scope sentence (p. 379, Grace's pin) is the license: irrespective of selection effects or source evolution, as long as the forward and backward measurements are done in the identical manner.

**Power, stated before the run, from the controls and not from an envelope (Elie 5757, 5759; K1900 §1 retires Keeper's per-bin envelope as a power expectation, not a criterion).** Count channel alone, WITH the boundary term: $\sigma_\beta c \approx 443$ km s$^{-1}$ at Quaia depth (Elie 5758; 207 without the term; v1.2's "150" was 5757's pre-term number, K1901 §3). Redshift channel ALONE: $\approx 170$ km s$^{-1}$ on the full sample and $250$–$415$ per flux quartile (Elie 5759). **The joint two-channel fit of 4.5(b) — count + redshift, both variants (full-sample moment; five flux-quantile moments): combined $\sigma_\beta c = 149$ km s$^{-1}$ in each (Elie 5760), calibrated (scatter/quote 0.93 over 20 seeds), recovered $\hat\beta/\beta_{\rm inj} = 1.20 \pm 0.09$ on the norm — which is the norm bias §5 now avoids — posted before step (iv) as required (Elie 5760).** $149 < \beta_{\rm CMB}/2 \approx 185$: **Quaia is A/B-capable at ≈ 2.5σ per run, known before the catalogue is opened.** A 3σ-equivalent Landing B (χ²₃ ≥ 14.156) at this precision requires a common boost of order 450 km s$^{-1}$ off the CMB's; DESI DR1's redshift method reaches $\pm 50$. Quaia's value is independence of method and contaminant, and direction.

**Inputs, named:** T2564/K1522 (the frame is the exterior's state recorded through the photon channel — one exterior, no matter channel); T2565 (geometry alone does not select the frame; only the velocity is the missing input); Cal §957 (ε is the rapidity between $u_\gamma$ and $u_m$, each an ℓ = 1 moment on the induced slice). **Scope, as the rows license it:** the rows license ONE boost. They say nothing about the intrinsic component, which is astrophysics (P2 of the draft). Lyra's L1 row may narrow P1 before the run; it may not widen it.

## 3. Per-bin $(x_i, \alpha_i)$ — computed within the mask, BEFORE any dipole, from the catalogue itself
3.1 **$x_i$ (v1.3, K1903 §2(a)):** the logarithmic slope of the integral counts in the selection band AT the selection limit: with $m$ the selection-band magnitude and $N_i(<m)$ the weighted cumulative count in bin $i$, $x_i = \dfrac{1}{0.4}\,\dfrac{d\log_{10}N_i(<m)}{dm}\Big|_{m = m_{\rm lim}}$, evaluated as the **derivative at $m_{\rm lim}$ of a quadratic least-squares fit of $\log_{10}N_i(<m)$ over the last 0.5 mag below the limit** — the window is unchanged; the estimator inside it is the edge derivative, not the window's secant. Reason, written now: Ellis–Baldwin's $x$ is the slope at the threshold (Secrest, von Hausegger, Rameez, Mohayaee & Sarkar, "Forty years of the Ellis–Baldwin test," arXiv:2501.06450, p. 2: robust against evolution "if measurements of $x$ and $\alpha$ are made at the threshold of a flux-limited survey"; their ref. 4 = von Hausegger 2024), and on a curved count law an octave average is a secant — Elie 5760's synthetic gives 0.859 (secant) against 0.600 (local), a 40 % error entering $f_i$ at order one in every bin. **Diagnostics and the x-systematic (v1.4, board 12:41, chosen blind on Elie 5761: on a power law the linear and quadratic-at-edge estimators agree to 0.004; on a kink inside the window they bracket the truth, 0.844 / 0.345 against 0.600, and a 0.2-mag local slope hits 0.600):** the frozen estimator REMAINS the quadratic derivative at the edge over 0.5 mag; the linear 0.5-mag slope AND a 0.2-mag local linear slope are posted beside it at step (v) as diagnostics; **the half-spread $|x_{\rm quad} - x_{\rm lin}|/2$ enters $\sigma_{f_i}$ in quadrature as the x-systematic**; a bin whose three estimates disagree beyond $2\sigma_{\rm sys}$ is FLAGGED "curved counts" in the (v) table and reported with the run — never re-estimated. Choosing the estimator after seeing x on the sky would be a tuning channel; the procedure is frozen and the spread is a systematic. The uncertainty on $x_i$ is the fit's propagated derivative uncertainty combined in quadrature with the x-systematic. (For a flux-limited radio catalogue in a later run the standard $N(>S)\propto S^{-x}$ form replaces this; not this run.)
3.2 **$\alpha_i$:** the effective spectral index between the catalogue's two selection bands (Gaia $G$ and unWISE $W1$ for Quaia), $\alpha = -\log(S_G/S_{W1})/\log(\nu_G/\nu_{W1})$ per source from the published photometry, and $\alpha_i$ = the weighted MEDIAN over the bin. Uncertainty on $\alpha_i$: the bin's median absolute deviation scaled by 1.4826, divided by $\sqrt{N_i}$.
3.3 **$f_i = 2 + x_i(1+\alpha_i) + B_i$**, with its uncertainty propagated from 3.1–3.2 and 3.4. **$x_i$, $\alpha_i$ and $B_i$ are computed and posted (by Elie, on the board, with the run's hash) BEFORE the dipole estimator is run on the catalogue.** A value changed after a dipole is seen voids the run.
3.4 **The observed-redshift boundary term $B_i$ (von Hausegger & Dalang, arXiv:2412.13162v2, read from the paper; Lyra's narrowing (c)).** A boost Doppler-shifts every observed redshift, $(1+z) = (1+\bar z)(1-\beta\cos\theta)$ (their Eq. 2), so a bin selected on observed redshift gains and loses sources across its edges along the velocity direction; the kinematic dipole of a bin is therefore (their Eq. 13, for a sharp top-hat $[z_1, z_2)$ on observed redshift) $\mathcal{D}_{\rm kin}(z_b) = [\,2 + \tilde x_b(1+\tilde\alpha_b) + (1+z) f_b(z)\big|_{z_1}^{z_2}\,]\,\beta$, with $f_b(z) = n(z)/\int_{z_1}^{z_2} n\,dz$ the bin's own normalised redshift distribution — the boundary term $B(z_b) \equiv (1+z) f_b(z)|_{z_1}^{z_2}$ (their Eq. 14) is *readable from the data* (their words) with no luminosity function. For a selection with redshift uncertainty (their Sec. III, Eqs. 15–17, 20, 21) the term becomes $B(z_b) = -\int_0^\infty dz\, f_b(z)\, \dfrac{d\log W_b(z)}{d\log(1+z)}$ with $W_b(z)$ the bin's selection function on true redshift, which reduces to Eq. 13 for a sharp cut (their Eqs. 22–23). **Frozen prescription:** $B_i$ is computed by the smooth form (Eq. 21) with $W_i(z) = \int dz'\, W'_i(z')\, P(z'|z)$ (Eq. 16), $W'_i$ the top-hat of bin $i$ on the catalogue's reported redshift and $P(z'|z)$ Gaussian with the catalogue's per-source uncertainty column **`redshift_quaia_err`** (Quaia, arXiv:2306.17749 p. 21, Table 2 — Grace's pin; Table 2 carries no second σ_z column); at the first opening the FITS header is recorded verbatim on the board, and any mismatch between the header and Table 2 is reported before step (v), never resolved silently, and $n(z)$ taken as the observed distribution within the mask (the deconvolution of Eq. 15 is neglected; the stated approximation, valid because the catalogue's $\sigma_z/(1+z) \approx 0.01$ is an order of magnitude below the bin widths of 2.5; H6 tests it). The sharp form (Eq. 13) is computed and reported beside it; a difference exceeding the propagated uncertainty on $B_i$ is a Landing-C trigger (Section 4.4). **Two terms the paper names and this freeze does NOT model, declared:** a flux-cut dependence of the redshift selection (their Sec. III C, deferred by the authors) — sized here by hatch check H2; and selection cuts on variables correlated with observed redshift, e.g. colour (their Sec. V and App. D; Quaia's classification uses colours) — sized by H5. **Magnitude, from the paper and not from any back-of-envelope:** for a constant $f$ the boundary term contributes $\mathcal{O}(20\%)$ of the Ellis–Baldwin amplitude; for realistic $n(z)$ it is *comparable to or larger than* the $2 + x(1+\alpha)$ term and changes sign across the peak of $n(z)$ (their Figs. 1–4). It is therefore not a correction to P1's per-bin factor; it IS part of it, and the model of 4.2 carries $f_i$ with $B_i$ inside. Elie's control S4 (a boosted synthetic sky binned on *observed* redshift) must land A with $B_i$ and is posted mis-landing without it.

## 4. The estimator, the model, the fit, and the Landing-C criterion — fixed now
4.1 **Estimator (Elie's E1, validated on E2 before use):** the weighted unit-vector sum $\mathbf{D}_i = \dfrac{3}{W_i}\sum_{s\in i} w_s\,\hat{n}_s$ corrected for the mask's own dipole by the standard mask-mode correction (the same estimator applied to a uniform sky under the same mask returns the correction vector, which is subtracted). Uncertainty: the covariance of $\mathbf{D}_i$ from 1,000 isotropic Poisson mocks drawn under the same mask, weights and $N_i$. The estimator's code is retained with the run and is not edited after the catalogue is opened.
4.2 **Model (the separation):** $\mathbf{D}_i = f_i\,\beta\,\hat{u} + A\,w_i\,\hat{w}$, with $f_i = 2 + x_i(1+\alpha_i) + B_i$ per Section 3 (the boundary term is a per-bin scalar multiplying the SAME common vector, so the model stays one boost), with ONE boost $(\beta,\hat{u})$ (3 parameters), ONE intrinsic direction $\hat{w}$ (2 parameters), ONE intrinsic amplitude $A \geq 0$ (1 parameter), and the FROZEN profile $w_i = (\bar\chi_1/\bar\chi_i)^2$, where $\bar\chi_i$ is the weighted median comoving distance of bin $i$ in flat ΛCDM with $H_0 = 67.4$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_m = 0.315$ (Planck 2018 base; Grace pins). Reason for the profile, written now: the clustering dipole of a shell falls with the shell's distance roughly as the inverse square (a fixed local structure subtends a solid angle falling as $1/\chi^2$), and $w_1 = 1$ normalises it to the lowest bin, where it dominates. **Named alternative, reported but NOT deciding:** $w_i = \bar\chi_1/\bar\chi_i$. The landing is read from the primary profile only.
4.3 **Fit:** weighted least squares over the five bins (15 data, 6 parameters) with the covariances of 4.1; $\beta$ and $A$ bounded below by 0; the fitted $(\beta,\hat{u})$ carry a covariance from which the amplitude uncertainty $\sigma_\beta$ and the 95% and 99% positional regions of $\hat{u}$ (2D confidence regions on the sphere from the fit's direction covariance) are read.
4.4 **Landing C — NOT DECIDABLE — criterion (fixed now):** $\sigma_\beta \geq \beta_{\rm CMB}/2$ (the run cannot separate "no boost" from "the CMB boost" at $2\sigma$), OR the fit's 95% positional region on $\hat{u}$ covers more than 25% of the sphere, OR the profile-alternative fit of 4.2 moves $\beta$ by more than $2\sigma_\beta$ (the separation is profile-dominated), OR (v1.4, K1905 §4 — concurred: the v1.2 clause "sharp and smooth forms differ by more than the propagated uncertainty" is an impossible requirement at $\sigma_z/(1+z) \approx 0.04$, where the two forms MUST differ, and a requirement no assembly can meet is not a requirement of the falsifier, K1827) **the smooth form of 3.4 is not determined by its own inputs: $B_i$ moves by more than TWICE its Poisson uncertainty $\sigma_{B_i}$ (from the noise in $n(z)$ at the edges) under either (b) the per-source $\sigma_z$ scaled $\times 0.5$ and $\times 2$, or (c) a catastrophic-outlier component at the paper's published fraction for the chosen cut (Grace pins; 6 %/10 % at $G < 20.0$) placed uniformly in $z$** — the sharp form stays posted as a diagnostic of how much the smoothing matters and is no longer a criterion; the top bin is expected to carry this test (its $B$ is read through the steep fall of $dN/dz$ above 2.4 and the outlier tail), OR the two channels of 4.5 disagree (Landing C′). Any of the five → Landing C, reported with the numbers; the row stays A9-live; the next catalogue (CatWISE2020) is named.

4.4a **The kinematic null bin — a CHECK, not a calibrator (v1.2; K1899 §5, K1900 §2; Elie 5759 S5: holds as a null, kinematic share 0.00–0.01, direction inside its cone; no precision as a calibrator, σ_Â = 0.026 against A = 0.05 at Quaia depth).** A bin is a KINEMATIC NULL iff its per-bin factor with the boundary term is consistent with zero, $|f_i| = |2 + x_i(1+\alpha_i) + B_i| < 2\sigma_{f_i}$, decided at step (v) from the catalogue's own $n(z)$ and posted with $f_i$ — a rule frozen now, a membership decided by the data. A null bin carries no kinematic lever arm, so it enters the fit of 4.2 ONLY through the intrinsic term $A\,w_i\,\hat w$ (its kinematic term is identically the null's small $f_i\beta\hat u$ and adds nothing); after the fit, the null bin's measured $\mathbf D_i$ must agree with the fitted $A\,w_i\,\hat w$ within the bin's covariance ($\chi^2$, 3 d.o.f., $p \ge 0.01$). If it does not, the frozen profile is refuted by its own null and the run is Landing C with that reason named (the alternative profile of 4.2 is reported, not substituted). A null bin never counts toward Landing A's per-bin residual test and never toward Landing B; P1 makes no claim in it (narrowing (b)). It does not calibrate $A$: S5 shows the null's amplitude estimate is unbiased but has no precision at this depth, so $A$ is fitted from all bins as in 4.3 and the null is a consistency check only.

4.5 **The second channel — P3 as ruled (Cal §965), RE-WINDOWED in v1.2 (K1900 §2 (iii), control-forced): P1 measured through the redshift dipole, not a second registered claim.** The same boost that aberrates positions and boosts fluxes Doppler-shifts every observed redshift (3.4, Eq. 2), so the ℓ = 1 moment of the redshift field on the sky — the Wu–Xia observable — carries the same $\beta\hat{u}$. **Windowing, corrected before any catalogue by the channel's own control (Elie 5759): a top-hat window on observed redshift is a window on the very variable the boost shifts, and the moment collapses to first order ($g \approx 0.05$–$0.09$ in the interior bins) — the same mechanism as $B_i$ on the count side. The redshift channel is therefore windowed on the FULL sample (primary; one moment $\mathbf R$, factor $g$, $\sigma_v \approx 170$ km s$^{-1}$ on synthetic Quaia) and on FLUX quantiles (secondary; the four quartiles of the catalogue's G-band magnitude column as Grace pins it from Table 2; one moment $\mathbf R_w$ and factor $g_w$ per quartile, $\sigma_v \approx 250$–$415$ km s$^{-1}$ each) — never on redshift.** Each window's factor $g$ is computed from the window's own observed $(1+z)$ distribution by the retained library of Elie's 5759 (hashed with the run) and posted at step (v) before any dipole; the estimator's positive control (5759: a boosted sky lands A′; a 600 km s$^{-1}$ source bulk flow with no observer boost lands C′, channels 112° apart) is the license. Its contaminant is not clustering but a coherent bulk flow of the sources (von Hausegger & Dalang, Sec. V) — a different contaminant from the count channel's, which is the point. **Frozen use — two fits.** (a) *The consistency fit:* $(\beta_z, \hat{u}_z)$ is fitted from the redshift moments alone, WITHOUT a profile term (with flux windows the depth lever arm is gone and an intrinsic term is degenerate with the boost; a source bulk flow is the channel's contaminant and is what C′ detects), and compared with the count channel's own single-channel $(\beta, \hat{u})$:  **Landing A′** iff $|\beta_z - \beta| \leq 2\sqrt{\sigma_{\beta_z}^2 + \sigma_\beta^2}$ AND each channel's best direction lies inside the other's 95% region; otherwise **Landing C′**, which routes P1 to Landing C (the two readings of one boost disagree, so at least one channel carries an unmodelled contaminant, and the run cannot decide P1). (b) *The joint fit (K1900 §2 (iii); prior art Nadolny, Durrer, Kunz & Padmanabhan 2021, JCAP 11, 009, arXiv:2106.05284 — positions, fluxes and redshifts fitted together for one velocity independent of the intrinsic dipole; Grace pins):* the count moments $\{\mathbf D_i\}$ with their factors $f_i$ and the profile term, and the redshift moments $\{\mathbf R, \mathbf R_w\}$ with their factors $g, g_w$ and no intrinsic term, are fitted TOGETHER for ONE shared vector $\beta\hat u$ by weighted least squares with the mock covariances of 4.1 (redshift-moment covariances from the same isotropic mocks); **the joint $(\beta,\hat u)$ with its $\sigma_\beta$ and positional regions is the quantity Section 5 compares to the CMB, and 4.4's Landing-C criterion is evaluated on the joint $\sigma_\beta$.** Landing A requires BOTH the joint fit inside Section 5's tolerances AND Landing A′ from fit (a). The redshift channel does not fire the descent by itself and does not hold it: **it can veto a Landing A and it cannot manufacture one** — in the joint fit it adds precision to one vector, not a second vector. In the corpus's own taxonomy it is a CHECK that can fail, not a second leg; it gets no separate registry row (Cal §940's rule: a consequence implied by the applied claim is a check, and the same claim is not registered twice). Lyra's operator statement — the count dipole as the ℓ = 1 moment of the Rac factor and the redshift dipole as the ℓ = 1 moment of the clock factor, one vector under one boost (K1898 §5; Theorem B) — is the corpus-native form of why the two channels must agree, and it is carried as a row NOTE, not as a claim of this file.

## 5. Tolerances and the three landings — fixed now, partitioning every outcome
**(v1.3, K1903 §2(b): the comparison is made on the fitted VECTOR, not on its norm.)** Let $\mathbf b = \beta\hat u$ be the joint fit's boost vector (4.5(b)) with covariance $\Sigma_b$ (4.3), and let $\mathbf b_{\rm CMB} = \beta_{\rm CMB}\hat u_{\rm CMB}$ with $\Sigma_{\rm CMB} = \sigma_{\rm CMB}^2\,\mathbf 1$ (Grace's pin). Define $\chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf c) = (\mathbf b - \mathbf c)^{\sf T}(\Sigma_b + \Sigma_{\rm CMB})^{-1}(\mathbf b - \mathbf c)$. Reason, written now: at S/N ≈ 2.5 the norm of a noisy 3-vector is biased upward ($E|\hat{\mathbf b}|^2 = |\mathbf b|^2 + \operatorname{tr}\Sigma_b$; Elie 5760: $\hat\beta/\beta_{\rm inj} = 1.20 \pm 0.09$ joint, 1.87 count-only), so a norm test leans toward a false Landing B — the one error the program has committed before (K1810); the ℓ = 1 moment is a vector in the SO(3) triplet and its norm is a coordinate. Prior art on estimator amplitude bias: Siewert, Schmidt-Rubart & Schwarz, A&A 653, A9 (2021), arXiv:2010.08366 (Grace pins). The debiased norm $\sqrt{|\mathbf b|^2 - \operatorname{tr}\Sigma_b}$ and the 95 %/99 % positional regions are POSTED as diagnostics, never used as the criterion.
- **Landing A — P1 HOLDS:** NOT Landing C (including C′ of 4.5), AND $\chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf b_{\rm CMB}) \le 7.815$ (95 %), AND, after subtracting the fitted intrinsic term, no non-null bin's residual $\mathbf{D}_i - A w_i \hat{w} - f_i\mathbf b_{\rm CMB}$ has $\chi^2$ (3 d.o.f., bin covariance) with $p < 0.01$, AND Landing A′ of 4.5(a).
- **Landing B — P1 FIRES (A9's kill condition met; the Machian descent falsified):** NOT Landing C, AND ( $\chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf b_{\rm CMB}) \ge 14.156$ (the 3σ-equivalent of χ²₃) OR $\mathbf b$ is consistent with ZERO — $\chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf 0) \le 7.815$ while $\sigma_\beta < \beta_{\rm CMB}/2$: no boost is not one boost ), AND every hatch check of Section 6 PASSES (the discrepancy survives all of them).
- **Landing C — NOT DECIDABLE:** the criterion of 4.4, OR any outcome between A and B ($7.815 < \chi^2_3 < 14.156$; an A-level χ² with a failed per-bin residual or a failed A′), OR a B-level discrepancy that FAILS a hatch check (then "did not survive the hatch," reported with the failing check named — not a fire, and not a hold either).
- **Does NOT fire, by construction:** the amplitude of the intrinsic term $A$, whatever it is; the total dipole's amplitude; an angle alone; the norm of $\mathbf b$ alone; any single bin.
Every outcome is exactly one of A, B, C. There is no fourth word.

## 6. The hatch, closed into named tests (carried from 08-21 §5 and made finite)
A Landing-B discrepancy is a fire only if ALL of the following pass. Each is a test with a written criterion; none is "an astrophysical account."
- **H1 Galactic mask:** repeat the fit with $|b| > 20°$ and with $|b| > 40°$; PASS iff the fitted $\beta$ and $\hat{u}$ move by less than $1\sigma_\beta$ and less than the 68% positional radius respectively.
- **H2 Magnitude/flux limit:** repeat with the selection limit brightened by 0.25 mag; PASS iff the same stability holds and the recomputed $f_i$ change by less than their propagated uncertainties.
- **H3 Ecliptic / Gaia scanning-law imprint:** (a) the dipole of the published selection-function map itself is computed and reported; (b) the fit is repeated with an additional frozen nuisance template — an ecliptic dipole and ecliptic quadrupole (5 amplitudes, fixed basis) marginalised over; PASS iff $\beta$ and $\hat{u}$ move by less than $1\sigma_\beta$ and the 68% radius; (c) the fit is repeated WITHOUT the selection-function weights of 2.3; PASS iff the discrepancy that triggered B is present in BOTH the weighted and the unweighted fit (a fire that appears in only one is a systematic).
- **H4 Malmquist / redshift-error mixing:** repeat with all bin edges shifted by $+0.1$ and by $-0.1$ in $z$; PASS iff the B-level discrepancy persists in both shifted binnings.
- **H5 Stellar contamination and dust:** repeat with the catalogue's published quasar-probability threshold raised by 0.1 and with an additional $E(B-V) < 0.05$ cut (the dust map Grace pins); PASS iff the discrepancy persists.
- **H6 Photometric-redshift outliers:** repeat with the catalogue's published redshift-uncertainty column cut at its median; PASS iff the discrepancy persists.
A discrepancy that fails any of H1–H6 is reported as Landing C with the failing check named. The list is closed: no seventh check may be added after a real dipole is seen, and none of H1–H6 may be dropped.

## 8. C2 — the landings can fail (written before the hash)
- **P1 can fail.** Landing B is reached by any of: a common boost of amplitude $\geq 3\sigma$ from $\beta_{\rm CMB}$ (including $\beta \approx 0$: a sky with no kinematic dipole at all falsifies P1, since "one boost" is not "no boost"); or a direction outside the 99% region; each surviving H1–H6.
- **Landing A is not vacuous — the synthetic cases that produce B under this exact protocol:** (S1) an isotropic population boosted at $2\beta_{\rm CMB}$ toward $\hat{u}_{\rm CMB}$ plus a clustering dipole with the Section-4.2 profile: the fit returns $\beta \approx 2\beta_{\rm CMB}$, $|\Delta\beta| \gg 3\sigma$ → B. (S2) boosted at $\beta_{\rm CMB}$ toward a direction $60°$ from $\hat{u}_{\rm CMB}$: the CMB direction falls outside the 99% region → B. (S3) NO boost, clustering only (Elie's negative control): the fit returns $\beta$ consistent with zero with $\sigma_\beta < \beta_{\rm CMB}/2$ → B. (S4, added in v1.1) a sky boosted at $\beta_{\rm CMB}$ toward $\hat{u}_{\rm CMB}$ and binned on OBSERVED redshift with a realistic $n(z)$: lands A with $B_i$ in the model and mis-lands (a false C or B) without it — the number is posted; a protocol without $B_i$ fails its own positive control, which is why $B_i$ is in the per-bin factor and not in the hatch. Elie's E2 must show S3 lands B and the positive control (boost at $\beta_{\rm CMB}$ toward $\hat{u}_{\rm CMB}$ plus clustering) lands A; a protocol that lands A on S1–S3 is broken and is not run on data.
- **No clause lets a large intrinsic dipole rescue P1.** The intrinsic term enters ONLY as $A\,w_i\,\hat{w}$ with the profile frozen and decreasing; it cannot absorb a redshift-INDEPENDENT residual, because a vector constant across bins is exactly what the kinematic term is and the profile is not constant. The Landing-C criterion (4.4) catches the case where the profile and the kinematic term become degenerate and reports it as undecidable, never as a hold. The hatch (Section 6) is a closed list of tests, not an "astrophysical account": a B-level discrepancy is either a fire or a named failed check; "the intrinsic dipole is large" is not on the list and cannot be invoked.
- **What would make the freeze itself wrong:** the Ellis–Baldwin form not applying to a magnitude-limited optical/IR sample as prescribed in Section 3 — then E2's positive control fails to recover the injected boost and the run stops there; that is what the control is for.
