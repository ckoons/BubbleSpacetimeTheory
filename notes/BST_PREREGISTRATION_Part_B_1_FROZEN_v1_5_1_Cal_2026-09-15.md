# Part B.1 — FROZEN v1.4

## 0. What is frozen, in one paragraph
A distant-source catalogue with per-source redshifts is cut into fixed redshift bins. In each bin, the source-count dipole vector is measured by a fixed estimator with a fixed mask and fixed weights. The dipole in every bin is modelled as ONE kinematic vector, common to all bins up to the bin's own Ellis–Baldwin factor, plus ONE intrinsic (clustering) vector with a fixed decreasing redshift profile. The fit returns the common boost. **P1 says the common boost is the CMB boost — amplitude and direction — and it is stated so that a large intrinsic dipole neither rescues nor condemns it.** The three landings partition every possible outcome; the hatch is a finite list of named tests with pass/fail written here; nothing in Sections 2–6 may be adjusted after a real dipole is seen.

## 1. The claim (P1), the presupposition, and the inputs
**P1 (as narrowed by Lyra R145 L1, narrowings (a) and (b), and carried here).** Let $\mathbf{D}_i$ be the source-count dipole vector in redshift bin $i$ (Section 2), with per-bin factor $f_i = 2 + x_i(1+\alpha_i) + B_i$ (Section 3; $B_i$ is the observed-redshift boundary term of Section 3.4). Under the model of Section 4, the fitted common boost $(\beta, \hat{u})$ is **one common velocity, of which the CMB's is the best-measured instance** — operationally, it equals the CMB boost $(\beta_{\rm CMB}, \hat{u}_{\rm CMB})$ within the tolerances of Section 5 (narrowing (a): the rows license one light-defined frame, not a photon privilege; the comparison is to Planck 2018 because that is the best ruler, not because the CMB is the law). **The claim is made in every bin that the separation model of Section 4 assigns to the exterior** (narrowing (b)): P1 makes no claim about a bin whose dipole the fit assigns to the intrinsic term, and the rows fix no depth — at low redshift a population shares the local bulk flow, which is peculiar velocity, not a second frame. The CMB boost's values are **Grace's pin from Planck 2018** (the draft quotes $369.82 \pm 0.11$ km s$^{-1}$ toward $(l,b) = (264.02°, 48.25°)$; the pinned primary governs, and the frozen quantity is *whatever the pin says*, not these digits).
**Presupposition on the face of the claim:** the CMB dipole is read as purely kinematic. Support: Planck's aberration and modulation measurement (Grace pins the primary and its significance). A partly intrinsic CMB dipole is a named alternative OUTSIDE this test; if it is ever established, P1 is re-stated against the kinematic part and this freeze does not cover that case.
**Prior art, consistent (K1898 §1; found on the day of registration, not after).** Wu & Xia, "Measuring peculiar velocity and tomographic redshift dipole with DESI DR1 catalogs," arXiv:2608.30914 (submitted 31 Aug 2026, revised 2 Sep 2026; abstract read), measure the boost by the *redshift-dipole* method — the Doppler modulation of observed redshifts, tomographically over $0.1 < z < 2.1$ — and report for the high-redshift QSO sample $v = 357.95^{+55.05}_{-48.47}$ km s$^{-1}$, in agreement with the CMB-inferred $369.82 \pm 0.11$. That is P1's Landing A on a different catalogue by a different estimator, two weeks before this file. **P1 is therefore registered as a REPLICATION with an independent estimator (Ellis–Baldwin number counts in bins, intrinsic part separated) on an independent catalogue**, not as a first test; its value is that the two methods carry different systematics and different contaminants (counts: clustering; redshifts: source bulk flow). The register's A9 row carries "prior art consistent" in its current-state column (Grace pins the DOI).

**The evolution debate, and why the per-bin prescription is the answer to it (K1898 §3).** Dalang & Bonvin 2022 (MNRAS 512, 3895; corrections MNRAS 521, 2225) and Guandalin, Piat, Clarkson & Maartens 2023 (ApJ 953, 144) show that luminosity-function evolution — $x$ and $\alpha$ varying with distance — can bias the *projected* expected dipole by more than $3\sigma$; von Hausegger 2024 (MNRAS Lett 535, L49) shows the Ellis–Baldwin test is robust to such evolution when $x$ and $\alpha$ are measured on the sample as selected. This freeze measures $(x_i, \alpha_i)$ **per bin, from the bin's own sources near the selection limit** (Section 3), so the evolution the projection argument worries about is absorbed by the binning; von Hausegger & Dalang (their footnote 4) say the same of $\alpha$ evolving with cosmological redshift. Ellis & Baldwin's scope sentence (p. 379, Grace's pin) is the license: irrespective of selection effects or source evolution, as long as the forward and backward measurements are done in the identical manner.

## 3. Per-bin $(x_i, \alpha_i)$ — computed within the mask, BEFORE any dipole, from the catalogue itself
3.1 **$x_i$ (v1.3, K1903 §2(a)):** the logarithmic slope of the integral counts in the selection band AT the selection limit: with $m$ the selection-band magnitude and $N_i(<m)$ the weighted cumulative count in bin $i$, $x_i = \dfrac{1}{0.4}\,\dfrac{d\log_{10}N_i(<m)}{dm}\Big|_{m = m_{\rm lim}}$, evaluated as the **derivative at $m_{\rm lim}$ of a quadratic least-squares fit of $\log_{10}N_i(<m)$ over the last 0.5 mag below the limit** — the window is unchanged; the estimator inside it is the edge derivative, not the window's secant. Reason, written now: Ellis–Baldwin's $x$ is the slope at the threshold (Secrest, von Hausegger, Rameez, Mohayaee & Sarkar, "Forty years of the Ellis–Baldwin test," arXiv:2501.06450, p. 2: robust against evolution "if measurements of $x$ and $\alpha$ are made at the threshold of a flux-limited survey"; their ref. 4 = von Hausegger 2024), and on a curved count law an octave average is a secant — Elie 5760's synthetic gives 0.859 (secant) against 0.600 (local), a 40 % error entering $f_i$ at order one in every bin. **Diagnostics and the x-systematic (v1.4, board 12:41, chosen blind on Elie 5761: on a power law the linear and quadratic-at-edge estimators agree to 0.004; on a kink inside the window they bracket the truth, 0.844 / 0.345 against 0.600, and a 0.2-mag local slope hits 0.600):** the frozen estimator REMAINS the quadratic derivative at the edge over 0.5 mag; the linear 0.5-mag slope AND a 0.2-mag local linear slope are posted beside it at step (v) as diagnostics; **the half-spread $|x_{\rm quad} - x_{\rm lin}|/2$ enters $\sigma_{f_i}$ in quadrature as the x-systematic**; a bin whose three estimates disagree beyond $2\sigma_{\rm sys}$ is FLAGGED "curved counts" in the (v) table and reported with the run — never re-estimated. Choosing the estimator after seeing x on the sky would be a tuning channel; the procedure is frozen and the spread is a systematic. The uncertainty on $x_i$ is the fit's propagated derivative uncertainty combined in quadrature with the x-systematic. (For a flux-limited radio catalogue in a later run the standard $N(>S)\propto S^{-x}$ form replaces this; not this run.)
3.2 **$\alpha_i$:** the effective spectral index between the catalogue's two selection bands (Gaia $G$ and unWISE $W1$ for Quaia), $\alpha = -\log(S_G/S_{W1})/\log(\nu_G/\nu_{W1})$ per source from the published photometry, and $\alpha_i$ = the weighted MEDIAN over the bin. Uncertainty on $\alpha_i$: the bin's median absolute deviation scaled by 1.4826, divided by $\sqrt{N_i}$.
3.3 **$f_i = 2 + x_i(1+\alpha_i) + B_i$**, with its uncertainty propagated from 3.1–3.2 and 3.4. **$x_i$, $\alpha_i$ and $B_i$ are computed and posted (by Elie, on the board, with the run's hash) BEFORE the dipole estimator is run on the catalogue.** A value changed after a dipole is seen voids the run.
3.4 **The observed-redshift boundary term $B_i$ (von Hausegger & Dalang, arXiv:2412.13162v2, read from the paper; Lyra's narrowing (c)).** A boost Doppler-shifts every observed redshift, $(1+z) = (1+\bar z)(1-\beta\cos\theta)$ (their Eq. 2), so a bin selected on observed redshift gains and loses sources across its edges along the velocity direction; the kinematic dipole of a bin is therefore (their Eq. 13, for a sharp top-hat $[z_1, z_2)$ on observed redshift) $\mathcal{D}_{\rm kin}(z_b) = [\,2 + \tilde x_b(1+\tilde\alpha_b) + (1+z) f_b(z)\big|_{z_1}^{z_2}\,]\,\beta$, with $f_b(z) = n(z)/\int_{z_1}^{z_2} n\,dz$ the bin's own normalised redshift distribution — the boundary term $B(z_b) \equiv (1+z) f_b(z)|_{z_1}^{z_2}$ (their Eq. 14) is *readable from the data* (their words) with no luminosity function. For a selection with redshift uncertainty (their Sec. III, Eqs. 15–17, 20, 21) the term becomes $B(z_b) = -\int_0^\infty dz\, f_b(z)\, \dfrac{d\log W_b(z)}{d\log(1+z)}$ with $W_b(z)$ the bin's selection function on true redshift, which reduces to Eq. 13 for a sharp cut (their Eqs. 22–23). **Frozen prescription:** $B_i$ is computed by the smooth form (Eq. 21) with $W_i(z) = \int dz'\, W'_i(z')\, P(z'|z)$ (Eq. 16), $W'_i$ the top-hat of bin $i$ on the catalogue's reported redshift and $P(z'|z)$ Gaussian with the catalogue's per-source uncertainty column **`redshift_quaia_err`** (Quaia, arXiv:2306.17749 p. 21, Table 2 — Grace's pin; Table 2 carries no second σ_z column); at the first opening the FITS header is recorded verbatim on the board, and any mismatch between the header and Table 2 is reported before step (v), never resolved silently, and $n(z)$ taken as the observed distribution within the mask (the deconvolution of Eq. 15 is neglected; the stated approximation, valid because the catalogue's $\sigma_z/(1+z) \approx 0.01$ is an order of magnitude below the bin widths of 2.5; H6 tests it). The sharp form (Eq. 13) is computed and reported beside it; a difference exceeding the propagated uncertainty on $B_i$ is a Landing-C trigger (Section 4.4). **Two terms the paper names and this freeze does NOT model, declared:** a flux-cut dependence of the redshift selection (their Sec. III C, deferred by the authors) — sized here by hatch check H2; and selection cuts on variables correlated with observed redshift, e.g. colour (their Sec. V and App. D; Quaia's classification uses colours) — sized by H5. **Magnitude, from the paper and not from any back-of-envelope:** for a constant $f$ the boundary term contributes $\mathcal{O}(20\%)$ of the Ellis–Baldwin amplitude; for realistic $n(z)$ it is *comparable to or larger than* the $2 + x(1+\alpha)$ term and changes sign across the peak of $n(z)$ (their Figs. 1–4). It is therefore not a correction to P1's per-bin factor; it IS part of it, and the model of 4.2 carries $f_i$ with $B_i$ inside. Elie's control S4 (a boosted synthetic sky binned on *observed* redshift) must land A with $B_i$ and is posted mis-landing without it.

## 4. The estimator, the model, the fit, and the Landing-C criterion — fixed now
4.1 **Estimator (Elie's E1, validated on E2 before use; v1.5.1 (e) — K1908 §4(e) on Elie's 10:20 question, ruled with the number):** the weighted unit-vector sum $\mathbf{D}^{\rm raw}_i = \dfrac{3}{W_i}\sum_{s\in i} w_s\,\hat{n}_s$ over the footprint (the mask of 2.2 ∧ the selection-function cut of 2.3), corrected for the footprint's own dipole by the mask-mode correction (the same sum over the catalogue's randoms under the same footprint and weights returns the correction vector $\mathbf D^{\rm corr}$, which is subtracted — on the (v) record: $|\mathbf D^{\rm corr}| = 0.00189$ for $G < 20.5$) **AND for the footprint's anisotropic RESPONSE: $\mathbf D_i = \mathsf R^{-1}(\mathbf D^{\rm raw}_i - \mathbf D^{\rm corr})$, with $\mathsf R = 3\langle\hat n\hat n^{\sf T}\rangle$ the randoms' weighted second moment under the same footprint and weights (eigenvalues 0.72, 0.73 in the plane and 1.55 at the pole for $|b| > 30°$, Elie 10:20). Reason, written now: the model of 4.2 is a statement about $\mathbf b$; the raw sum on a partial sky estimates $\mathsf R\,\mathbf b$, and the offset correction removes only the uniform sky's first moment; comparing $\mathsf R\,\mathbf b$ with $\mathbf b_{\rm CMB}$ compares different objects. Both moments come from the same randoms already on the (v) record — nothing new is read. The literal offset-only $\mathbf D_i$ (v1–v1.5's sentence) is posted beside as a diagnostic and is never the criterion. Under the fallback weights of 2.3 one $\mathsf R$ serves every bin; if per-bin weights run, $\mathsf R_i$ per bin.** Uncertainty: the covariance of $\mathbf{D}_i$ from 1,000 isotropic Poisson mocks drawn under the same footprint, weights and $N_i$, passed through the same correction ($\mathsf R^{-1}\Sigma\,\mathsf R^{-\sf T}$). ## 5. Tolerances and the three landings — fixed now, partitioning every outcome
**(v1.3, K1903 §2(b): the comparison is made on the fitted VECTOR, not on its norm.)** Let $\mathbf b = \beta\hat u$ be the joint fit's boost vector (4.5(b)) with covariance $\Sigma_b$ (4.3), and let $\mathbf b_{\rm CMB} = \beta_{\rm CMB}\hat u_{\rm CMB}$ with $\Sigma_{\rm CMB} = \sigma_{\rm CMB}^2\,\mathbf 1$ (Grace's pin). Define $\chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf c) = (\mathbf b - \mathbf c)^{\sf T}(\Sigma_b + \Sigma_{\rm CMB})^{-1}(\mathbf b - \mathbf c)$. Reason, written now: at S/N ≈ 2.5 the norm of a noisy 3-vector is biased upward ($E|\hat{\mathbf b}|^2 = |\mathbf b|^2 + \operatorname{tr}\Sigma_b$; Elie 5760: $\hat\beta/\beta_{\rm inj} = 1.20 \pm 0.09$ joint, 1.87 count-only), so a norm test leans toward a false Landing B — the one error the program has committed before (K1810); the ℓ = 1 moment is a vector in the SO(3) triplet and its norm is a coordinate. Prior art on estimator amplitude bias: Siewert, Schmidt-Rubart & Schwarz, A&A 653, A9 (2021), arXiv:2010.08366 (Grace pins). The debiased norm $\sqrt{|\mathbf b|^2 - \operatorname{tr}\Sigma_b}$ and the 95 %/99 % positional regions are POSTED as diagnostics, never used as the criterion. **(v1.5, K1907 §1–§2: two absolute 95 % regions cannot partition the outcomes when each hypothesis sits inside the other's region — at $\sigma_\beta = 166$ km s$^{-1}$, $\chi^2_3(\mathbf b_{\rm CMB}, \mathbf 0) = 4.96 < 7.815$, so v1.3–v1.4's zero clause was satisfied by the CMB vector itself and P(B | P1 true) = 0.56. The zero comparison is therefore made as a likelihood ratio between the two simple hypotheses, not as a second absolute region.)** Define $\Delta\chi^2 \equiv \chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf 0) - \chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf b_{\rm CMB})$ (twice the log-likelihood ratio of the CMB boost over no boost under the Gaussian of 4.3). The threshold $\Delta\chi^2 \ge 3.84$ is an ODDS threshold — $e^{3.84/2} \approx 6.8 : 1$ for the CMB over zero — frozen now; it is not a 95 % calibration of anything (under no boost the statistic is Gaussian with mean $-|\mathbf b_{\rm CMB}|^2/\sigma_\beta^2$, and the false-A rate it gives at 166 km s$^{-1}$ is 2.4 %, from the instrument).
- **Landing A — P1 HOLDS:** NOT Landing C (including C′ of 4.5), AND $\chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf b_{\rm CMB}) \le 7.815$ (95 %), AND **(v1.5) the CMB boost is preferred over no boost, $\Delta\chi^2 \ge 3.84$** — A is reported as **"decisive"** when additionally $\chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf 0) \ge 7.815$ (zero excluded on its own at 95 %) and otherwise as **"A, not decisive against zero alone"; both are Landing A and the label changes nothing downstream** — AND, after subtracting the fitted intrinsic term, no non-null bin's residual $\mathbf{D}_i - A w_i \hat{w} - f_i\mathbf b_{\rm CMB}$ has $\chi^2$ (3 d.o.f., bin covariance) with $p < 0.01$, AND Landing A′ of 4.5(a).
- **Landing B — P1 FIRES (A9's kill condition met; the Machian descent falsified):** NOT Landing C, AND $\chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf b_{\rm CMB}) \ge 14.156$ (the 3σ-equivalent of χ²₃), AND every hatch check of Section 6 PASSES (the discrepancy survives all of them). **(v1.5) The separate "consistent with zero" clause of v1.3–v1.4 is REMOVED.** "No boost is not one boost" still fires — but only when zero is *distinguishable* from the CMB, which is the same $\chi^2_3(\mathbf b, \mathbf b_{\rm CMB}) \ge 14.156$ with $\mathbf b \approx \mathbf 0$, i.e. $\sigma_\beta \lesssim 98$ km s$^{-1}$. Every outcome is exactly one of A, B, C. ## 6. The hatch, closed into named tests (carried from 08-21 §5 and made finite)
A Landing-B discrepancy is a fire only if ALL of the following pass. Each is a test with a written criterion; none is "an astrophysical account."
- **H1 Galactic mask:** repeat the fit with $|b| > 20°$ and with $|b| > 40°$; PASS iff the fitted $\beta$ and $\hat{u}$ move by less than $1\sigma_\beta$ and less than the 68% positional radius respectively.
- **H2 Magnitude/flux limit:** repeat with the selection limit brightened by 0.25 mag; PASS iff the same stability holds and the recomputed $f_i$ change by less than their propagated uncertainties.
- **H3 Ecliptic / Gaia scanning-law imprint:** (a) the dipole of the published selection-function map itself is computed and reported; (b) the fit is repeated with an additional frozen nuisance template — an ecliptic dipole and ecliptic quadrupole (5 amplitudes, fixed basis) marginalised over; PASS iff $\beta$ and $\hat{u}$ move by less than $1\sigma_\beta$ and the 68% radius; (c) the fit is repeated WITHOUT the selection-function weights of 2.3; PASS iff the discrepancy that triggered B is present in BOTH the weighted and the unweighted fit (a fire that appears in only one is a systematic).
- **H4 Malmquist / redshift-error mixing:** repeat with all bin edges shifted by $+0.1$ and by $-0.1$ in $z$; PASS iff the B-level discrepancy persists in both shifted binnings.
- **H5 Stellar contamination and dust:** repeat with the catalogue's published quasar-probability threshold raised by 0.1 and with an additional $E(B-V) < 0.05$ cut (the dust map Grace pins); PASS iff the discrepancy persists.
- **H6 Photometric-redshift outliers:** repeat with the catalogue's published redshift-uncertainty column cut at its median; PASS iff the discrepancy persists.
- **H7 Mock mis-specification (v1.5.1 (b), added before any dipole; it replaces the per-run σ_β trigger of 4.4):** the run's own mock $\sigma_\beta$ (4.3, joint) is compared with the per-run band of Elie's v1.5.1 synthetic Quaia (P1-true arm: mean $\bar\sigma^{\rm syn}_\beta$ and per-run scatter $s_{\rm syn}$, posted on the board BEFORE (vi)); PASS iff $|\sigma^{\rm run}_\beta - \bar\sigma^{\rm syn}_\beta| \le 2\,s_{\rm syn}$. A miss names its cause (weights, footprint, $N_i$, the mocks' isotropy against the sky's clustering variance) and is reported beside a Landing A as well; it gates B like every hatch.
A discrepancy that fails any of H1–H7 is reported as Landing C with the failing check named. The list is closed: no eighth check may be added after a real dipole is seen, and none of H1–H7 may be dropped.
