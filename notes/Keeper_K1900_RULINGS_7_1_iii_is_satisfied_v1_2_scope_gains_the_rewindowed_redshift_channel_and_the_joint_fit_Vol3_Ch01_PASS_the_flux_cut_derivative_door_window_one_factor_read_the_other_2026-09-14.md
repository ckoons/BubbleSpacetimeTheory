## 4. The reconnect, and why my envelope failed: window one factor, read the other
Lyra's Theorem B (H² ≅ Rac ⊗ clock) separates the two things a source carries — its place in the count (the Rac factor) and its reading (the clock factor, the observed redshift). A boost acts on both. **If you window a sample on the clock reading and then read the clock, the window moves with what it reads and the first-order moment cancels** — that is g → 0 in observed-z bins, and it is the same mechanism as the boundary term B_i on the count side (a window on z′ selects a boosted population). **If you window on the Rac factor (flux, counts) and read the clock, nothing cancels** — g ≈ 1 + m. So the rule is: *window on one factor, read the other.* Elie found it in the numbers; the algebra says why. My envelope windowed on z and read z, and used the photometric error as the noise when the noise is the bin's own width in the clock reading (0.7 in z for the full sample, not 0.14).

## 5. A door worth an hour: the flux-cut derivative (a kinematic-only observable within one catalogue)
The intrinsic (clustering) dipole of a population does not depend on the flux cut to first order — the Rac anisotropy does not know the magnitude limit; the kinematic dipole does, through x(1+α) (and B_i). So **the derivative of the count dipole with respect to the flux cut is kinematic-dominated: D(cut₂) − D(cut₁) ≈ [Δ(x(1+α)) + ΔB]·βû.** Quaia ships two cuts already (G < 20.5 and G < 20.0). Prior art, pinned: Mittal & Lewis, "The Ellis and Baldwin test of the Cosmic Dipole: exploring the impact of multiple flux density cuts," arXiv:2605.27520 (26 May 2026) — disjoint flux bins fitted simultaneously, a higher Bayes factor when the luminosity function is not a power law; they fit the common dipole across cuts rather than taking the difference, but the lever is the same. In operator terms: the kinematic ℓ = 1 moment is the *cut-derivative* of the count moment; the intrinsic moment is its cut-*invariant* part.
