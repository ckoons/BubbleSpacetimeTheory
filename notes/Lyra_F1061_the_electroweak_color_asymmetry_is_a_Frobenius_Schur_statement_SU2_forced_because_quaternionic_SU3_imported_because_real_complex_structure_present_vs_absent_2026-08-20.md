## The statement

The gauge group attached to an internal sector is the unitary group of the sector's commutant division algebra. From F1060:

| sector | division algebra (commutant) | contains a complex structure? | gauge group | forced / imported |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| charge (SO(2)) | **ℂ** | yes (i itself) | U(1) = U(ℂ) | **FORCED** |
| weak (Sp(2) spinor) | **ℍ** | yes (J, with J² = −1) | SU(2) = U(ℍ) | **FORCED** |
| color (Peirce V₁₂) | **ℝ** | **no** | SO(3) = U(M₃(ℝ))⁺; SU(3) needs M₃(ℝ)→M₃(ℂ) | **IMPORTED** (the complexification) |

**The single principle:** a **complex** or **quaternionic** representation *is already a complex vector space* (ℂ has i; a quaternionic structure J is by definition a complex structure, J² = −1). So its unitary group — U(1), SU(2) — is intrinsic: the geometry hands it over with no extra choice. A **real** representation is *not* a complex vector space; it has no distinguished i. The color gauge group SU(3) = U(3, ℂ)/… requires a complex structure on the color space, and the geometry's color space (V₁₂) is **real** (T2511). So SU(3) can only be reached by *imposing* a complex structure — the complexification M₃(ℝ) → M₃(ℂ), SO(3) → SU(3) — which is exactly the #108 import (K1720), and which F1060 pinned to this one algebraic step.
