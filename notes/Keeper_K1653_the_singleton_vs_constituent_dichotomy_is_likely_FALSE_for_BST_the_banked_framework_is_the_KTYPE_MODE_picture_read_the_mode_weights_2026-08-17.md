# K1653 — The singleton-vs-constituent dichotomy is likely false: BST builds particles as K-type modes, so read the mode weights

The K1652 gate asks "are BST's singletons particles or constituents?" — Cal's (a) vs (c). Reconnecting to the *banked mass/mixing derivations* suggests the honest answer is **neither**, and that reframes the whole question productively.

## BST's banked framework is the K-type mode picture
The corpus doesn't build the SM as singletons-as-particles *or* as Flato–Fronsdal composites. It builds particles as **discrete-series modes on H²(D_IV⁵) at Wallach addresses**:
- "SM particle masses at K-types at Wallach positions";
- "the whole fermion sector is the **SVD of ONE overlap (Gram) matrix** on H²(D_IV⁵) — masses = singular values, mixing = the angular part" (F603/K768);
- the electron is the K-type **V_(1/2,1/2)^{(0)}**;
- "a fermion is the substrate-Dirac field — the square root of the oscillator" (the spinor native to the type-IV spin-factor domain).

So the physical fermions are **modes at specific addresses**, not the Di singleton itself, and not Rac⊗Di composites (BST reads modes off the one Gram matrix; it doesn't tensor singletons to make particles). The Rac and Di are *two specific minreps*; the physical spectrum is spread across *many* K-types.

## Why that dissolves the dichotomy — and makes the question decidable
The #Rac law is really a statement about a **mode's conformal weight**: exp(2πiJ)=(−1)^{2E}, so a mode rides **4π ⟺ E is half-odd**, **2π ⟺ E is integer**. So "which particles ride the double cover" is **not** the FF singleton-vs-composite question at all — it's concrete and **readable**: take each physical SM mode, read its conformal weight E off its banked Wallach address, and half-odd ↔ 4π. **Decidable from banked data**, no framework choice required. That's the productive form of the K1652 gate: not "particles or constituents" (a false either/or), but *read the weights.*

## The honest caveats (still the team's audit)
- **Loose spots to reconcile:** F338 says "Higgs = Rac, the SO(5)-**singlet**"; F603 says the Higgs condensate is the SO(5)-**vector** — singlet vs vector is a real reconcile. And "Rac = muon" (Elie_Lepton_Sector) is *likely a loose address-label* ("the muon sits at weight 3/2," not "the muon *is* the Rac singleton") — verify, don't take verbatim.
- **A banked disambiguation applies (K1201/K1212):** the *scalar* Bergman genus n_C=5 ≠ the *fermion* mode weight g=7 — two different objects both called "genus." The mode weights must use the fermion-weight structure, not the scalar genus. (A prior CI conflated them; the pin exists to stop it.)
