# WALL THEORY

## 1. THE OBJECT, NAMED

For two completions f, f′ of one pinning, define the **difference field**
δ(v) := id if f(v) = f′(v), else the transposition (f(v) f′(v)) — canonical, pointwise,
well-defined. **Domains** are maximal connected regions of constant δ; **walls** are the edge
sets between distinct domains; the **fixed locus** is δ⁻¹(id). The **wall system**
𝒲(f, f′) := (domain partition, transposition assignment, fixed locus) is the fork's third door
made formal: for the twins, two domains (transpositions (2 3) and (0 3)), walls through the
fixed center, 6 of 7 interior vertices moved.

𝒲 is RELATIONAL — a functional of the pair, not of either coloring — which is precisely why
every static separator died: the separating object never lived on one twin. (Cal's twin-datum
clause, now discharged by an object of the right type.)

## 2. THE DOMAIN ALGEBRA (local compatibility, derived)

Across an edge (u, v) with u in a δ₁-domain and v in a δ₂-domain, BOTH colorings must be proper:
f(u) ≠ f(v) and δ₁f(u) ≠ δ₂f(v). This forbids exactly the pairs where δ₂δ₁⁻¹ maps f(u)'s orbit
onto f(v) — a finite local rule set. Two consequences worth stating tonight:
- **Interior termination is impossible:** a wall cannot end at an interior vertex — around any
  interior vertex the δ-values must chain consistently (the domain partition restricted to a
  link cycle has an even interface count; a dangling wall end would give an odd count).
  Therefore **every wall component is a closed curve or a path with BOTH endpoints on the
  pinned boundary.**
- **In height coordinates the wall is a CREASE:** within a domain, f′ = τf lifts to h′ = ρ(h)
  for a fixed isometry ρ of the height lattice (a transposition of labels acts as a lattice
  reflection); the wall is where two reflections meet — a crease between mirror-related tilings.
  "Ground-state degeneracy" is now literal: two tilings of one boundary condition, exchanged by
  a crease system.

## 3. THE ANCHORING PREDICTION (why the wall is pinned — the mechanism, pre-registered)

**Anchoring:** wall paths end only on the PINNED boundary (Section 2), and a wall whose
endpoints are pinned cannot be dissolved by legal moves — dissolving a wall means realizing one
domain's transposition by chain swaps, and the chains that would do it necessarily cross the
pinning (the board's sentence, now with the mechanism: the domain's boundary-adjacent chains
contain pinned vertices by anchoring). Contrapositive, and the invariant-shaped statement:

**Wall Dissolution Criterion (candidate):** two completions of a pinning are relatively
Kempe-equivalent iff their wall system can be dissolved domain-by-domain by chains avoiding the
boundary — equivalently, iff no wall component is anchored at both ends. This is U2's cocycle
made concrete: **the wall IS the support of the availability cocycle**, and the height-sector
invariant (G5) should register exactly the anchored-wall content — pre-registered: G5 sees the
twins' wall where mod-2 was blind.

## 4. THE FACTORY CLAUSE, ANSWERED IN ADVANCE (what a second frozen disc must exhibit)

For the wall to graduate from observation to theory, the next frozen pinning examined (from the
15-family) must show, without retuning:
1. A difference field decomposing into ≥ 2 constant-transposition domains (not a global phase,
   not a scatter).
2. Every wall path anchored: both endpoints on the pinned boundary; no interior ends (the
   Section-2 parity argument makes interior ends a REFUTATION, not a variant).
3. A nonempty fixed locus compatible with the domain algebra (the two domain transpositions
   must share a fixed color; the fixed locus is colored by it).
4. Wall minimality: the crease system is geodesic-shaped (ground-state pairs differ by minimal
   walls; a fat or meandering wall system breaks the degeneracy reading).

satisfied on its own terms. (Prediction 2 is the sharp one — it is the mechanism, not the
phenomenology.)
