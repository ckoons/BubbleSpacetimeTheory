## Why one invariant covers so much

Frobenius–Schur reality-type is the answer to a single question about a representation: *is it real, complex, or quaternionic?* — equivalently, *what commutes with it* (ℝ, ℂ, or ℍ). Nearly every **discrete internal choice** in the Standard Model is exactly such a question:
- "Is there a gauge group here?" = does the block carry a complex structure to gauge (ℂ, ℍ yes; ℝ no).
- "Is this fermion chiral?" = is its rep complex (so its mirror is a different object)?
- "Can this fermion be Majorana?" = is its rep real under the unbroken group (so it is its own conjugate)?
- "Is CP violated?" = is the spinor complex/pseudoreal (so a √−1 cannot be rotated away)?
- "Is there custodial symmetry?" = how does the real vector decompose (does a (2,2) appear)?

Each is a reality-type. So it is not a coincidence that one invariant reads them all — the SM's discrete internal structure *is* a list of reality-type questions, and D_IV⁵ answers each by decomposition.
