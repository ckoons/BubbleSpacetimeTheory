# L9 — The Lax–Phillips face of RH on Γ\D_IV⁵: which semigroup, on which space, would have to decay — and why nobody has it. One page, no claim.
**Lyra, Sunday 2026-09-06, 15:52 EDT. Round 122 L9. Citations from memory; pin before any external use.**

## 1. The rank-one statement, as proved (Lax–Phillips 1976, Ch. 9–10; Pavlov–Faddeev 1972; Lax–Phillips, "Scattering theory for automorphic functions", Ann. Math. Studies 87)
On X = Γ\H with Γ = SL₂(ℤ), take the automorphic wave equation u_tt = (y²(∂_x²+∂_y²) + ¼)u and the energy form E(u) = ∫(|∇u|² − ¼|u|²/y²) + |u_t|². Define the incoming/outgoing subspaces D_± as data supported in the cusp and moving in/out along the cusp's half-line (the translation representation in the variable log y); K = H ⊖ (D_+ ⊕ D_−) is finite-energy data that is neither. The semigroup **Z(t) = P_K U(t) P_K** is a contraction semigroup on K; its generator B has discrete spectrum. Theorem: the spectrum of B on the orthocomplement of the cusp forms is {(ρ−1)/2 : ζ(ρ) = 0} — the scattering poles of φ(s) = ξ(2s−1)/ξ(2s) — and **RH ⟺ that part of the spectrum lies on the line Re = −¼ ⟺ Z(t), restricted to K ⊖ {cusp forms}, decays at the sharp rate ‖Z(t)f‖ ≤ Ce^{−t/4}‖f‖^{(*)}**, the decay exponent being the distance of the pole set from the axis. (The (*) is Lax–Phillips' Theorem 9.x as I remember it; the honest form is "RH ⟺ the resonance set of B sits on Re = −¼", with the norm-decay equivalent modulo the standard log-factor caveats for non-normal generators.) Everything below asks what the same sentence would be in rank 2.

## 2. The objects on Γ\D_IV⁵, in L1's coordinates
- **The scattering operator is known:** T2621. Its spherical scalar is c(w₀,λ) on the two-dimensional axis i𝔞*, and its pole set is (i)–(iv) of T2621: ζ's zeros at four shifts plus the 2-comb.
- **RH in these coordinates:** all poles of type (i) on Re(λ₁∓λ₂) = −½, of type (ii) on Re λ_i = −¼, of type (iii) on Re λ_i = −1. Three lines, one for each way ζ enters — a rank-2 "resonances on a line" statement, exactly the shape Lax–Phillips uses, three times over.
- **The wave equation:** D_IV⁵ has no canonical time. Lax–Phillips uses ∂_t on the product ℝ × H because H is rank one and the cusp is a half-line. On a rank-2 space the flat is 𝔞 ≅ ℝ², the cusp neighbourhood of P₀ is a CONE (a Siegel set over the positive Weyl chamber 𝔞⁺), and the natural "wave equation" is the system of invariant differential operators (the two generators of the centre of U(𝔤), degrees 2 and 4 for B₂) — two commuting evolutions, not one.
- **The semigroup that would have to decay:** Z(H) = P_K U(H) P_K for H ∈ 𝔞⁺, a TWO-PARAMETER contraction semigroup indexed by the Weyl chamber, on K = H ⊖ (⊕_{w∈W} D_w), where the D_w are the eight translation-representation subspaces of data moving along the chamber w𝔞⁺ in the cusp (one per Weyl chamber; "incoming/outgoing" becomes "which chamber"). Its generator would be a pair (B₁, B₂) of commuting operators with joint spectrum = the pole set of c(w₀,λ), and **RH ⟺ the joint spectrum on the orthocomplement of the discrete spectrum lies on the three hyperplane-lines above ⟺ Z(H) decays at the sharp exponential rate along every direction of 𝔞⁺, with rate given by the distance from i𝔞* to the pole set, which is ¼, ½ or 1 by root type.** That is the sentence. Nobody has proved that such a Z(H) exists with those properties.

## 3. The known obstruction, named
1. **No rank-2 Lax–Phillips theory.** The translation representation needs the cusp to be a half-line: data in D_+ is a function of one variable (log y) moving one way. In the Siegel set over 𝔞⁺ the constant term is a sum over eight Weyl-group terms, the "direction of motion" is a point of a two-dimensional cone, and the orthogonality D_+ ⊥ D_− that makes K well-defined has no proven analogue for eight chambers. (Lax–Phillips' own remark: their method is "rank one"; the higher-rank spectral theory is Langlands', which is not a scattering theory in their sense.)
2. **The continuous spectrum has two parameters and three layers.** Besides the P₀ Eisenstein series (two-parameter), the maximal parabolics P₁, P₂ contribute one-parameter families (residues of the P₀ series along ζ's pole and along the kernel's L-function — L1's Λ(λ)'s pole at λ = 3/2), and the residual spectrum sits at the ρ-shifts. A decay statement must separate these; in rank one there is only one layer.
3. **Positivity of the energy form.** Lax–Phillips' E is positive on K because of the spectral gap ¼ of Γ\H (Selberg's λ₁ ≥ 3/16 is enough for their argument; the exact ¼ appears in the wave operator). On D_IV⁵ the bottom of the continuous spectrum is |ρ_𝔞|² = 25/4 + 9/4 = 17/2 and the Laplacian is one of two generators; which quadratic form plays the role of E, and whether it is positive on the would-be K, is not known.
4. **The barrier (T2620) still applies.** Even with Z(H) constructed, "its spectrum lies on the lines" is the statement to be proved, and the Lax–Phillips construction is ζ → ζ_{ℤ⁵}-invariant in form: build the same semigroup for a scattering matrix with ζ_{ℤ⁵}'s ratios and it exists just as well, with poles off the line. What Lax–Phillips gives is a REFORMULATION (a spectral-geometric reading of the zero set), not a mechanism; the mechanism would have to enter through the one place the construction is not barrier-invariant, the 2-adic surgery — and that is a statement about ONE prime.

## 4. What is worth doing, and what is not
Worth doing (a real, finite piece of new mathematics, credit-bearing whatever RH does): construct D_w and K for the P₀ cusp of a rank-2 arithmetic quotient and prove Z(H) is a two-parameter contraction semigroup whose joint spectrum is the pole set of c(w₀,λ). That would be the first higher-rank Lax–Phillips theory, and D_IV⁵ is as good a place as any. Not worth doing: reading the resulting equivalence as progress on RH. It is RH in different clothes; the clothes are new, the body is the same.
