## L1 — The Three-Sector Theorem (row candidate)

XIII in my memory [pin].) **G-invariance.** G = SO₀(5,2) acts on T_Ω by biholomorphisms (linear-fractional maps of the tube preserving it, hence preserving the cone Ω up to the conformal factor) and on the boundary by conformal maps; it therefore maps H²(T_Ω) to itself and H²(T_{−Ω}) to itself (each tube is preserved as a set), and so preserves their orthocomplement L²_sp: **the three sectors are G-invariant, and the reason is that G preserves the cone.** In the Lie-ball model (Š = (S¹ × S⁴)/ℤ₂, Hua modes e^{imθ}Y_k): H²₊ is the causal sector k ≤ m (K1860 Results 1), H²₋ is k ≤ −m, and L²_sp is |m| < k — the same three sectors in the coordinates where the count is finite. **What it forbids ("no pockets"):** a holomorphic pocket "inside the continuum" would be a third domain with the same Shilov boundary; there is none, because a boundary datum's holomorphic extension is governed by the convex hull of its Fourier support (the tube theorem), and the only proper open convex cones whose duals carry L² data disjointly are ±Ω. ## L2 — The No-Boundary-Nucleation Lemma, and the bulk-point survivor
**Lemma.** Let z ∈ Š be a Shilov-boundary point and P = Stab_G(z). Then the P-fixed vectors of H² = H²(D_IV⁵) are {0}.
*Proof (two lines).* P is a maximal parabolic subgroup — in the tube model, the stabiliser of a boundary point is (dilations × SO(1,4)) ⋉ (special conformal transformations ℝ⁵), a noncompact closed subgroup containing a one-parameter group a_t → ∞. H² is an irreducible unitary representation of the simple group G with finite centre and has no nonzero G-invariant vector. By Howe–Moore, every matrix coefficient ⟨π(g)v, v⟩ tends to 0 as g → ∞ in G. If v were P-fixed, ⟨π(a_t)v, v⟩ = ‖v‖² for all t, which does not tend to 0 unless v = 0. ∎ (Howe–Moore: matrix coefficients of irreducible unitary representations without invariant vectors vanish at infinity — Howe & Moore, J. Funct. Anal. 32 (1979) [pin]; Zimmer, *Ergodic Theory and Semisimple Groups*, Thm 2.2.20 [pin].) **Consequence:** "keep what a boundary point's isotropy cannot distinguish" keeps the zero vector — not the vacuum, nothing. A boundary point cannot host the nucleation; it is the round's item 2, now proved.
**The bulk-point survivor, at the origin.** The isotropy of the origin 0 ∈ D_IV⁵ is K = SO(5) × SO(2). Its SO(5)-part's invariants in H²: the holomorphic SO(5)-invariant polynomials on ℂ⁵ are generated by the single invariant z·z (first fundamental theorem for the orthogonal group on one vector: the only invariant of a single vector is its quadratic form; determinants need five vectors), so H²^{SO(5)} = closure of ℂ[z·z] = {F(z·z) : F holomorphic}. The map w = z·z sends the Lie ball onto the unit disc (|z·z| < 1 inside, |z·z| = 1 on Š where z·z = e^{2iθ}), so **the survivor is H²(𝔻) in the variable w = e^{2iθ}: the Hardy space of the time circle, with modes w^j = e^{2ijθ}, j = 0, 1, 2, … — the windings, and only the windings.** SO(2) acts on it by time translation (w ↦ e^{2iφ}w). This corrects "K-invariants": the full K includes SO(2), whose invariants in ℂ[z·z] are the constants (j = 0) — the vacuum alone. The survivor's angular content is zero by construction (k = 0): the reset forgets every direction (K1860 §G's "angular degree restarting at zero"), and its information is the winding spectrum {a_j}.
**Transport to a bulk point.** For z₀ = g·0 (every bulk point is one, G transitive on D_IV⁵), Stab_G(z₀) = gKg⁻¹, its "angular" part g SO(5) g⁻¹, and its invariants in H² are π(g)(ℂ[z·z]) = {F((g⁻¹z)·(g⁻¹z))}: the same Hardy space of a circle, transported — the survivor's TYPE is independent of the nucleation point in the bulk (one circle's Hardy space, infinite-dimensional, windings j ≥ 0), and only its EMBEDDING moves. **What degenerates as z₀ → Š:** g → ∞ in G; the transported vacuum π(g)1 = the normalised Szegő kernel at z₀, whose boundary mass concentrates toward one direction (Casey's "one dimension closes down"); the transported windings π(g)(z·z)^j concentrate the same way; and in the limit the isotropy passes from the compact gKg⁻¹ to the noncompact P, where by the Lemma the invariants are {0}. So the survivor is not a continuous function of the nucleation point at the boundary: infinite-dimensional at every bulk point, zero at the boundary. **The physical clause this supports (still the posit):** the nucleation point is a bulk point pressed toward one direction; how far it is pressed is a parameter of the reset (the concentration of the Szegő kernel), and it is not fixed by anything above.

## L3 — The sign statement, and which positive-time clause is now a theorem
**Statement.** (a) The survivor lies in H² = H²₊, hence its frequencies are m = 2j ≥ 0: windings are nonnegative by Hardy membership.
