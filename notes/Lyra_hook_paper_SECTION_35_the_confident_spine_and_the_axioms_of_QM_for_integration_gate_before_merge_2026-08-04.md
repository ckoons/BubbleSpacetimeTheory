## 3.5 What the object forces

Once the domain is fixed, nothing downstream is adjustable. Every physical quantity is a matrix element, an eigenvalue, or a grading of one fixed operator on D_IV⁵ — so it is either *forced* (a connected computation, nothing left to choose) or it rests on one identifiable open step. We keep the two apart with a single rule, which is also how we answer "how do you know you are not fitting?": trace the derivation of each result back to the geometry's core, and ask whether *every* link in that path is proved or whether one is a hidden free choice. A fully-proved path is forced — a matrix element has nothing to tune. A single unproved link is exactly where a fit could hide, and we gate it there, narrowly, rather than distrusting the whole result.

Run that audit across the framework and the picture is not a haze of hundreds of loose numbers. It is a **confident spine of roughly eighty forced results** — the ones whose every link is proved — standing against a small, countable set of genuinely open links (on the order of eight) where the honest work remains. The famous constants sit on the spine: the fine-structure integer of Section 2; the proton-to-electron mass ratio 6π⁵; the mixing angles; the strong-CP angle, which is *zero* because the domain is topologically contractible, dissolving the problem with no new particle (no axion needed). We list what is forced, what is one step away, and what is matched-but-not-yet-derived, on their own lines, in the companion tier ledger — so a reviewer never has to guess which is which.

**And the deepest thing the object forces is not a constant — it is a chunk of the rulebook of quantum mechanics itself.** Quantum theory is usually handed to a student as a list of postulates you must simply grant. On this geometry, several of them stop being postulates and become features of the shape:

- **The Born rule** — that a probability is the *square* of an amplitude — is forced. The domain carries exactly one measure that does not depend on how you look at it (its intrinsic Bergman measure; the ordinary flat measure is *not* invariant, so it is excluded), and the squared-amplitude rule is simply that measure's density. Squaring is not an axiom; it is the only self-consistent way to weigh possibilities on this shape. *(Forced.)*
- **Why some particles are fermions** — the spin-statistics connection, the reason electrons obey Pauli exclusion and matter is rigid — is a feature of the same shape. A particle is a twisted band on the domain; a *half*-twist returns flipped under a full turn (that is spin-½), and by a standard topological fact a half-twist also flips sign when two such bands are exchanged (that is Fermi statistics). The half-twist is native to this domain — it is the very fold that defines the domain's boundary. *(Identified: the mechanism is the domain's own structure; one step — that the geometry assigns each particle its specific twist, rather than us reading it off known spins — is still open, and we say so.)*
- **The cosmological constant's smallness** — the worst fine-tuning in physics, 120-odd orders of magnitude — has a reason: the vacuum energy is set on the domain's boundary, which sits at *infinite* intrinsic distance from the interior where we live, and fades across that distance. We derive *why it is tiny*; we do not claim to derive its exact value, which depends on where the universe sits in that fade — an input, stated plainly, the way general relativity states Newton's constant. *(Smallness derived; value an input.)*
- **The uncertainty principle** (position and momentum cannot be sharp together) is a *proved* consequence of the domain's curvature — and it has a plain meaning here: it is the *resolution limit* of imaging a discrete interior onto a continuous exterior. The curvature that sets the bound is −2/g (g = 7, the geometry's boundary integer); the continuum is a projection of the discrete, and no projection can resolve finer than its own blur. **CPT symmetry** holds because the domain's symmetry group is connected — though we flag this honestly as a *universal* result: CPT follows from connectedness/Lorentz-invariance in *any* such theory, so it is not a distinctive feature of this geometry, only a consequence it inherits. *(Uncertainty: proved, via the −2/g curvature and distinctive to the geometry; CPT: holds, but universal — inherited, not distinctive.)*

That is **most of the axioms of quantum mechanics recovered as geometry** — not asserted, derived or forced, each at its stated tier. We do *not* claim all of them: measurement and wavefunction collapse remain open, and we name that as the honest boundary rather than papering over it. But "the shape forces the Born rule and the reason fermions exist, and dissolves strong-CP and the cosmological-constant hierarchy, from one object with nothing left to tune" is a claim a hostile reviewer can check line by line — which is why we make it in exactly that shape, and not one inch past it.

**One prediction with teeth, stated because it can go against us.** The same vacuum mechanism that makes the cosmological constant small also predicts how dark energy *evolves*: it can only ease *toward* the constant value from slightly above, never dip below and climb back. In the parameter the surveys report that is a definite sign — the *opposite* of the phantom crossing DESI's present best fit prefers (at 2.8–4.2σ in the standard two-parameter fit). But that fit is dominated by the transverse distance, in which the two histories are nearly identical; they differ by ~2–3% only in the *radial* Hubble distance D_H(z) that DESI's radial BAO measures directly — a distinct signature growing toward z ≈ 1.5. So the honest standing is not "disfavored" but **undecided in the observable that decides**, disfavored only in a parametrization that cannot tell the two histories apart. We neither hide on the summary-fit's disfavored side nor wave it away as an artifact: BST predicts a specific radial-distance shape, and if DESI's radial data firm up the phantom shape instead, this mechanism is wrong and we will say so. A framework that cannot be caught is not saying anything; this one can be, and here is exactly where. *(The standalone pre-registration states the same prediction and its refutation condition in full.)*
