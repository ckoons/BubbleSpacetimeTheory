# 1. THE KEMPE-PAIRING THEOREM (derived) — what happens when the far chain MISSES the middle road

Let c₀ be stuck, canonical, with **H₁: ζ ∩ F_i = ∅** (bit (α,ζ) = 0). Write K_i := ζ·η = (B₁,(r,s_i))·(B₂,(r,s_j))
— Kempe's own pairing, each copy with the pair of the singleton it does NOT touch; Gethner's Gadget 5₂ and
Kittell's α, β. Frame: T−v with T's embedding; the wall F_i ∪ v (the (s_M,s_i)-road from n_sM to n_si closed
through v) separates B₂ (position 2) from {B₁, n_sj} (positions 0, 4).

- **Stage 1.** ζ ∌ B₂, n_si (forced (r,s_i) partition); n_sM, n_sj outside the world. Under H₁, ζ uses no
  vertex of F_i, so ζ cannot cross F_i ∪ v and **ζ lies entirely on B₁'s side.** Swap: B₁ → s_i.
  c₁ = (s_i, s_M, r, s_i, s_j). F_i is untouched (no vertex of it recoloured).
- **Stage 2.** η₁ := B₂'s (r,s_j)-chain in c₁. The (r,s_j)-world is colour-disjoint from F_i ∪ v (s_M, s_i;
  and v), so η₁ lies on B₂'s side, where c₁ = c₀; hence **η₁ = η**, the c₀ chain, which by the forced (r,s_j)
  partition contains neither B₁ nor n_sj. Swap: B₂ → s_j. **c₂ = (s_i, s_M, s_j, s_i, s_j): r ABSENT. Kempe's
  two swaps insert v.** [This is Kempe's 1879 argument, correct exactly when H₁ holds; Heawood's counter-
  example is H₁ failing — ζ touching F_i lets the new r-material of ζ reconnect B₂ to n_sj.]
- **Stage 3.** ζ at B₁ (s_i): legal. B₁'s (r,s_i)-chain in c₂ cannot cross F_i ∪ v; on B₁'s side only ζ was
  recoloured, in place, so the chain is ζ as a set. Swap: ζ returns to its c₀ colours. c₃ = (r, s_M, s_j, s_i, s_j).
- **Stage 4.** η at B₂ (s_j): legal; by the same argument the chain is η as a set; swap returns it.
  **c₄ = c₀.** ∎

**Theorem KP.** Under H₁ the family word ζ·η is fully legal and equals the identity, while its two-stage
prefix inserts v. Mirror: under H₁′: η ∩ F_j = ∅, the word η·ζ is the identity and its prefix inserts.

# 4. The Δ-flip — which first words can break far-chain reach (the rule, from the letter table)

The bit (α,ζ) is "B₁'s (r,s_i)-chain meets the (s_M,s_i)-road from n_sM to n_si." A first word O changes it
only by recolouring inside N[ζ ∪ α] in a way that changes the s_i-material they share or the r/s_M material
that connects each. From the letter table: **(r,s_i)-letters (δ, ζ) recolour ζ's and α's s_i-vertices —
they can cut the meeting; (s_M,s_i)-letters (α) recolour α's own vertices — same; θ-letters recolour r-
vertices of ζ to s_M (ζ shares B₁ with θ = M) — they shorten ζ; (r,s_j)-letters (γ, η) and ε touch neither
world unless through the seeds' link edges.** So the orbits that can flip (α,ζ) are those with a δ-, ζ-, α-
or θ-letter; those with only γ/η/ε/β letters cannot. Mirror for (β,η). For (ε,ζ): letters δ, ζ, ε, α (all carry
s_i) and θ. Per-orbit rates are Elie's to measure; the rule predicts ZERO flips of (α,ζ) for orbits built
from {γ, η, ε, β} alone — a derived-empty cell, positive control on the rate table.
