# K1662 — The Kostant constant 25/4 = (n_C/2)² is forced; the 5 is the domain's n_C

Elie flagged 25/4 = (n_C/2)² as candidate-not-bank because he hadn't shown the 5 is the domain's n_C. It is — and the derivation is clean linear algebra on D_IV⁵.

## The ρ-truncation identity
The Kostant constant is ‖ρ_G‖² − ‖ρ_K‖², both Weyl vectors of orthogonal algebras:
- **G = SO(5,2) → so(7,ℂ) = B₃:** ρ_G = (5/2, 3/2, 1/2), ‖ρ_G‖² = (1²+3²+5²)/4 = **35/4**.
- **K semisimple part = SO(5) = B₂:** ρ_K = ρ(B₂)⊕0 = (3/2, 1/2, 0) (SO(2) abelian → ρ=0), ‖ρ_K‖² = (1²+3²)/4 = **10/4**.
- Difference = **25/4**.

Because ‖ρ(B_m)‖² = Σ_{k=1}^{m} ((2k−1)/2)², the difference ‖ρ(B₃)‖² − ‖ρ(B₂)‖² is **just the top term of the B₃ sum** — ((2·3−1)/2)² = (5/2)². The constant *is* the single ρ-entry B₃ carries that B₂ doesn't.

## The 5 is n_C — an identity for odd n_C
Is 2·rank_G − 1 = n_C? For **odd n_C**, the domain's signature (n_C, 2) gives so(n_C+2) = so(odd) = B_{(n_C+1)/2}, so rank_G = (n_C+1)/2, and

$$2\,\mathrm{rank}_G - 1 = (n_C+1) - 1 = n_C, \qquad \text{top } \rho\text{-entry} = \frac{2\,\mathrm{rank}_G-1}{2} = \frac{n_C}{2}.$$

So **the Kostant constant = (n_C/2)² is forced by n_C being odd**, and the 5 traces to the domain's n_C through the signature (5,2) → so(7) → rank 3 → top-ρ 5/2. Elie's candidate **promotes to a forced result.**

## Why it advances the gravity thread
This isn't just a pretty identity — it's the constant Elie needs. Kostant's formula gives D² = Ω_G − Ω_K + (‖ρ_G‖² − ‖ρ_K‖²), so **25/4 is the curvature-shift term of the bulk Kostant-D²** — the bulk analog of the Lichnerowicz "+3" in Elie's S⁴ computation (S⁴: D² = Δ + R/4 = Δ + 3). So the bulk Kostant D² = (Casimir difference) + 25/4, and that 25/4 = (n_C/2)² feeds straight into the bulk (t^{−5}) heat trace Elie is running. Hand him the constant.

This is *not* the same error as the double-cover "forced by n_C odd" (which claimed a *structure* that exists in every dimension).
