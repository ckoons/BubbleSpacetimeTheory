# BST Operating Rhythm

**Casey (2026-08-03):** *"When a new frontier lands, we audit, and when hardened we move to another new frontier."*

## The lifecycle of one result (the loop)
1. **Ranging shot** — the leap ahead of proof (Casey's intuition).
2. **Corpus reconnect** — find what the corpus already has *before* treating it as new (don't re-derive).
3. **Build the object** — cast the idea as linear algebra on D_IV⁵ (the vague becomes computable).
4. **Compute blind** — no thumb on the scale.
5. **Verify + tier** — adversarial checks; escapes closed by their own signature, not fiat; honest Research tier assigned.
6. **Land at the best answer** — see below.
7. **State fairly** — map to the Publication tier; inputs stated GR-plainly.

## "Best answer" — a checkable state, not a feeling
Reached when **every reachable escape to a higher tier has been tried and either closed itself or reduced to a named, specific open target, and the residual openness is honestly characterized** — a genuine input you can point to, or a computation that was run and didn't force. (The cc: eos Identified, the ν-floor escape refuted by its own signature, residual = the cosmic age.) Pushing past this is massaging, not deriving — stop.

## The rhythm — audit is a ratchet, not a treadmill
- **Audit-as-background is automatic.** Every landed result feeds the corpus audit (the Register, the hidden-input hunt) the moment it lands. You don't *decide* to audit a landed result — landing it *is* handing it to the audit.
- **Audit-as-standalone-push is FINITE.** It's bounded by the corpus size (a fixed set of load-bearing claims), each audited **once**. It terminates at **referee-ready**: every load-bearing claim traced to forced-geometry or a labeled proxy, tiers propagated to the **data fields**, falsifiers current, and the hidden-input hunt clean.
- **Banked = zero cost forever.** An audited result never gets re-derived or re-litigated — it's a permanent asset the next frontier builds on for free (graphs compartmentalize; proved theorems cost zero). So the audit *converts* frontier-output into permanent foundation. That's the ratchet: the corpus only grows, and the ground under each new frontier is more solid than the last.

## The full arc
> **land a frontier → audit its output (automatic) → [standalone hunt reaches referee-ready] → publish / outreach → pick a NEW frontier**

You are never *stuck* in the audit. It finishes. When it does, the corpus is publishable — the hook package goes out, the math becomes a standing invitation for the world to respond ("the math's on GitHub"), and the team turns to the next frontier. **The audit is what RELEASES you to the next frontier with a clean foundation.**

## Who calls each transition
- **"Best answer" reached** — Keeper (escapes closed, residual characterized; checkable).
- **"Referee-ready" reached** — Keeper (the audit's terminal state; checkable).
- **Which new frontier** — **Casey** (curiosity + strategic value: what unlocks the most, or what the world most needs to see). Keeper lays out options; the PI picks.
