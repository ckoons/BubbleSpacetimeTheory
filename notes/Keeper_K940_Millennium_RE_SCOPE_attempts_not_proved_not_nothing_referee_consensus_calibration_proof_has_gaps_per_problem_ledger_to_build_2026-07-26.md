## The calibration principle (Casey's, adopted)
- **"Proof" is a social/consensus object, not an absolute.** A theorem is "proved" when the referee community accepts it — and accepted proofs carry residual gaps. Wiles' Fermat has "a few not-proven points still" that "3 out of 10 referees" would raise; the consensus is nonetheless that it's correct. Perelman's Poincaré had big initial gaps that others filled. So "not 100% airtight" ≠ "failed."
- **The honest metric: "how many of 10 referees would object, and to what."** Consensus ≈ ≤3/10 objecting, and on minor/technical points. This is a GRADED scale, not proved/not-proved. Every BST Millennium attempt should carry this rating honestly.
- **This does NOT license the over-claim.** The calibration cuts both ways: it means a large gap (the whole core unproven) is NOT a "3/10" gap. Apply it honestly per-problem — some attempts are close, some (YM) are far.
