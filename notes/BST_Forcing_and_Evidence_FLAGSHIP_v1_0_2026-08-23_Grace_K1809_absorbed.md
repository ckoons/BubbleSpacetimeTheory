### III.1 — The problem, stated against ourselves
BST's algebra is closed over five small integers (Part 0.5). **A closed algebra over {3, 5, 6, 7, 137} and rank 2 generates many simple expressions inside any given error bar.** So the observation *"quantity X equals a simple BST integer ratio, to 0.4%"* is, on its own, **worth nothing** until one further question is answered:

> **How many other equally simple BST forms also sit inside that error bar?**

If the answer is "several," the match has **no discriminating power**: the theory did not predict the number, it merely failed to exclude it — along with its neighbours. **The number of competitors is part of the result, and a result reported without it is incomplete.**

### III.2 — The test, and the honest reporting form
For any BST-integer form banked against a measured band:
1. Generate the competitor pool **at the published form's own complexity class** (not below it).
2. Count the members that land inside the band. Call it **N**.
3. **Score N against the null** — how many forms of that class does chance put in a band that width? Then report **"smallest-of-N indistinguishable BST forms, N reported"** — never a bare match. **This form is available only where the family is enumerable and theory-generated; where the count is chance-saturated the only honest disposition is retirement (III.4a).**
4. If a competitor of equal simplicity fits **better** than the published form, that fact is reported in the same sentence.

**And the instrument itself must be positive-controlled.** A pool that generates only simple forms (`a`, `√a`, `a/b`, `√(a/b)`, `a·b`, `1/(2√a)`) **cannot clear a compound form**, and its silence on one is a **limitation of the instrument, not a clearance**. A search that cannot succeed proves nothing.

### III.4a — ★ Why "smallest-of-N, with N reported" is NOT available here
The honest reporting form of III.2 — *"smallest of N indistinguishable BST forms"* — **presupposes two things: that the family is enumerable, and that choosing within it is a meaningful act.** Both hold when a theory narrows a set of candidates and picks one. **Neither holds when N is simply what chance supplies.**
