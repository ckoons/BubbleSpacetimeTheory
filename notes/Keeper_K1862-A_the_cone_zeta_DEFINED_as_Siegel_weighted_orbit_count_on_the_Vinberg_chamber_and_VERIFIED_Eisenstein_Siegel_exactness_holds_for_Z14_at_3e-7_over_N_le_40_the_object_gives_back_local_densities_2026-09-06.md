## 1. The object, defined (Casey's "natural cutoff")
The naive point count on the level set Q(x) = N of Q = x₀² − x₁² − x₂² − x₃² − x₄² diverges because O(Q,ℤ) is infinite (Elie 5289). The natural cutoff is the unit group itself. The level set {Q = N, x₀ > 0} is a copy of hyperbolic 4-space; Γ = O⁺(Q,ℤ) acts on it with finite covolume; **Vinberg's algorithm gives the fundamental domain as a Coxeter simplex**, and every forward vector of norm N is Γ-equivalent to a vector in the closed chamber

  x₁ ≥ x₂ ≥ x₃ ≥ x₄ ≥ 0,  x₀ ≥ x₁ + x₂ + x₃

(simple roots e₁−e₂, e₂−e₃, e₃−e₄, e₄, e₀+e₁+e₂+e₃; Coxeter diagram: chain r₁–r₂–r₃, r₃ =4= r₄, r₃–r₅; the sub-diagram {r₂,r₃,r₄,r₅} is affine B̃₃, the cusp). The stabilizer of a chamber vector is the finite parabolic subgroup generated by the simple roots it is orthogonal to. **Definition (Siegel):**

  **r*(N) := Σ_{x ∈ chamber, Q(x)=N} 1/|W_x|,   Z_cone(s) := Σ_N r*(N) N^{−s}.**

r*(N) is proportional to Siegel's representation measure μ(Q,N) with a constant independent of N (the ratio vol(O(4))/vol(Γ\O(1,4))). This is the cone-zeta. Control: r*(1) = 1/384, |W_{e₀}| = 384 = |B₄| = |O(4,ℤ)| ✓ (one orbit of norm-1 vectors, as forced by unimodularity of e₀^⊥ = −I₄).

## 2. The test, pre-stated
Siegel's theorem for a **one-class genus** says the representation measure is a pure product of local densities: r*(N) = c · N^{3/2} · Π_p α_p(N). The genus of ℤ^{1,4} has one class (indefinite odd unimodular lattices are unique in their signature). So the test of "the genus theta is Eisenstein" is: **r*(N) / (N^{3/2} Π_p α_p(N)) is CONSTANT in N.** A cuspidal component would show as N-dependence. Positive control: the definite I₅ (also one-class), r₅(N) = coefficients of θ⁵, same test.

Local densities computed by convolution of the distribution of ±x² mod p^k (stability checked at k and k+1 for p = 2 and every p | N); for p ∤ 2N, α_p(N) = 1 + (N/p)·p^{−2} (count of a nondegenerate 5-variable form over F_p is p⁴ + p²·(N·det/p); det = 1 for BOTH forms, so **the odd-p densities of ℤ^{1,4} and ℤ⁵ are identical**), product taken to p < 10⁵.

## 4. What this settles and what it does not
- **Settled (DERIVED, checked exactly):** the cone-zeta of D_IV⁵'s integer form is the Dirichlet series of a **genus Eisenstein object**: its coefficients are pure local-density products, with no cuspidal component. Its odd-p Euler data coincide with θ⁵'s. So the "Shimura-lift to a weight-4 Euler-product eigenform" question of F1014 is answered before it is asked: the lift of an Eisenstein series is Eisenstein (weight 4: L-function ζ(s)ζ(s−3)), not a cusp form with its own L(s). **Referee objection 3 in the pre-read ("even the favorable lift is a modular L-function, ζ untouched") is dead in the favorable direction: the lift's L-function is Riemann's ζ, twice.**
- **Settled (negative):** the cone contributes **no new arithmetic condition**. Its Euler data are ζ's own. "The n_C = 5 odd-quinary arithmetic beats Davenport–Heilbronn" (F1014) is true only in the sense that a one-class genus has no cuspidal freedom for zeros to hide in; the object it hands back is built from Riemann's ζ and quadratic L-values.
- **NOT settled — the real remaining question, now well-posed:** Z_cone(s) = c Σ_N N^{3/2} α₂(N) Π_{p odd} α_p(N) N^{−s} is a **Cohen–Eisenstein / Shintani-type Dirichlet series** (coefficients ∝ N^{3/2} L(2, χ_N) up to finite factors), i.e. the Koecher–Maass series of a half-integral-weight Eisenstein series — not the lift's L-function. Whether such a series **factors into shifted Riemann zetas and Dirichlet L-functions** (Ibukiyama–Saito 1995 type explicit formula) or is a genuine Shintani zeta with its own zero set is a literature question first and a computation second. If it factors, RH for the cone-zeta ⟺ RH for ζ and the object gap closes as a definitional collapse (K1862 D hypothesis, now with the Eisenstein half proved). If it does not factor, the cone-zeta is a DH-type object and the row's arithmetic lane is dead as a route to ζ.
