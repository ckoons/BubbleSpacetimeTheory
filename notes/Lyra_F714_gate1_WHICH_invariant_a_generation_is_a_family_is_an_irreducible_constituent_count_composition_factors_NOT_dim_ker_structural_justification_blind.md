## The divergence (K959) and why it is real
After the recast, "the count" was named two ways of the same radical M = null space of the contravariant Gram matrix G at reduction:
- **dim ker(G)** — the raw nullity (Grace/Elie's phrasing).
- **# composition factors of M** — the number of irreducible constituents (Lyra F713).
These are equal only if each family contributes exactly one null direction; they diverge if a family's whole multiplet lies in the radical (then dim ker = Σ family-sizes, not # families). Since the K-types at a level are dim 4, 16, 40, a raw nullity is *irrep-sized* — so the two invariants genuinely differ, and choosing wrong is the last place a 3-or-4 could hide. It must be pinned structurally.

## The structural argument (decisive, sourced)
1. **A generation is a fermion family** — the full replica of one generation's fermion content.
2. **One generation = the domain's 16-real spinor** — one full irreducible fermion multiplet (16 = 2^{n_C−1}, the D₅/SO(10) spinor; sourced: BST_1920_WeylGroup, Five-Absence "one generation = ℍ⁴ = 16"). A family is *one irreducible module*, not a vector.
3. **Therefore a generation is one irreducible CONSTITUENT** of the radical M — a whole composition factor carrying the 16-spinor — not a single null *direction*.
4. **Therefore the generation count = the number of irreducible composition factors of M that carry the fermion multiplet**, i.e. the Jordan–Hölder count of fermion-family factors — **NOT dim ker(G)**, which counts directions (sub-family) and would over-count a family by its multiplet size.
In linear-algebra terms (Casey's frame): after finding the null space of G, **block-diagonalize it under the K-action and count the irreducible blocks that are fermion families** — count blocks, not total nullity.
