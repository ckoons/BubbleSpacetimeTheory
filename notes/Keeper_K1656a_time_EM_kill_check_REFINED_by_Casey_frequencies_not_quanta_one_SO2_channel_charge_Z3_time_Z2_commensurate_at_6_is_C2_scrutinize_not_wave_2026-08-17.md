# K1656a — Casey corrected the kill-check: it's channel-identity, not quantum-match; and the ⅓/½/6 is the clue

## I measured the wrong thing
K1656's kill-check asked "is the charge-SO(2) the same U(1) as the time-SO(2) *given the quantizations differ* (⅓'s vs +2 steps)?" — implying the mismatch is evidence against one circle. **Wrong measure.** An SO(2)/U(1) is *one channel* carrying infinitely many characters e^{ikθ}; different states ride different weights on the *same* circle, and the flow exp(−τJ) turns all of them at once. So charge and energy needn't match numerically to share the SO(2). Casey: *"Does the quantum have to match, or might this be like different frequencies riding in the same channel?"* — the latter, and it's the correct statement of the one-SO(2) hypothesis. I own the mis-framing.

## The refined kill-check (two prongs)
**(1) One SO(2) or two?** Is T2470's charge-SO(2) the *K-center* SO(2) whose flow is time (J = the conformal Hamiltonian), or an *internal* SO(2) (inside the SO(5) factor, or the boundary SO(4,2))? Decidable from the group structure — which SO(2) the T2470 proof actually means, no new physics. Note: if it's the *same generator on the same state*, charge and energy would have to be numerically equal — they're not — so charge is **not** the raw J-eigenvalue. The live option is **one circle, two gradings of the weight.**

**(2) ★ Casey's ⅓/½/6.** The two quantizations are each a *known* structure:
- charge in **thirds** = **ℤ₃** = color triality = **N_c = 3** (quarks combine to unit charge — Casey's read, correct);
- time in **halves** = **ℤ₂** = the double cover = fermion parity / spin.

Two gradings of the *one* SO(2)-weight lattice, commensurate at **lcm(2,3) = 6 = C_2**. If forced, the beat closing *both* the charge-thirds and the time-halves is exactly the Casimir — a Schur-web statement (C_2 counting precisely these two gradings). That would make charge and time genuinely one channel at two commensurate frequencies, with C_2 the beat.

6 = 2·3 is a clean number. **C_2 = lcm(ℤ₃, ℤ₂) is a candidate, not a result** — it could be *forced* by the geometry (real) or a coincidence two structures share. Our rule gives a clean number *more* suspicion, not less. **Do not bank the C_2 = lcm claim.** It's exactly the kind of elegant coincidence-or-forcing the team tests before it earns a tier.
