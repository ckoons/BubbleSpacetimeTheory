## L2 — the open question: why should the colour count be the characteristic multiplicity?

**Name the two objects precisely, because they are not the same kind of thing.**
- **Object A, the multiplicity.** a = dim_ℂ V_1(e), the Peirce ½-space of a minimal tripotent e ∈ V (equivalently the off-diagonal V_{12} of a frame; equivalently the multiplicity of the restricted roots ½(γ_i ± γ_j)). For D_IV⁵ the Peirce decomposition of a minimal tripotent is **5 = 1 + 3 + 1**, so a = 3. This is a dimension of a complex vector space, fixed by root data.
- **Object B, the colour count.** N_c = 3 is the number of basis states of the fundamental representation of the strong gauge group SU(3). This is a *representation-theoretic label of a group*, not a dimension of a distinguished subspace of the domain.
**The gap is therefore not numerical. It is that A is a dimension and B is a group's fundamental, and a dimension match is not a group action.**

**What the corpus already has, and this is why the question is answerable rather than open-ended.**
1. **T2543 (DERIVED, Cal seven-checkpoint cold read) already makes the identification**, in these words: *a record is an idempotent, the idempotent count is the fermionic diagonal of the Peirce decomposition, and the* **mediator J_½ = V₁₂ = colour** *carries neither.* So "colour lives in the Peirce ½-space" is registered, and with it N_c = dim V_1(e) = a. **The identification this theorem rests on is T2543's, and it should be cited as such rather than introduced fresh.**
2. **But the corpus holds a SECOND home for colour**, the bulk-colour mechanism: colour as the SO(3) sub-vector of the SO(5) vector under the branching **5 ↓ SO(3)×SO(2) = (3,0) + (1,±1)**, at CANDIDATE tier.
3. **And Grace has already computed that these two are not the same object**: the Peirce split 5 = 1 + 3 + 1 and the branching split 5 = 3 + 1 + 1 have *the same shape and are different formal objects, and the corpus does not interchange them anywhere.* **So the program has two three-dimensional spaces both called colour, and the theorem's identification uses the first while the mechanism work uses the second.** Naming which one N_c = a refers to is the first thing a derivation must do, and it costs nothing to do today.
4. **The obstruction is already proved, and it is rigorous, not suspected:** `Lyra_BulkColor_v0_3` records, multi-source verified, that **SU(3) = A₂ is not a subgroup of SO(5) = B₂ — SU(3) is not in K.** So no subgroup of the isometry group acts on either three-dimensional space as SU(3)'s fundamental. The natural group acting on the Peirce ½-space of D_IV⁵ is SO(3), and SO(3) ⊂ SU(3) is a real form, not the gauge group.

**So the derivation, stated as the precise thing it must connect.** To turn N_c = a from an identification into a theorem one must exhibit **a group acting on the Peirce ½-space whose fundamental representation is that space and which is the physical colour group** — and, since SU(3) ⊄ K is settled, that group cannot come from the domain's isometries. The two live routes the corpus already contains are: **(a)** get SU(3) from the boundary operator algebra rather than from the isometry group (the Toeplitz route, where the corpus's own count 8 = 3 + 3 + 2 was corrected to a Cartan–Weyl basis and the SO(3)-decomposition mismatch was closed at v0.6), or **(b)** argue that the colour *count* is a dimension and not a group rank, in which case the identification is about how many labels a mediator carries and the gauge group is a separate question. **Route (b) is the honest reading of T2543 as it stands — the mediator carries neither a record nor a bit, and its label count is dim V_1(e) — and it does not need SU(3) inside the geometry at all.**

**The question, sharpened to the form I would put on the board:** *is the number of colour labels the dimension of the mediator space of a record idempotent?* If yes, T2543's chain closes it and the measured input becomes a derivation, because the Peirce decomposition is **forced** the moment one posits that a record is an idempotent — nothing about the domain is chosen at that step. If no, then N_c = a is a coincidence of two threes and the theorem keeps its one measured input. **Either answer is worth more than the eight criteria we struck today**, and the second is a real possibility that must stay on the table: this corpus has spent a week finding threes that were the same three read twice.
