# Round 149 — L2: is f_i − 2 one boundary term read on two factors? Yes as a FORM — edge density × edge motion under the boost — on two windows; and here is exactly where "one term" breaks. One paragraph, hashed; a note on a note.
**Lyra, Monday 2026-09-14, 12:3x EDT.** Inputs: Ellis & Baldwin's threshold term (their Eq. (1) as vHD quote it); von Hausegger–Dalang Eqs. (13)/(14) and (20)/(21); K1903 §2(a) (x at the threshold, not an octave average; Secrest et al. 2025 p. 2); my R148 note.

Take the count law of a window as the cumulative number inside it and ask what a boost does at the window's edge. **On the flux window:** N(>S) is the count above the threshold S*; a boost multiplies every flux by the Doppler factor to the power (1+α), so the edge moves by Δ log S = (1+α) log δ ≈ (1+α)β cos θ, and the count changes by [−d log N/d log S]_{S*} × (1+α)β — i.e. **x(1+α)β = (normalised density at the edge) × (edge displacement per unit β)**, with x ≡ −d log N(>S)/d log S *at the threshold*, the logarithmic edge density of the Rac factor's count law. That is why x must be the derivative at the limit and not a secant across half a magnitude (K1903 §2(a)): a boundary term is evaluated at the boundary. **On the redshift window:** the count inside [z₁, z₂] is ∫ n dz; a boost moves each observed redshift by Δz = (1+z)β cos θ, so each edge moves by (1+z_e)β and the count changes by [(1+z)f_b(z)]_{z₁}^{z₂}·β — **B_i = (normalised density at the edge) × (edge displacement per unit β)**, the same form, on the clock factor's window, with two edges of opposite sign instead of one. The remaining "2" is the sky window's Jacobian (aberration of solid angle), a volume term with no edge. **So f_i − 2 = Σ_windows (edge density) × (edge speed under the boost) — one form, read on two factors — and the R148 rule follows from it: a window on the factor you read contributes its own edge term, a window on the other factor does not, because the reading's ℓ = 1 moment sees an edge only in its own variable.** Where "one term" breaks, three ways, each a fact the estimator must respect: (i) they are derivatives of *different laws* — x of the luminosity function at the limit (a property of the population), B of the redshift distribution at the bin edges — so they share a form and not an object, and only the flux one is what Ellis–Baldwin proved evolution-robust when taken at the threshold; (ii) a flux *bin* (Mittal–Lewis's disjoint cuts) has two edges and its term becomes a difference like B_i, while a flux *threshold* has one — the count channel as frozen (G < 20.5) is one-edged in flux and two-edged in redshift, and the flux-cut derivative of R148 is exactly the second edge's term, taken by subtraction; (iii) with photometric redshifts the clock edge is smooth and the "edge density × edge motion" becomes −∫ f_b · d log W_b/d log(1+z) (Eq. (21)) — density × window gradient — and **the flux side has the same generalisation whenever the selection in flux is smooth or depends on redshift (vHD Sec. III C, Eqs. (25)–(27), which they leave unresolved): the unresolved ∂W_b/∂S* dependence is the smooth-window version of the x term, and the estimator escapes it only because the G-limit is a sharp cut.** Net: f_i − 2 is one boundary form on two windows; the form is exact, the two terms are not one object; and the sharp-cut/two-edge distinctions are where a v1.3 reader should look first if a bin misbehaves.
