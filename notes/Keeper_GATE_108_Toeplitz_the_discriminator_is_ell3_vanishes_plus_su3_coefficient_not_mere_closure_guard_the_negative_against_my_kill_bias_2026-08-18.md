# GATE-108-Toeplitz — the discriminator is "ℓ=3 vanishes + su(3) coefficient," and the NO is guarded

Set before the numbers, and deliberately careful on the negative: three times today I closed a door one notch too hard (color SO(3) swing, C4 "closed structurally," "ℓ=2 not in the geometry"), so the gate must make a NO *earn* its kill, not inherit my bias.

## The exact test (from the reconnected rep-theory)
The gluon quadrupole is the ℓ=2 (spin-2) rep of the color SO(3); the color rotations are ℓ=1 (so(3)). The commutator [Q,Q] is antisymmetric, and for SO(3):
- Λ²(ℓ=2) = ℓ=1 ⊕ ℓ=3 (dims 3 + 7 = 10).
- su(3)/so(3) is a symmetric space, so **[coset, coset] ⊂ so(3) = ℓ=1 only**.

So su(3) makes two sharp, computable demands on the second-shell Toeplitz operators:
1. **The ℓ=3 part of [Q,Q] vanishes.** (If ℓ=3 survives, the algebra is bigger than su(3) — not a finite gauge algebra of the right size.)
2. **The ℓ=1 part = su(3)'s structure constant × L** (the color rotation), with the *right numerical coefficient* (the 2⊗2→1 Clebsch matching su(3)'s f-symbols).

## PASS vs FLOOR
- **PASS (the shell carries su(3); #3 gauge-emergence promotes):** ℓ=3 vanishes AND the ℓ=1 coefficient equals su(3)'s. Then the second-shell quadrupole + the color so(3) close into exactly su(3), and the gauge group has a geometric home.
- **NO / FLOOR (the shell does not carry su(3)):** ℓ=3 present (bigger/different algebra) OR the ℓ=1 coefficient is wrong (SO(3)-related but not su(3)). **BUT a NO must be robust** (see the negative guard) — otherwise it's my kill-bias, not a result.

## ★ The traps, fenced both directions
- **Against a false PASS (construction-guaranteed closure):** "[Q,Q] lands in so(3)" is NOT sufficient — for a generic operator algebra, commutators of ℓ=2's will have *some* ℓ=1 component. The su(3)-SPECIFIC content is (a) the ℓ=3 part being *exactly zero* (not generic) and (b) the ℓ=1 *coefficient* matching su(3) (not just being nonzero). Require both; a bare "closes into so(3)" is empty.
- **Against a false NO (my kill-bias, guarded explicitly):** a NO is only genuine if it survives — (a) **normalization:** the Q's and L's in the natural K-covariant normalization (a wrong scale makes the coefficient look wrong when it isn't); (b) **basis:** the ℓ=2 quintet must be the *color-SO(3)* ℓ=2 inside the SO(5)-14, not some other 5; (c) **the right product:** the *antisymmetric* commutator, not the symmetric anticommutator (Sym²(5)=ℓ=0,2,4 is a different object). A NO must exhibit either a *nonzero ℓ=3* or a *coefficient that is wrong after correct normalization* — not merely "the numbers didn't match at first pass." I will not ratify a NO on a first-pass mismatch; it goes back for the normalization/basis check.
