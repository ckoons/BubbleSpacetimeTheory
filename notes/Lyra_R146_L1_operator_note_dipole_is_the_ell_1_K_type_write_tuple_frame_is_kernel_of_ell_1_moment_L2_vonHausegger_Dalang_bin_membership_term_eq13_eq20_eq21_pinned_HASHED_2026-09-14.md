## L1 — the operator note (two paragraphs)

**The dipole is the ℓ = 1 K-type, and the write tuple is its operator.** On the Šilov boundary Š = (S¹ × S⁴)/ℤ₂ a boundary scalar expands in Hua modes e^{imθ} Y_k, and its ℓ = 1 (k = 1) component is its projection onto H₁ ≅ ℝ⁵, the vector K-type — the coordinate functions x_u. The write tuple of T2630, W_u = M_{z_u} = e^{iθ} M_{x_u}, is multiplication by exactly those ℓ = 1 functions times one unit of clock phase, and Σ_u W_u*W_u = I is the statement that the five ℓ = 1 directions resolve the identity. So the dipole vector of a boundary density ρ, d_u = ⟨x_u, ρ⟩, is the k = 1 matrix element of the first write — "the dipole of a boundary scalar is its first write," in K1898 §5's words — and it sits, by Theorem B (H² ≅ Rac ⊗ C as K-modules), in the k = 1 level of the *Rac factor*, at winding j = 0. After the descent (T2545: the three spatial directions are the real form of V₁₂, so the celestial sphere is the unit sphere of the SO(3) slot), a *sky* dipole is the projection of a sky scalar onto that slot's vector representation — the ℓ = 1 K-type of the SO(3) that T2565's velocity leaves standing. A **frame**, in this language, is the boost (an element of the sky's conformal group, the SO(3,1) of the induced slice) under which the ℓ = 1 moment vanishes: **the kernel of the ℓ = 1 moment map on the boost orbit.** T2565's "one velocity" is then "one kernel," and Cal §957's ε is the rapidity between two kernels.

**Two moments, one vector — where P3 comes from and what it does not claim.** Theorem B splits a record into a Rac factor and a clock reading χ_{1+2j}. A *number-count* dipole is the ℓ = 1 moment of the density of Rac-factor states in a bin (which sources are where). A *redshift* dipole is the ℓ = 1 moment of the clock factor: an observed redshift is a clock reading as received, and its Doppler modulation across the sky is the ℓ = 1 component of the clock-charge field (Wu–Xia's estimator, K1898 §1). Under a single boost both moments are proportional to the same β⃗, so their directions coincide at the common frame's — that is P3, and it is the operator restatement of "one boost, not two": the Rac's ℓ = 1 moment and the clock's ℓ = 1 moment are one vector. **What the rows license and what they do not:** T2630 licenses the write tuple as the ℓ = 1 operators on H²(Š); T2625 (i) licenses that the physical state lives in H²₊; Theorem B licenses the K-module split; the identification of Rac-density with source counts and of clock charge with observed redshift is the same F588-class identification Cal §960 named (particles = the Rac factor), extended by one step to "redshift = clock reading" — IDENTIFIED, on the row's face, and the reason this is a *note* on the row and not a second theorem. Nothing here fixes the intrinsic component, which lives in the ℓ = 1 moment of the *source distribution's own* Rac density and is P2. One structural aside, flagged as structure: since the celestial sphere is the unit sphere of V₁₂, every sky dipole is a vector in the mediator space — the colour slot of Lecture 2 — read as a spatial direction; that is the (3,1) descent seen from the sky, not a new physics claim.

## L2 — the bin-membership correction, in von Hausegger–Dalang's notation (arXiv:2412.13162v2, 20 May 2025; read from the PDF this hour)

**Setting (their Sec. II).** Ellis–Baldwin for a full flux-limited sample: D = [2 + x(1+α)] β, their Eq. (1), with x the integral-count slope and α the spectral index (their footnote 4: α may evolve with z so long as it is measured on the sources near the flux limit). The redshift-resolved kinematic dipole, once the luminosities are power laws L ∝ ν^{−α}, is **their Eq. (6):**
> **D_kin(z) = [ 3 + x(z)(1 + α(z)) + d log n(z) / d log(1+z) ] β,**
where n(z) ≡ dN/dΩdz (z, S > S*) is the observed redshift distribution above the flux cut, and z is the *observed (boosted)* redshift. The "3" is the projected "2" plus one from the redshift boost; the logarithmic-derivative term is what integrates by parts into boundary terms.

**Sharp top-hat bin in redshift (their Sec. II A).** For a bin b = [z₁, z₂], with f_b(z) ≡ n(z)/∫_{z₁}^{z₂} n dz (their Eq. (8)) and effective redshift z_b = ∫ z f_b dz (Eq. (9)), the bin's expected kinematic dipole is (Eq. (7) with Eq. (6), reduced by Eqs. (10)–(12)) **their Eq. (13):**
> **D_kin(z_b) = [ 2 + x̃_b(1 + α̃_b) + (1+z) f_b(z) |_{z₁}^{z₂} ] β,**
with x̃_b, α̃_b the values of x and α computed from the bin's own sources only, and the **boundary term** **B(z_b) ≡ (1+z) f_b(z)|_{z₁}^{z₂}, their Eq. (14).** It reduces to Eq. (1)'s form iff (1+z₁)f_b(z₁) = (1+z₂)f_b(z₂), e.g. when f_b vanishes at both edges — true for a full sample, false for a slice. For f(z) = const the normalised f_b = 1/(z₂ − z₁) gives B = 1: the bin's kinematic amplitude is [3 + x̃(1+α̃)]β, not [2 + x̃(1+α̃)]β. **The term is read off the data (n(z) at the edges) with no luminosity function**, and it can be negative — the paper's abstract: "the correction terms can become substantial, even to the degree that the direction of the dipole flips." **For the freeze:** a reversed or shrunken per-bin dipole is not Landing B until B(z_b) has been subtracted.

**Top-hat bin in *observed* (photometric) redshift z′ (their Sec. III) — the Quaia case.** Quaia's redshifts carry per-source errors, so a sharp cut on z′ is a smooth selection on the boosted redshift z. With P(z′, z) the conditional distribution learned from a spectroscopic cross-match (Eq. (15)), the selection function on z is **their Eq. (16):** W_b(z) ≡ ∫₀^∞ dz′ W′_b(z′) P(z′, z), and the bin's true-redshift distribution is n_b(z) = n(z) W_b(z) (Eq. (17)); f_b and z_b are then Eq. (18) over n_b. Assuming the selection is flux-cut independent (∂W_b/∂S* = 0), the bin's kinematic dipole is **their Eq. (20):**
> **D_kin(z_b) = [ 2 + x̃_b(1 + α̃_b) + (1+z) f_b(z)|₀^∞ − ∫₀^∞ dz f_b(z) · d log W_b(z) / d log(1+z) ] β,**
whose selection ("smooth boundary") term is **their Eq. (21):** **B(z_b) ≡ −∫₀^∞ dz f_b(z) d log W_b(z)/d log(1+z)**; for a top hat on z itself the delta functions at the edges return Eq. (14) (their Eqs. (22)–(23)); for a Gaussian W_b of width σ_z (narrow top hat on z′, Gaussian errors) it is **their Eq. (24):** B(z_b) = σ_z^{−2}[∫ z² f_b dz − (∫ z f_b dz)²], which equals 1 when f is constant across the bin (the Gaussian variance divides out) and departs from 1 exactly when f(z) is not constant — i.e. when the bin sits on a rising or falling part of n(z), which is every Quaia bin except one.

(16); (4) n(z) from the catalogue, n_b = n W_b, f_b and z_b by Eqs. (17)–(18); (5) x̃_b and α̃_b from the bin's own sources by Ellis–Baldwin; (6) B(z_b) by Eq. (21), numerically; (7) the per-bin expectation D_kin(z_b) by Eq. (20), with the boundary term at 0 and ∞ dropped when n_b vanishes there. **The frozen model is Eq. (20) with Eq.
