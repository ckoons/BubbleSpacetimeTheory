## 3. The evolution debate, pinned, and why the per-bin prescription is the answer to it
Dalang & Bonvin 2022, MNRAS 512, 3895 (arXiv:2111.03616; corrections MNRAS 521, 2225, 2023) and Guandalin, Piat, Clarkson & Maartens 2023, ApJ 953, 144 (arXiv:2212.04925): luminosity-function evolution (x(r), α(r)) can bias the *expected* projected dipole by > 3σ. von Hausegger 2024, MNRAS Lett 535, L49 (arXiv:2404.07929): the Ellis–Baldwin test is robust to such evolution. The freeze already measures (x, α) *per bin from the catalogue's own counts* — the binning absorbs the evolution the projection argument worries about — and should say so, citing both sides, so a referee does not raise it as if unconsidered. Ellis & Baldwin's own scope sentence (p. 379, Grace's pin) is the license: "irrespective of selection effects or source evolution, as long as the forward and backward measurements are done in the identical manner."
