# Two openings for the package

Both drafts are the **same content in the same tier**. Only the order changes. That is the whole experiment: order is not presentation, it decides which question the reader asks on page two.

---

## OPENING A — lead with what the geometry forces

> **One geometry, and what follows from it.**
>
> This package reports what a single mathematical object — the bounded symmetric domain D_IV⁵ = SO₀(5,2)/[SO(5)×SO(2)] — determines about the Standard Model when nothing else is added.
>
> The domain is fixed by internal criteria, not by fitting. Its invariants are five integers — rank 2, N_c = 3, n_C = 5, C₂ = 6, g = 7 — each a property of the geometry: root multiplicities, dimensions, a Casimir. **There are no free dimensionless parameters, and exactly one dimensionful input** — a scale, as general relativity takes G and the Standard Model takes v.
>
> From that premise, without further assumption:
>
> - **the number of colours is three** — it is the short-root multiplicity of the domain's restricted root system, not a count put in by hand;
> - **the colour symmetry the geometry supplies is the real group SO(3)**, and we can prove it contains no more (Section 3);
> - **baryon number is a topological winding**, exactly conserved, forbidding proton decay permanently;
> - **the proton-to-electron mass ratio is 6π⁵ = N_c!·π^{n_C}**, 1836.12 against 1836.15 — 0.002%;
> - **the one-loop running of the strong coupling comes out of the same operator's heat kernel**, blind, with no coefficient adjusted — including the sign that makes the theory asymptotically free.
>
> Where the geometry stops, we say so and prove where the line is. **SU(3)'s gluon dynamics is not forced by the shape**: we import the gauge principle, as every derivation of the Standard Model does. **The boundary spectral gap we compute is a compactification gap, not the Yang–Mills mass gap of the Clay problem** — the two scale oppositely under decompactification (Section 6). And **n_C = 5, the domain's own dimension, is an input we have not derived.**
>
> The claim is not that everything follows. It is that a surprising amount does, from a premise fixed before any measurement was consulted — and that the places it does not follow can be named exactly.

## OPENING B — lead with the boundaries

> **Where this geometry stops.**
>
> It is easy to write a theory that explains everything and hard to write one that says precisely what it cannot do. This package tries to do the second, because the first is not evidence of anything.
>
> So we begin with the boundaries, four of them, each proved rather than conceded:
>
> - **The geometry does not give us SU(3).** It gives the real group SO(3), and we prove it *provably does not contain* the eight gluon generators. The colour dynamics is the gauge principle, imported — the same standard input every derivation of the Standard Model takes.
> - **The spectral gap we compute is not the Yang–Mills mass gap.** It is a compactification gap of a free operator, colour-blind, and it vanishes in exactly the limit where the Clay gap must survive. They scale oppositely. We do not offer one as evidence for the other.
> - **The charged-lepton mass forms are a patchwork**, not readings of one equation. We call a patchwork a patchwork.
> - **n_C = 5 — the dimension of the domain itself — is an input.** Four independent routes say it is special rather than forced.
>
> That is the honest edge. **Everything on the other side of it is forced by the same one object, with no free dimensionless parameter and a single dimensionful scale:** the number of colours (three, as a root multiplicity), the real colour symmetry SO(3), exactly-conserved baryon number as a topological winding, the proton-to-electron mass ratio 6π⁵ to 0.002%, and the one-loop running of the strong coupling — asymptotic freedom included — falling blind out of the same operator's heat kernel.
>
> We also state what would kill it. A single neutron–antineutron oscillation event, a proton decay, a supersymmetric partner, a magnetic monopole, a sterile neutrino: any one ends this. **A theory that forbids is falsifiable in a way that a theory that accommodates is not**, and that asymmetry is the point of the exercise.
>
> The instrument we used to draw those boundaries is the same one that lowered our own count of derived results from twenty-one to twelve while none of the numbers moved.

## Costing — both branches, per the every-branch-costed rule

Neither opening is free. The costs are different in kind, which is why this is a judgement rather than a calculation.

### Opening A — lead with forces

| | |
|---|---|
| **Buys** | Conventional structure; results first, which is what a physicist expects and skims for. The strongest true claims land while attention is highest. Reads as confident. |
| **Costs** | **Pattern-matching risk is highest in the first paragraph, which is where dismissal happens.** "One geometry determines the Standard Model" is the sentence Eddington, Wyler and a hundred others also wrote — and referees classify before they evaluate. The boundaries arrive in paragraph six, by which point the reader has already decided what kind of paper this is. |
| **Fails when** | The reader is a sceptical specialist with limited time — precisely our target. |

### Opening B — lead with boundaries

| | |
|---|---|
| **Buys** | **Disarms the dismissal reflex before it fires.** Leading with a *proved* negative ("provably does not contain the gluon generators") is not a hedge; it is a demonstration of technical command, and it cannot be faked by a crank — the failure mode being screened for cannot produce it. The forced results then land on a reader who has already decided the authors are careful. |
| **Costs** | **Reads as diffident to some editors, and confidence is a real signal.** A reader who stops after two paragraphs takes away only what the theory cannot do. It is unconventional enough that a subset of referees will find it affected or evasive — "why publish if you're this unsure?" And it risks the results being read as consolation prizes. |
| **Fails when** | The reader is generous but hurried, and needs a reason to keep going that the first paragraph does not supply. |

### The asymmetry that matters

The two failure modes are not equally recoverable. **A reader lost to pattern-matching in paragraph one is lost permanently and silently** — no reply, no referee report, no second look. **A reader who finds Opening B diffident but keeps reading is recoverable**, because the forced results are still there two paragraphs down and they are strong.

**One is a silent, total, irreversible loss. The other is a stylistic complaint from someone still reading.**

### Recommendation, flagged as such

**Opening B**, on that asymmetry alone — with one modification: **the first sentence must earn the right to be modest.** "It is easy to write a theory that explains everything and hard to write one that says precisely what it cannot do" does that work; a flat "here is what we cannot do" would not.

But this is a judgement about audience and register, not about physics, and **Casey knows the audience.** The decision is his, and Opening A is defensible — it is how nearly every successful paper is written, and the pattern-matching risk is a probability, not a certainty.
