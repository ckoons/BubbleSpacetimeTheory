# Abstract

A proper 4-colouring of a triangulated surface lifts to a height function with values in the lattice ℤ² whenever every vertex has even degree; at an odd vertex the height acquires a reflection monodromy. We show that the lift lives naturally on the double cover branched at the odd vertices, that the sign of the lift is a proper 2-colouring of the faces of that cover which does not depend on the colouring, and that its class in the cover's first cohomology with ℤ₃ coefficients — the charge class — decides, for the whole triangulation at once, whether the lattice of dislocation heights of every colouring lies in the charge kernel. The verdict is a computation on the rotation system; it never looks at a colouring. We organise this as an observer tower: each floor is the covering space that unwinds the monodromy of the floor below, each floor realizes exactly the records whose monodromy the floor was built from, and the price of each floor is the logarithm of its sheet number. On the torus the tower for the Heawood sign record has height one; for the height record it has three forced layers, sign, charge and periods, the third infinite and excluded from the finite height by convention. We prove that a fullerene dual can be confined only if its atom count is a multiple of six and at least sixty, and we report four predictions that were hashed before the data: the confinement of the C₆₀ dual, ten graph-level verdicts on isolated-pentagon fullerenes through C₇₈, the derived halves of a para-position criterion on 1,267 isomers, and the divisibility law on 39,621 isomers through C₁₂₀. Every count is beside the null that predicts it. We state what is asserted for all triangulations and what is not.

# 0. Two registers

**The formal register** is Sections 1–7: statements with their inputs, proofs or pointers to the registered re-derivations, and every count with its population and its null.

**The intuitive register** is this paragraph and the first paragraph of each section. A colouring is a record written on a surface with a local rule. Someone reading the record along a closed path may find that it does not come back to itself: the record is locally fine everywhere and globally realized by nothing. The reader one floor up lives on the surface that unwinds exactly those paths; their extra coordinate is which sheet they are on. Each floor's new knowledge is precisely what the floor below could not realize, and each floor costs the logarithm of its number of sheets. For 4-colourings the floors are forced: a sign floor, a charge floor, a period floor. How high the tower goes is a property of the surface's graph, not of any colouring drawn on it, and for fullerenes it is decided by where the pentagons sit.

# 1. The height dictionary and the branched double cover

*Intuitive.* Give the three colour differences three directions in the plane that sum to zero. A colouring becomes a landscape: walk any edge and climb or descend by the edge's direction. Around a vertex of even degree the walk closes; around a vertex of odd degree it does not — that vertex is a dislocation, and the landscape lives on a two-sheeted surface branched there.

**1.1 Setting (T2577).** Colours V = GF(2)²; edge labels ℓ(uw) = f(u) + f(w) ∈ {a, b, c}; step vectors A = (1,0), B = (0,1), C = (−1,−1) with A + B + C = 0 and A, B, C ≡ a, b, c mod 2. A height is h: V(T) → ℤ² with h(w) − h(u) = σ·L(ℓ(uw)), σ the sign of the face to the left of u→w. A face closes with either sign; coherence across an edge forces adjacent faces to opposite signs; a height exists on T iff every vertex has even degree.

**1.2 The cover.** Let D be the set of odd-degree vertices, |D| = k (even). Σ → T is the double cover branched at D, unique (each puncture loop must flip the sign); genus g = k/2 − 1 for k ≥ 2 (Riemann–Hurwitz, χ(Σ) = 2χ(T) − k). Every vertex of Σ has even degree and the lifted sign σ̃ alternates across every edge, so the increment cochain ω̃ ∈ Z¹(Σ; ℤ²) is defined.

**1.3 Theorem (rank of the lift; E1).** The global datum of the lift is [ω̃] ∈ H¹(Σ; ℤ²) ≅ ℤ^{2k−4} (k ≥ 2). Three routes agree: cohomology; the monodromy representation in ℤ² ⋊ ℤ₂, in which each dislocation acts as a point reflection through its own height, with one relation and a two-dimensional gauge; and the arc periods 2(h(q) − h(p)) between dislocations. All periods are even, which is exactly why the colouring h mod 2 is single-valued while the height is not. The centre lattice L := P/2 is spanned by the tree differences of the dislocation heights and equals their span exactly (the monodromy is generated by reflections through the tree centres). Registered: T2577 (with the Section-4 Burgers-translation clause withdrawn 2026-09-02); the rank and the reflection form in the E1 file of 2026-09-02.

**1.4 Corollary (Fisk).** If k = 2 then Σ is a sphere, the height is single-valued on Σ, the deck involution forces both branch points to one height, and the two odd vertices receive the same colour in every 4-colouring. (Re-derived unaimed; controls 144/144.)

# 2. The sign record and its realizability

*Intuitive.* Heawood's face signs are the record. On the sphere every locally valid record is realized by a colouring; on a torus some are not, and the reason is never that a record has too many completions — it always has exactly twelve — but that some records are orphans.

**2.1 Lemma R and R′ (T2574, T2598).** On any closed orientable triangulated surface a realized Heawood sign record determines its colouring up to A₄ = V ⋊ ℤ₃ (one completion per realized record modulo A₄; the direction uses only the local-completion torsor, never H₁ = 0). Realizability is staged: a Heawood-closed record is realized iff its transport class θ ∈ H¹(S; ℤ₃) vanishes and, given that, its labelling class [ℓ] ∈ H¹(S; V) vanishes. On the sphere both groups vanish (Heawood's theorem). Measured: sphere and torus records, 2,374 + 1,332, one completion per realized record; orphans on every torus tested.

**2.2 The torsor and the tower (T2601, T2602).** A closed record is a flat A₄-torsor with monodromy Φ: π₁(S) → A₄; realized iff Φ = 1; the derived cover of Φ realizes it with |im Φ| ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 12} sheets (never 6). The full derived cover realizes every orphan on every surface in one floor by construction (5.1(ii)); the content on the torus is the staged statement. **One-Floor Theorem (torus, staged form).** On the torus π₁ is abelian, so im Φ is an abelian subgroup of A₄, hence either inside V or cyclic of order 3 (no abelian subgroup mixes a 3-cycle with V); therefore the ℤ₃-floor alone or the V-floor alone realizes every torus orphan, and the two-stage series of 2.1 never needs both stages (906/906 transport orphans; two independent proofs, commutator and deck-isotopy). At genus two the staged form fails as predicted: every A₄-image record needs the twelve-sheet floor (695,054 witnesses).

**2.3 The count and the decision cost (T2600, T2609).** The number of monodromies is |Hom(π₁(Σ_g), A₄)| = 12^{2g−1}(3 + 3^{2−2g}) = 48, 5,376, 749,568 (Frobenius–Mednykh). On fixed-width lattice tori the decision cost D = log₂(N_closed/N_realized) has limit log₂|H| with H the subgroup generated by achievable long-cycle holonomies (Perron–Frobenius on the group-graded transfer operator): on one free generator the stage-two cost tends to log₂ 4 = log₂|Hom(ℤ, V)| (1.585 measured at width 6 for a = 90 and 120, Grace 5652 on the 5628/5647 artifacts, i.e. 3.585 − log₂ 3), and the full torsor's log₂ 12; the square-torus value is not asserted here (Section 7); the finite-size term decays as the colouring transfer matrix's second eigenvalue ratio (0.750, 0.854 at widths 3, 6), whose cube-root phases give both residue branches from one eigenvalue.

**2.4 The odd-cycle theorem (T2608).** For a θ-clean record and a label a, the V-image lies in the line ⟨a⟩ iff the graph of b- and c-edges (a quadrangulation of the surface) is bipartite, and every odd b∪c cycle is non-contractible (28,441/28,441 measured; proved by the holonomy count and a rerouting across a-edges).

# 3. The charge class theorem

*Intuitive.* The sign of the landscape is not something the colouring chooses. It is a chessboard colouring of the faces of the two-sheeted surface, the same for every colouring. Reading that chessboard around loops gives a number mod three; whether those numbers can all be made to vanish is a property of the surface alone.

**3.1 Theorem (T2603).** The height sign of a 4-colouring is a proper 2-colouring of the faces of Σ, well defined and independent of the colouring (the colouring chooses which of A, B, C is stepped, never the sign). The cocycle c(u→w) := σ̃(left face) ∈ ℤ₃ has class [c] ∈ H¹(Σ; ℤ₃), and [c](γ) is the charge x + y mod 3 of the period of γ. The centre lattice L of every colouring lies in the charge kernel Λ₀ = {x + y ≡ 0} iff [c] = 0. Hence for a fixed triangulation either every colouring is confined or none is.

**3.2 The turning-parity rule.** Along a path, s_out = s_in·(−1)^{j+1} at a vertex entered at spoke 0 and left at spoke j; the charge of a dislocation-pair difference is the signed step count mod 3; a path of length one is charged (the adjacency law, T2605); a two-step through an even vertex is neutral iff the spoke separation is even. Tree neutrality is equivalent to [c] = 0 (L = span of tree differences; 7,036/7,036 graphs, seventeen path systems).

**3.3 Fourth formulation (Fisk's criterion).** [c] = 0 iff Σ, an Eulerian triangulation of genus k/2 − 1, is properly 3-colourable. On the sphere Eulerian ⟺ 3-colourable is Heawood 1898; on a closed surface Eulerian is necessary and not sufficient, and the obstruction to 3-colouring an even triangulation is a class in H¹(Σ; ℤ₃) — Fisk, Adv. Math. 24 (1977) 298–340. The criterion of this section is therefore Fisk's. What is new is that Fisk's obstruction class of the branched double cover IS the height charge of the 4-colourings of the base, the same for every colouring (3.1), together with the face count of 4.3 and the height reading of 5.2. (Referee pin owed: [c] verified against Fisk's definition on the page, not from memory.) Mohar–Salas 2009 define an invariant of a 4-colouring on a 3-colourable base, its degree mod 12, and prove it Kempe-invariant; [c] is an invariant of the triangulation, and no Kempe statement is made in this paper. The observer tower of Section 5 gives a fifth formulation: height one iff confined.

# 4. Confinement

*Intuitive.* A fullerene is confined when its dislocation landscape is stuck in one third of the lattice. The pentagons decide: two pentagons opposite across a hexagon unstick it; twelve pentagons connected by "meta" neighbours keep it stuck. And no fullerene smaller than C₆₀ can be stuck at all.

*Scope.* 1.3, 1.4, 3.1–3.3 and 5.2 hold for every sphere triangulation with an odd vertex; 4.3 is specific to fullerene duals because its face count uses the degrees 5 and 6. The fullerene is the example, not the theorem.

**4.1 C₆₀ (T2604).** The pentakis dodecahedron is confined: predicted from adjacency on 2026-09-03 07:16, measured on all 3,190 colourings (Elie 5642; independently on all 76,560 raw colourings), and derived on both halves (every hexagon vertex sees its three pentagons at spokes 0, 2, 4; [c] = 0 on Σ computed by three instruments).

**4.2 The para criterion (T2611).** On an isolated-pentagon fullerene dual: any para pair (two pentagons on one hexagon at spoke separation 3) forces [c] ≠ 0 (1,243/1,243); a para-free dual with a connected meta graph on the twelve is confined (11/11); the conjectured clause "meta-connectivity is automatic" died on ten para-free, meta-disconnected, unconfined isomers (2026-09-03 14:29), and three confined isomers are also meta-disconnected — that cell is decided by the class, not by any shortcut. Symmetry is enriched but not determining (T2612; confined isomers with trivial automorphism group exist from C₁₀₈).

**4.3 Divisibility Theorem (T2614; derived 2026-09-03 14:43; the face count was derived independently by the referee at 14:45 with an exception class for a disconnected pentagon two-step graph; the deck-involution step below closes that class, so the theorem is unconditional; referee pass 2026-09-03 15:09).** Let the fullerene dual be confined and let φ: V(Σ) → ℤ₃ be a proper 3-colouring of Σ (3.3). The deck involution ι reverses the sign and so satisfies −φ∘ι = φ + κ for a constant κ; at a branch point, fixed by ι, this gives 2φ = −κ, hence φ = κ: all twelve dislocations share the colour κ, with no connectivity hypothesis. Every face of Σ has exactly one vertex of each colour, so the 2m faces are counted by the degrees of the class-κ vertices: twelve branch points of degree 10 and e_κ further vertices of degree 6, 120 + 6e_κ = 2m, i.e. m = 60 + 3e_κ. The class κ is ι-stable (φ(v) = κ forces φ(ιv) = −2κ = κ) and ι is fixed-point-free off the branch points, so the e_κ non-branch vertices of class κ come in ι-pairs and e_κ is even. Hence **6 | m and m ≥ 60**, and D := e_κ/2 = (m − 60)/6 is the number of base hexagon vertices with both lifts in the dislocation class. Necessary, not sufficient. It explains without a colouring why no fullerene dual below C₆₀ is confined (0 of 3,958; sealed census 8500f714) while at C₆₀ exactly one of 1,812 is (the icosahedral isomer, D = 0), and why every confined isomer through C₁₂₀ has 6 | m (14 + 78 confined, all at 6 | m; 0 of 1,003 isolated-pentagon isomers C₆₀…C₁₀₀ with 6 ∤ m, and 0 of 21,964 at C₁₀₂…C₁₂₀). D is exact on all 92 confined isomers (Elie 5659, hashed before the run).

**4.4 The lists.** The fourteen confined isolated-pentagon isomers through C₁₀₀ (there are none at C₉₈ or C₁₀₀, as 6 ∤ m), fullgen ipr order: C₆₀; C₇₂ #0; C₇₈ #1; C₈₄ #19, #22; C₉₀ #30, #36, #37; C₉₆ #1, #132, #133, #155, #161, #184; and 78 more through C₁₂₀ (C₁₀₂: 6 of 616; C₁₀₈: 15 of 1,799; C₁₁₄: 17 of 4,468; C₁₂₀: 40 of 10,774). Nothing is asserted about isomers not computed except the theorems of 3.1 and 4.3.

# 5. The Observer Tower Theorem

*Intuitive.* Build the floors; do not describe them. Floor one is the two-sheeted surface that unwinds the sign; floor two is the three-sheeted surface that unwinds the charge; floor three unwinds the periods and is infinite. Each floor's records are the floor below's orphans. The bill is additive.

*Standard names first.* The covering construction in (i) below is the voltage/derived-graph construction of Gross–Tucker; the count in 2.3 is Frobenius–Mednykh and its limit is Perron–Frobenius; the cost is the logarithm of a uniform choice, Shannon's. Two theorems in this paper are new: 3.1 (the height sign is a colouring-independent proper 2-colouring of the cover's faces whose ℤ₃ class decides confinement for the whole triangulation) and 4.3 (6 | m and m = 60 + 6D). Record, observer, floor and tower are the intuitive register's names for these objects and are used as such.

**5.1 Theorem (Round 114; Cal §839 PASS with four amendments, incorporated).** Let a record on a closed oriented surface carry a local law and a monodromy Φ: π₁(S) → G, and fix a normal series G = G₀ ⊳ G₁ ⊳ … ⊳ 1 (the full derived cover is the series of length one). (i) Floor k+1 is the covering space of S_k for Φ_k⁻¹(G_{k+1}), built by the voltage construction from the record's own cocycle; faces lift because the local law makes face voltages trivial; the pulled-back record has monodromy Φ_k∘p_* and loses exactly the monodromy the floor was built from. (ii) The height of the tower is, by definition, the length of the chosen series; the theorem is that the full derived cover has height one, and that for the height record the series is forced in its first step (the sign layer, because the charge cocycle exists only on Σ) and natural in its second (the ℤ₃ charge is a finite quotient of the period group, taken before the infinite ℤ² period layer, which is read modulo the period lattice by convention). The ℤ² period layer is infinite and is excluded from the finite height by this convention; that is how the abstract's three forced layers give the height two of 5.2. (iii) Floor k+1 newly realizes exactly the floor-k orphans whose monodromy image meets G_{k+1} trivially (for a series of length one: all of them); pull-back preserves realizability, reflects it exactly for the killed monodromy, and is injective on closed records, while the cover also carries new closed records that are not pull-backs. (iv) Costs add: log₂|G₀| = Σ log₂|G_k/G_{k+1}| — on a single graph a position (a group-order sum by definition); on a transfer family a derived limit, by Perron–Frobenius on the graded transfer operator with the achievable-holonomy subgroup exhibited (T2609, T2610).

**5.2 Corollary (height = verdict).** For the height record of any sphere triangulation with an odd vertex: height 0 iff no odd vertex; height 1 iff [c] = 0 iff confined; height 2 otherwise; finite cost log₂ 2 or log₂ 2 + log₂ 3. Measured on 7,037 graphs (Grace 5655): 7,022 at height 2, 15 at height 1, matching the class list exactly.

**5.3 One theory at two resolutions.** A closed height record is a rainbow labelling (the sign data are graph-determined), realized iff [ℓ] = 0: the sign record's stage two. Its cost exceeds the sign record's stage-two cost by exactly log₂ 3 (N_closed^h = 3·N_{θ=0}, N_realized^h = N_realized), so its limit is the full torsor's: log₂ 12 on one free generator. Pre-registered 2026-09-03 13:35 (kill: an offset other than exactly log₂ 3); measured the same day (Grace 5652 on the registered 5628/5647 artifacts): D_h = log₂ 3 + D₂ identically, limit 3.5850 = log₂ 12 at width 6 (a = 90 and 120; 3.5843 at a = 60; width 3: 3.5834 at a = 30, converging on the same value), finite-size decay ratios 0.751 and 0.854, the colouring transfer matrix's second eigenvalue, as in 2.3. The square-torus value log₂ 48 follows by the same identity from the pre-registration of 2.3 and is not measured (Section 7).

# 7. What is asserted for all triangulations, and what is not

**Asserted:** 1.3 (the rank and the reflection form), 1.4, 2.1, 2.2 on the torus, 3.1–3.3 and 5.1–5.2 for every sphere triangulation with an odd vertex, and 4.3 for fullerene duals, as stated with their inputs. Charge, confinement, dislocation and observer name the combinatorial objects of 1.2, 3.1 and 5.1; no physical correspondence is asserted. **Not asserted:** the Four Colour Theorem or anything about it; the confined list beyond C₁₂₀; sufficiency of 6 | m; the square-torus decision-cost limit log₂ 48 (predicted in 2.3, not measured); any mechanism for the label-asymmetric lines (T2606: C₄₈ #0's {x − y ≡ 0}); anything about record spaces without a local-completion torsor (3-SAT has none: the satisfying assignments of a clause are not a coset of a group acting simply transitively on the local completions, so no torsor and no monodromy are defined); the identification of the decision-cost limit with a group order beyond the equidistribution that Perron–Frobenius provides on the families measured. The theory's second record space — boundary functions on the Shilov boundary of D_IV⁵ with the Hardy space as the realized set — EXISTS but has no local law (the Shilov boundary is totally real, so the tangential Cauchy–Riemann system is empty and every square-integrable function is "closed"); without a local law there is no monodromy, no floors and no tower, and nothing in this paper is asserted about it beyond that sentence.
