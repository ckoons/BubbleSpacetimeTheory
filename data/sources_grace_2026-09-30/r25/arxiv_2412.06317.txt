                                             ON THE CLASSIFICATION OF UNITARY HIGHEST WEIGHT
                                                    MODULES IN THE EXCEPTIONAL CASES




arXiv:2412.06317v2 [math.RT] 21 Jan 2025
                                           PAVLE PANDŽIĆ, ANA PRLIĆ, GORDAN SAVIN, VLADIMÍR SOUČEK, AND VÍT TUČEK


                                                    Abstract. In our previous paper [PPST3], we gave a complete classification
                                                    of the unitary highest weight modules for the universal covers of the Lie groups
                                                    Sp(2n, R), SO ∗ (2n) and SU (p, q), using the Dirac inequality and the so called
                                                    PRV product. In this paper, we complete the classification of the unitary
                                                    highest weight modules for the remaining cases; i.e., universal covers of the
                                                    Lie groups SOe (2, n), E6(−14) and E7(−25) . We also describe unitary highest
                                                    weight modules with given infinitesimal characters.

                                           highest weight Harish-Chandra modules, i.e., unitary Harish-Chandra modules gen-

                                           root spaces in the complexified Lie algebra g of G ([HC1], [HC2]). Let g0 = k0 ⊕ p0

                                           in our cases (G, K) is Hermitian, the K-module p decomposes into two irreducible
                                           submodules, p = p+ ⊕ p− , and each of p± is an abelian subalgebra of g. Further-
                                           more, g and k have equal rank, and we choose a Cartan subalgebra t of g contained

that D is self adjoint with respect to this inner product. It follows that D2 ≥ 0;
this is Parthasarathy’s Dirac inequality mentioned above.
Dirac inequality can be rewritten in more concrete terms using the formula (1.1)
for D2 and the relation between Casimir actions and infinitesimal characters. As
in the introduction of [PPST3] it can be easily shown that if L(λ) is unitary, then

unitary if and only if the inequality (1.2) holds strictly for any K-type µ 6= λ of
L(λ).
The problem with this approach is that it is difficult to determine all the K–types of
L(λ). Luckily, it is enough to consider the so-called PRV components of the tensor
products of the irreducible finite-dimensional k-module Fλ with highest weight λ
and each K–type of the module S(p− ), the so-called Schmid modules, which are
very well known (see [S], [PPST3]). More precisely, the following corollary holds
(see [PPST3, Corollary 2.5] for the proof):
Corollary 1.4. (1) Let s0 be a Schmid module such that for any Schmid module s
of strictly lower level than s0 , the strict Dirac inequality
(1.5)                       k(λ − s)+ + ρk2 > kλ + ρk2
holds. (Here (λ − s)+ denotes the unique Wk –conjugate of λ − s). Furthermore,
suppose that
                         k(λ − s0 )+ + ρk2 < kλ + ρk2 .

Then L(λ) is not unitary.
(2) If (1.5) holds for all Schmid modules s, then N (λ) is irreducible and unitary.
In [PPST3], we constructed all unitary highest weight modules in the discrete part
of the classification via the Segal-Shale-Weil representation and its decompositions,
tensor products and PRV products (see [PPST3, Definition 2.10.]). Unfortunately,
in exceptional cases, we cannot construct unitary highest-weight modules in the
discrete part via the PRV products. However, we give a shorter proof of [EHW,
Proposition 12.6] in Section 6 using the theta correspondence. For the proof that
all highest weight modules in the discrete part of the classification for the case E7
are unitary see [EHW, Pages 139 and 140]). The situation is similar to the classical
case in the sense that for E7 the proof of [EHW] uses the decomposition of the
second tensor power of scalar unitarizable module from the discrete part.
In the second part of the paper, we describe unitary highest-weight modules with
given infinitesimal characters.

                     2. Classification for so(2, 2n − 2), n ≥ 3
In this case g is so(2n, C) and k is so(2n − 2, C) plus a one-dimensional center.
We use standard coordinates and the standard positive root system. The positive
compact roots are
                             ǫi ± ǫj , 2 ≤ i < j ≤ n
and the positive noncompact roots are
                                  ǫ1 ± ǫj ,   2 ≤ j ≤ n.
So the half sum of all positive roots is
                                   ρ = (n − 1, . . . , 0)
and a weight λ = (λ1 , . . . , λn ) is k-dominant if and only if
                             λ2 ≥ λ3 ≥ · · · ≥ λn−1 ≥ |λn |.
Integrality with respect to k amounts to λi ± λj ∈ Z for 2 ≤ i, j ≤ n.
The highest (noncompact) root is β = ǫ1 + ǫ2 . There are just two basic Schmid
representations, one with highest weight β and the other one with highest weight
β2 = 2ǫ1 .
The basic Dirac inequality for a PRV component with a Schmid module s is
(2.1)                         k(λ − s)+ + ρk2 ≥ kλ + ρk2
This is equivalent to
(2.2)                              2hγ | λ + ρi ≤ kγk2
where γ is defined by (λ − s)+ = λ − γ. Since every root has norm squared equal
to 2, this is further equivalent to
(2.3)                                hγ | λ + ρi ≤ 1.
The Weyl group of k acts on weights by permuting coordinates λ2 , . . . , λn and
changing an even number of their signs. It is because of these sign changes and
spinor representations that we have to distinguish several cases. The following
lemma was proved in [PPST1, Lemma 3.1.].

(2.5)
              λ1 + λ2 ≤ 2 + p − 2n for λ = (λ1 , λ2 , . . . , λp , . . . , λn )
                        where 1 ≤ λ2 = · · · = λp > λp+1 and 2 ≤ p ≤ n.
We will refer to the first case as the scalar case, the second case is the spinor case
and the remaining one is the general case. The following theorem gives a complete
classification of unitary highest weight modules for SO(2, 2n − 2).
Theorem 2.6. Non-scalar modules are unitarizabile if and only if (2.5) holds. If
the inequality is satisfied strictly, the modules are irreducible Verma modules. In the
scalar case, unitarizable highest weight modules are the trivial module, the Wallach
module (2 − n, 0, . . . , 0) and irreducible Verma modules (λ1 , 0, . . . , 0), λ1 < 2 − n.
Proof. In view of Corollary 1.4, the theorem follows from [PPST1, Theorems 3.5,
3.6 and 3.7]. Namely, the only module which is not in the continuous part of its
line, and is not at the end of the continuous part, is the trivial module, which is of
course unitary.                                                                     
