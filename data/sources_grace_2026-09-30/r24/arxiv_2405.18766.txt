                                              A COMBINATORIAL INTERPRETATION OF THE BERNSTEIN DEGREE
                                                        OF UNITARY HIGHEST WEIGHT MODULES

                                                                        WILLIAM Q. ERICKSON AND MARKUS HUNZIKER


                                                   Abstract. The Bernstein degree (Deg) is a fundamental invariant of admissible representations
                                                   of a real reductive Lie group GR . Our main result concerns the classical dual pairs (GR , HR (k)),
                                                   namely (U(p, q), U(k)), (Mp(2n, R), O(k)), and (O∗ (2n), Sp(k)), where k is any positive integer.




arXiv:2405.18766v3 [math.CO] 18 Mar 2026
                                                   In this setting, via Howe duality, each irreducible representation σ of HR (k) corresponds to a
                                                   unitary highest weight module Lλ(σ) for GR . A landmark result of Nishiyama–Ochiai–Taniguchi
                                                   (2001) expressed Deg Lλ(σ) as a product of two quantities: the dimension of σ and the degree of
                                                   the associated variety. However, this result was limited to a specific range of the parameter k
                                                   (namely k ≤ r, the real rank of GR ). The present paper resolves this limitation by introducing, for
                                                   all k, the combinatorial interpretation Deg Lλ(σ) = #(Qk (σ) × Pk ), where Qk (σ) is a certain set of
                                                   semistandard tableaux and Pk is a set of plane partitions. (The result remains partly conjectural
                                                   in the Mp(2n, R) case.) Beyond the dual pair setting, we generalize the set Pk to all groups GR
                                                   of Hermitian type, and we exhibit analogues of the Nishiyama–Ochiai–Taniguchi result for certain
                                                   families of unitary highest weight modules of E6 and E7 .

1.3. Main result of this paper. The Bernstein degree formula (1.7) is limited to the range
k ≤ r. In this paper, we generalize (1.7) by introducing a uniform combinatorial interpretation
that is valid for all k. In particular, we replace dim Uσ in (1.7) by the cardinality of a certain set
Qk (σ) of semistandard tableaux, and we replace deg Ok by the cardinality of a set Pk of plane
partitions. (See Definitions 3.1 and 4.1.) Our main result is the following theorem.
Theorem 1.1. Let GR be one of the groups in the dual pair setting (1.4), where k is any positive
integer. (In the GR = Mp(2n, R) case, the result remains conjectural within a certain range of
k-values; see details in Theorem 6.2.) For all σ ∈ H(k),

of general linear groups (the group K in our setting). In this way, our construction of Qk (σ) sheds
light on the mysterious gap r < k < s left open in (1.7).
   The cardinality of Pk equals the degree of the orbit closure Omin{k,r} in (1.6), and the plane
partitions in Pk are bounded by k and contained inside a certain diagram Dk (see Definition 4.1).
In the dual pair setting, Omin{k,r} is a determinantal variety inside a space of generic, symmetric,
or skew-symmetric matrices (7.7); consequently, this fact #Pk = deg Omin{k,r} is likely known to
experts, due to the extensive work in the 1990s on determinantal rings and lattice paths. (The
bijection Θ in Definition 4.4 connects the plane partition perspective with that of lattice paths.)
It is worth mentioning that the classical plane partition formulas for #Pk (see Proposition 4.2)
are much simpler than the original degree formulas due to Giambelli–Thom–Porteous [18, 22, 34].
Within the vast literature concerning plane partitions, we highlight Proctor’s work [35] relating
various families of bounded plane partitions to weight bases for representations of classical Lie
groups.

1.4. Method of proof. Via Howe duality (see Section 2), the unitary highest weight modules
Lλ(σ) can be viewed as modules of covariants Mσ (see (2.4) below) for the complex classical groups
H(k) from (1.4). The identification Lλ(σ) ∼
                                          = Mσ , as graded modules over the ring C[p+ ], follows

from viewing Lλ(σ) as the Uσ -isotypic multiplicity space in the Howe duality decomposition (2.6).
In our previous work [14, Thm. 3.2], we introduced a combinatorial model for Mσ by organizing
Jackson’s [25] linear basis of standard monomials into a Stanley decomposition (see Definition 5.3).
In particular, we expressed Mσ as a direct sum of Stanley spaces indexed by a set Jk (σ) consisting
of combinatorial objects we call jellyfish (see Definition 5.6). Very roughly speaking, a jellyfish
is an ordered pair (T, F) where T is a semistandard tableau of shape σ, and F is a union of k
nonintersecting lattice paths whose endpoints are compatible (in a technical sense) with the initial
column of T . Section 5 in the present paper is a self-contained summary of our results from [14].

   The two invariants in the opening paragraph, namely the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension Dim X
and the Bernstein degree Deg X, have analogues in the setting of graded modules: these are the
Krull dimension Kdim M and the multiplicity e(M ), respectively. Thus, in translating between
this paper and our previous paper [14], we have Deg Lλ(σ) = e(Mσ ). In turn, it is a general fact
(see Lemma 5.4) that for any Stanley decomposition of a graded module M , the multiplicity e(M )
equals the number of Stanley spaces of maximum Krull dimension. Thus by identifying the subset
Jbk (σ) of jellyfish with maximum Krull dimension, we obtain the following (see Lemma 5.7):

                                   Deg Lλ(σ) = e(Mσ ) = #Jbk (σ).

The heart of the argument, leading immediately to Theorem 1.1, occurs in Lemma 6.1, where we
establish a bijection

(1.9)                             id × Θ : Qk (σ) × Pk −→ Jbk (σ),

where Θ takes a plane partition in Pk to its corresponding union of lattice paths. In this way, upon
restriction to the k ≤ r case, we produce a new and very different proof of the Nishiyama–Ochiai–
Taniguchi result (1.7), in addition to extending it to arbitrary values of k.

   We mentioned above that in the case GR = Mp(2n, R), Theorem 1.1 remains partially conjectural,
namely in the range r < k < s. (See Conjecture 6.3, which we have verified extensively by
computer.) In the U(p, q) and O∗ (2n) cases, our construction of the jellyfish in [14] was guided
by the standard monomial results in Jackson [25], whereas the Mp(2n, R) case (being considerably
more delicate) fell outside the scope of Jackson’s methods. Hence in the Mp(2n, R) case, our proof
of Theorem 1.1 for k ≤ r and k ≥ s appeals to (1.7)–(1.8).

1.5. Beyond the dual pair setting. The ultimate goal of this research program is to generalize
Theorem 1.1 uniformly outside the dual pair setting. This goal requires finding analogues for the
sets Qk (σ) and Pk , for all groups GR of Hermitian type (including the exceptional groups). In this
paper, we take the first step toward this goal by generalizing the set Pk as follows.

   Recall that in the dual pair setting detailed above (see also Definition 4.1), we defined Pk as the
set of plane partitions bounded by k and contained in a certain diagram Dk (either a rectangle or a

4.1. Plane partitions and the set Pk . We use the term diagram to describe any edge-connected
union D of unit squares (boxes), not necessarily the Young diagram of a partition. In particular, the
following diagrams play an important role. A p × q rectangle is the Young diagram with shape (q p ).
Following Proctor [35], we define an n-staircase to be the Young diagram with shape (n, . . . , 3, 2, 1),
and a shifted n-staircase to be the horizontal reflection of an n-staircase.
   Let k be a positive integer. In this paper, a plane partition bounded by k, and contained in a
diagram D, is a filling of D with entries taken from [0, k], such that the entries weakly increase from
left to right, and from bottom to top. NB: This means that our two-dimensional pictures of plane
partitions are 90-degree clockwise rotations of the usual convention in the literature. Nonetheless,
when using the typical three-dimensional depiction of a plane partition — where an entry t is
represented by a stack of t unit cubes — this difference in convention vanishes. See Figure 1 for an
example of the plane partitions in the following definition.

                             7. Open problems and observations
  The main result in this paper leads naturally to the following problems.
Problem 7.1. Prove Conjecture 6.3.

   From our perspective, the ideal way to solve Problem 7.1 would be to extend Lemma 5.5 to the
GR = Mp(2n, R) case, by defining analogues of jellyfish for Mp(2n, R). Note that Conjecture 6.3
at least provides a candidate for the subset of maximal jellyfish, namely Jbk (σ) = Qk (σ) × Θ(Pk ).
We hope that this may ultimately suggest the correct way to define the full set Jk (σ) of jellyfish,
which (upon proof) would then lead to the solution of Problem 7.1.
Problem 7.2. Extend Theorem 6.2 to those unitary highest weight modules of O∗ (2n) arising
outside the dual pair setting.

   Unlike the groups U(p, q) and Mp(2n, R), the group O∗ (2n) admits unitary highest weight rep-
resentations arising outside the dual pair setting; that is, certain unitary highest weight modules
are not of the form Lλ(σ) for some σ ∈ H(k).
                                          b      For details, see [7, pp. 48–49]. Without the group
H(k) in the picture, Problem 7.2 amounts to determining a set of combinatorial objects, whether
tableaux or otherwise, to play the role of Qk (σ) in Theorem 6.2.

                                                 27
   Solving Problems 7.1–7.2 would establish a uniform combinatorial interpretation of the Bernstein
degree of all unitary highest weight modules for the groups U(p, q), Mp(2n, R), and O∗ (2n). The
ultimate goal of the program is to extend such an interpretation beyond those groups:
Problem 7.3. Extend Theorem 6.2 uniformly to arbitrary unitary highest weight modules (in-
cluding those of the exceptional groups).

of both Qk (σ) and Pk in a uniform manner for Hermitian symmetric pairs of all types. As a first
step toward solving Problem 7.3, we now give a uniform generalization of the diagram Dk that
yields an analogous set Pk for all groups of Hermitian type; see Proposition 7.6 below.
