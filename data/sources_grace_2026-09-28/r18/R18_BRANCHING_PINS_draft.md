## 0. Families and conventions (stated before any formula)

**The group being restricted.** It is G = SO(2,4), or its universal cover, which is the Lorentzian conformal group of 4D Minkowski space and of dS4. The papers write it as SO(2, d+1) with d = 3.

**The representation being restricted.** R_Δ is the scalar positive-energy (lowest-weight) unitary irreducible representation (UIR). It is a unitary highest-weight module of scalar type in Kobayashi's language, with a one-dimensional minimal K-type.
- In PSS convention, Δ is the eigenvalue of H = −iL_{0,d+2} on the primary state (F.6).
- Unitarity requires Δ ≥ (d−1)/2 = 1 (F, line 5139).
- Δ = 1 is the free massless scalar. This is the Kobayashi–Ørsted representation ϖ^{4,2} (positive-energy half).
- **Derived, not pinned (PIN OWED):** R_Δ is a (relative) holomorphic discrete series of the universal cover exactly when Δ > 3. I got this by evaluating Kobayashi's criterion (8.3.2) (math/0607002:2456) with ρ = (2,1,0) for D3 and Δ(p+) = {e1 ± e2, e1 ± e3}. On that reading, Δ = 7/2, 9/2, … are holomorphic discrete series, and Δ = 5/2 lies in the continuous part (1, 3] of the Wallach set, where the representation is unitary but not discrete series.

**The two subgroups.**
- dS subgroup H = SO(1,4). PSS define it as "an SO(1, d + 1) subgroup, that is generated by all LAB with 0 ≤ A, B ≤ d + 1" (2301.04146:5109). With η = diag(−1, 1, …, 1, −1), this is the stabilizer of the timelike vector e_{d+2}.
- AdS subgroup H' = SO(3,2), the stabilizer of a spacelike vector.

**Representations of SO(1, d+1), PSS convention** (2301.04146:398–411):
- P_{Δ,s} is the principal series, with Δ ∈ d/2 + iℝ, so Δ = 3/2 + iλ for dS4.
- C_{Δ,s} is the complementary series: "0 < ∆ < d when s = 0".
- The Casimir is "∆(d − ∆) − s(d + s − 2)". For a spin-0 principal series this is 9/4 + λ².
- Here Δ is an SO(1,4) label, which is a different quantity from the SO(2,4) Δ of R_Δ. **d = 3 is the boundary dimension of dS4.**

**Kobayashi–Ørsted (KØ) convention** (math/0111085:~380–405):
- X(p,q) = {|x|² − |y|² = 1}, with ϖ^{p,q} realized on R^{p−1,q−1}.
- For 4D Minkowski, take (p,q) = (4,2), so the two time directions sit in the q-block. Then O(p, q−1) × O(1) = O(4,1) × O(1) is the dS stabilizer.
- π^{p,q}_{+,λ} is a "discrete series representation on hyperboloids X(p,q) for λ > 0 or their analytic continuation for λ ≤ 0" (Intro).
- The Laplacian eigenvalue is −λ² + (p+q−2)²/4 (5.2.3).
- Caution: KØ Part I's physics gloss (math/0111083:137–138) calls O(2,q−1) "De Sitter" and O(1,q) "anti De Sitter". That is opposite to the modern physics usage used in this file. Go by the group, not the label.

## 1. The dS branching: what the sources say

**P1. Only spin-0 principal or complementary series can occur. No exceptional or discrete series.** (2301.04146, Section 5.4)
> "the absence of two-row representations of SO(d+1) implies that the only possible SO(1, d+1) structure in R∆ are the spinless principal series, complementary series, and the type I exceptional series Vs,0." (:3472–3474)
>
> "Altogether, R∆ does not contain any SO(1, d + 1) representation belonging to the type I exceptional series, and hence the only possible SO(1, d + 1) species is spinless principal or complementary series representation." (:3512–3514)

Status: this is an argument in the text (a recurrence, eq. (5.31)), not a numbered theorem.

**P2. The branching rule, eqs. (5.34) and (5.36) [V2].**
- For (d−1)/2 < Δ < d/2, the rule is R_Δ|_{SO(1,d+1)} = ∫_0^∞ dλ P_{d/2+iλ} ⊕ C_{d−Δ}.
- For Δ > d/2 = 3/2, it is the direct integral over spin-0 principal series only.
- Quote: "which suggests ∆c = d/2 and the existence of a single complementary series representation C_{d−∆} in R∆ when (d−1)/2 < ∆ < d/2" (:3571–3572).
- Only a **relative** density between R_Δ and R_{Δ'} is finite: K_rel(λ) = (1/2π) Σ_± [ψ(Δ' − d/2 ± iλ) − ψ(Δ − d/2 ± iλ)] (5.34).
- Table 1.4 [V1] states the result, with footnote 7: "A complementary series is present if and only if ∆̃ < d/2" (:384).
- Status: this is **character analysis plus numerics, not a proof**. The derivation is "Assuming the absence of complementary series in R∆ when ∆ is larger than some critical value ∆c, which will be confirmed by numerics" (:3517–3518).

**P3. The numerical confirmation is for dS4 specifically.** (Fig. 5.3, :3635–3637 and :3668–3671)
> "when 1 < ∆ ≲ 3/2, the smallest eigenvalue of Q becomes smaller than 9/4, lying on the line Q = ∆(3 − ∆) which is equal to the SO(1, 4) Casimir of the complementary series representations C3−∆, and when ∆ > 3/2 all the eigenvalues are larger than 9/4."

Fig. 5.4 checks K_rel at Δ = 4.5 against Δ' = 1.7 (:3727): "confirms that ρrel matches Krel".

**P4. An independent route: the explicit Källén–Lehmann density of a CFT scalar in dS_{d+1}.** (2306.00090, eqs. (5.47)–(5.52) [V4])
- The density is ρ^{P,0}_O(λ) = c_O 2^{1+d−2Δ} π^{(d−1)/2} Γ(Δ − d/2 ± iλ) λ sinh(πλ) / [Γ(Δ) Γ(Δ − (d−1)/2)].
- Quote: "complementary series is also absent when ∆ > d/2" (:3469).
- Quote: "We numerically verified that the Källén–Lehmann integral over the principal series of this spectral density fully reproduces the two-point function, if ∆ > d/2" (:3489).
- Below d/2, the poles λ = ±i(Δ − d/2) (5.48) cross the contour and give the C_{d−Δ} term (5.50)–(5.51).
- At Δ = (d−1)/2, ρ^P = 0, and only the conformally coupled scalar remains (5.52), at :3529.
- Caveat: this is the spectral weight of the states O(Y)|Ω⟩, not a statement of multiplicity.

**P5. Multiplicity-free.** (math/0607002, Theorem A, :286–288)
> "If π is an irreducible unitary highest weight representation of scalar type of G, then the restriction π|H is multiplicity-free."

This holds for any symmetric pair (G, H) with G of Hermitian type. so(2,4) = so(m,2) with m = 4 is Hermitian (:196). So each P_{3/2+iλ} occurs with multiplicity at most 1. The PSS "density" K(λ) is a character or Casimir-spectrum density and should not be read as a multiplicity. (That reading is my interpretation, flagged.)

**P6. The dS pair is anti-holomorphic. For holomorphic discrete series the restriction is purely continuous.**
- Table 3.4.2 (anti-holomorphic type) lists "so(2, n) | so(1, p) + so(1, n − p)" (math/0607002:1099; [V7]). With n = 4 and p = 4, this is so(1,4). Directly: the reflection fixing so(1,4) sends the SO(2) centre generator Z to −Z.
- Remark 5.2.2 (:1394–1398): "the restriction π|H is not discretely decomposable if (G, H) is of anti-holomorphic type and if π is a holomorphic discrete series representation of G ([38, Theorem 5.3])". Reference [38] is secondary here; its Theorem 5.3 is not opened, so PIN OWED.
- Sec. 1.8, item 2-b (:562–564): "the restriction π|H for a symmetric pair (G, H) of non-holomorphic type is multiplicity-free and is decomposed into only continuous spectra if π is a holomorphic discrete series of scalar type. This case was studied by G. Ólafsson–B. Ørsted ([69])." Reference [69] is not opened, so PIN OWED.

**P7. The pair (so(4,2), so(4,1)) is not excluded from discrete decomposability.** (1202.5743)
- Theorem 5.2 (:717–728): some infinite-dimensional irreducible module is discretely decomposable if and only if σβ ≠ −β, which is equivalent to (a) Cartan, (b) holomorphic type, or (c) Table 1.
- Table 1 row "so(m,n) | so(m,k) ⊕ so(n−k) (m > 1)" (:745; [V5]), with m = 4, n = 2, k = 1, gives exactly so(4,1). The A_q(λ) column is circled.
- **This corrects the brief's prior.** The pair does admit discretely decomposable modules: some Zuckerman A_q(λ), and every minimal-GK-dimension module by Theorem 4.10 (:638–642).
- The scalar highest-weight R_Δ with Δ > 1 has larger associated variety, so Theorem 4.10 does not apply to it. P6 is the relevant statement.

**P8. The endpoint Δ = 1 (minimal rep) is discrete. A consistency check.**
- KØ II, Theorem 4.2 (math/0111085:233–239): ϖ^{p,q} is discretely decomposable under O(p',q') × O(p'',q'') if and only if "min(p′ , q′ , p′′ , q′′ ) = 0". For (4,1) × (0,1), the minimum is 0, so the restriction is discrete.
- Theorem 7.1 (7.1.1) [V6], with q'' = 1, gives only l = 0, 1: ϖ^{4,2}|_{O(4,1)×O(1)} ≅ π^{4,1}_{+,−1/2} ⊠ 1 ⊕ π^{4,1}_{+,1/2} ⊠ sgn. This is eq. (7.2.1), :839, which the source states under "q ≥ 3" (:826). Here Theorem 7.1 itself (p+q even) is what applies.
- Both summands have Laplacian eigenvalue −1/4 + 9/4 = 2, which is the conformally coupled dS4 mass m²H⁻² = 2 (5.2.3).
- **Inference:** these are the SO_0(4,1) complementary series with Casimir 2, that is, SO(1,4) label Δ_dS ∈ {1, 2}. This matches PSS C_{d−Δ} = C_2 at Δ = 1 and LPSS (5.52). ϖ^{4,2} is positive-energy plus negative-energy (math/0111083:865–869), so the sign/energy bookkeeping between the two KØ summands and R_1 alone is not pinned.

## 2. Answer for Δ = 5/2 + k (k = 0, 1, 2, …), dS4, d = 3

1. **Content.** R_{5/2+k}|_{SO_0(1,4)} is a direct integral over the spin-0 principal series P_{3/2+iλ}, λ ≥ 0, only.
   - There is no complementary series: this needs Δ < 3/2 (P2, P3, P4).
   - There is no exceptional series V_{p,0} (P1).
   - There are no discrete series, and no spin s ≥ 1 principal series (P1: only single-row SO(4) types).
   - The measure is absolutely continuous and the multiplicity is at most 1 (P5).
2. **Rigour by k.**
   - k ≥ 1 (Δ ≥ 7/2): R_Δ is a holomorphic discrete series (derived threshold Δ > 3). Kobayashi Remark 5.2.2 and Sec. 1.8 item 2-b then give "only continuous spectra" and multiplicity-free on theorem-level footing, subject to the secondary refs [38] and [69].
   - k = 0 (Δ = 5/2): Theorem A still gives multiplicity-free. The absence of discrete pieces rests on PSS character analysis plus numerics and on LPSS eq. (5.47) with numerics. I have not seen a numbered theorem for this case.
3. **Explicit spectral density** (my evaluation of LPSS (5.47) at d = 3; checked to about 1e-16 in `r18_check.py`):

   ρ(λ) = c_O · 2^{−1−2k} π² λ² ∏_{j=1}^{k}(j² + λ²) / [Γ(5/2+k) Γ(3/2+k)].

   - This is a positive polynomial in λ.
   - For k = 0, it is (4π/3) c_O λ².
   - The poles λ = ±i(1 + k + n), n ∈ ℕ, never cross the real axis, so there is no complementary pole contribution.
   - The relative density against another R_{Δ'} is PSS (5.34) with Δ − 3/2 = 1 + k.
4. **Why the naive formula fails.**
   - m² ∝ Δ(3−Δ) is the SO(1,4) Casimir of C_{3−Δ}. It is realized as a discrete component only for 1 < Δ < 3/2 (P3). At Δ = 1 it is the whole restriction (P8).
   - For Δ = 5/2 it would predict C_{5/2} with Casimir 5/4. That representation is absent.
   - For Δ ≥ 7/2 it gives a negative Casimir, which matches no unitary piece.
   - Retained check (`r18_check_output.txt`, truncated (5.39) at N = 4000): the lowest Casimir eigenvalues for Δ = 2.5, 3.5, 4.5, 5.5 are 2.356, 2.381, 2.398, 2.410. All are above 9/4 and approach it as N grows, which is the continuum edge. At Δ = 1.2, the lowest eigenvalue is 2.1607, matching Δ(3−Δ) = 2.16. At Δ = 1.4, it is 2.2506 against a predicted 2.24, not converged at this N, consistent with PSS "≲ 3/2".

## 3. AdS comparison: SO(3,2)

- The pair (so(2,4), so(2,3)) is of holomorphic type: Table 3.4.1, "so(2, n) | so(2, p) + so(n − p)", math/0607002:1073.
- Fact 5.1(1) (:1350–1356): π|_H "splits into a discrete Hilbert sum of irreducible unitary highest weight representations of H … where the multiplicity mπ(µ) is finite".
- Theorem 8.10 (:2947–2949), for (so(n,2), so(n−1,2)): "any irreducible unitary highest weight representation of G decomposes discretely into a multiplicity-free sum of irreducible unitary highest weight representations of H". The source attributes this to Jakobsen–Vergne [31, Cor. 3.1] (secondary, not opened).
- So the AdS restriction is discrete, multiplicity-free, and consists of positive-energy reps of SO(3,2). The dS restriction is purely continuous (for Δ > 3/2).
- **Explicit list: INFERENCE, not pinned.** R_Δ|_{SO(3,2)} = ⊕_{n≥0} D^{SO(3,2)}(Δ+n, 0). This is consistent with Theorem 8.3 (8.3.3) having one strongly orthogonal root (ℝ-rank of g^{τθ} = 1). Characters check: q^Δ/(1−q)^4 = Σ_n q^{Δ+n}/(1−q)^3. This is also consistent with the boundary-operator poles at Δ + ℕ in LPSS (:3529–3531). Pinning the explicit formula needs Jakobsen–Vergne.
