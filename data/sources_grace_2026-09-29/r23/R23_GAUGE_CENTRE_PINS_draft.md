## 1. Pin 1 -- Tong, "Line Operators in the Standard Model", arXiv:1705.01853v2 (JHEP 07 (2017) 104 as given by the requester; not re-pinned)

File: `tong_1705.01853.pdf` / `.txt`. **No equation number is attached to the Z6 generator or to the charge table.** The nearest numbered equation, (3.1), is the Dirac quantisation condition, not the Z6 element. Cite as "Section 3, p. 7" and "Section 3, p. 9".

1a. Introduction, p. 1 (`tong_1705.01853.txt:55-57`):
> "As we review below, the matter content of the Standard Model is invariant under a suitably chosen Z6 subgroup of G̃. For this reason, it is sometimes stated that one should take the gauge group to include the quotient Γ = Z6 ."

1b. Section 3, p. 7, the explicit element (`tong_1705.01853.txt:288-293`; visually [V1]):
> "where Γ ⊆ Z6 . The quotient group Γ lies in the centres of SU(2) and SU(3), combined with a suitable U(1)Ỹ rotation. The quotient Γ = Z6 is generated by
>  ξ = e^{2πiq/6} ⊗ η ⊗ ω
> where η ∈ Z(SU(2)) obeys η² = 1 and ω ∈ Z(SU(3)) obeys ω³ = 1 and q is the U(1)Ỹ charge. The quotient Γ = Z3 is generated by ξ² and the quotient Γ = Z2 is generated by ξ³."

1c. Section 3, p. 9, representations and the trivial-action condition (`tong_1705.01853.txt:420-430`; visually [V2]):
> "Leptons: lL : (2, 1)−3 ... eR : (1, 1)−6 ... Quarks: qL : (2, 3)+1 ... uR : (1, 3)+4 ... dR : (1, 3)−2"
> "The Higgs also obeys the relationship between electric charges, sitting in the representation (2, 1)3 ⇒ (z2e , z3e )q = (1, 0)3 . The fact that all Standard Model fields satisfy q = 3z2e − 2z3e mod 6 is, of course, equivalent to the statement made in the introduction that the Standard Model gauge group is consistent with U(1) × SU(2) × SU(3)/Z6 ."

1d. Section 3.2, p. 12 (`tong_1705.01853.txt:580-581`):
> "The Higgs field H lies in the (2, 1)3 representation and condenses, breaking U(1)Ỹ × SU(2) → U(1)em of electromagnetism."

**DERIVED from 1b + 1c** (instrument: `z6check.py`, output `z6check_output.txt`, exact rational arithmetic, η = -1, ω = e^{2πi/3}):
- ξ^k acts trivially on every field in the table (l_L, e_R, q_L, u_R, d_R, H, nu_R) for k = 0..5.
- In particular ξ³ = e^{iπq} ⊗ (-1) ⊗ 1 is trivial on all fields. Equivalently, on every SM field the **SU(2) centre element -1** and the **hypercharge rotation e^{iπ Ytilde} = e^{2πi·3Y}** act the same way: -1 on l_L, q_L, H (the doublets) and +1 on e_R, u_R, d_R, nu_R (the singlets). That is the precise form of "a hypercharge rotation at suitable θ reproduces the signs": θ = 3 in e^{2πiYθ} (Y_H = 1/2 convention), i.e. e^{6πiY}.
- The smaller rotation e^{2πiY} (θ = 1) also gives -1 on H (and on l_L), but gives phases e^{2πi/6}, e^{2πi·2/3}, e^{2πi·2/3} on q_L, u_R, d_R. So it is not a pure sign pattern on quarks.
- That -1 ∈ SU(2) acts as -1 on every doublet and +1 on singlets follows from the definition of the fundamental and trivial representations. Tong labels this with z2^e = 1 and 0 (1c). No separate pin is needed.

## 2. Pin 2 -- EW vacuum manifold is S³ (connected) and walls need π0(M) ≠ 1

2a. Vacuum manifold = S³. Achucarro and Vachaspati, "Semilocal and Electroweak Strings", arXiv:hep-ph/9904229v2 (journal reference not pinned), Section 3.1 "The model", eq. (54), p. 19 (`hep-ph_9904229.txt:1055-1060`; visually [V5]):
> "Like in the GSW model, the vacuum manifold is the three sphere
>  V = {Φ ∈ C² | Φ†Φ = η²/2} ≅ S³ ,   (54)
> which is simply connected, so there are no topological string solutions."

(Context: eq. (54) is written for the semilocal model, and the sentence asserts the same manifold for the GSW model.)

2b. Same, stated directly for the SM. Battye-Brawn-Pilaftsis, "Vacuum Topology of the Two Higgs Doublet Model", arXiv:1106.3482v3 (journal reference not pinned), end of Section 2.3 "The Vacuum Manifold", p. 19 (PDF p. 22), paragraph after eq. (2.52) (`../r22/battye_brawn_pilaftsis_1106.3482.txt:1019-1020`; visually [V7]):
> "Finally, we should note that the breaking of the SM gauge group SU(2)L ⊗ U(1)Y to U(1)em gives rise to a vacuum manifold, which is homeomorphic to S 3 ."

2c. Same, Hindmarsh-Kibble, "Cosmic strings", arXiv:hep-ph/9411342v1 (title page: "Submitted to: Rep. Prog. Phys."; volume/page not pinned), Section 2.9 (`hindmarsh_kibble_hep-ph9411342.txt:1389-1391`):
> "As mentioned above, the electroweak theory does not possess any topologically stable string solutions, because its gauge orbit space is isomorphic to S 3 , which is simply connected."

2d. The wall criterion is π0. Vachaspati, "Lectures on Cosmic Topological Defects", arXiv:hep-ph/0101270v2, Section 2.1 "Topology: π0" (`hep-ph_0101270.txt:201-202, 210-213`):
> "Domain walls occur when the vacuum manifold has two or more disconnected components."
> "In the case when the vacuum manifold has disconnected components, π0 (G/H) is non-trivial since there are points (zero dimensional spheres) that lie in different components that cannot be continuously deformed into one another. Therefore domain walls occur whenever π0 (G/H) is non-trivial."

2e. The "only if" direction. Achucarro-Vachaspati, Section 1 "Introduction" (before 1.1), p. 5-6 (`hep-ph_9904229.txt:239`):
> "Thus, one can have topological domain walls only if π0 (V) ≠ 1, topological strings only if π1 (V) ≠ 1 and topological monopoles only if π2 (V) ≠ 1."
(pdftotext renders ≠ as "6=". It is ≠ in the PDF.)

2f. Hindmarsh-Kibble, Section 1.3 "Defect formation", after eq. (1.4) (`hindmarsh_kibble_hep-ph9411342.txt:297-298`):
> "It is the disconnected nature of the set of equilibrium states of the theory which allows domain walls to exist."

**DERIVED (standard topology, not pinned to a page):** S³ is path-connected, so π0(S³) = 1. "Simply connected" in 2a and 2c already includes path-connected by the standard definition. With 2e: **no topological domain walls from SU(2)_L x U(1)_Y -> U(1)_em.**

**CAVEATS, pinned (a physics team should see these):**
- Embedded walls exist. Achucarro-Vachaspati, Section 5.3 "Embedded defects and W-strings" (`hep-ph_9904229.txt:2381`): "It is straightforward to embed domain walls in the GSW model." These are embedded walls, not topological ones and not protected. The claim must be stated as "no **topological** (stable) domain walls".
- The topology-to-defect map is not an equivalence. A-V (`hep-ph_9904229.txt:250-252`): "note that a trivial topology of the vacuum manifold does not imply the non-existence of stable defects." Their example is semilocal strings with π1 = 1 that are stable for β < 1. The honest form is "π0(M) = 1 ⇒ no topological walls".
- Vachaspati-Kibble-Vilenkin-Shellard books: NOT on disk (paywalled). Not pinned.

## 3. Pin 3 -- the sign flip is a U(1)_Y gauge move when only one doublet has a VEV

No paper found on disk or in one search that states the one-doublet sentence verbatim ("in the one-doublet SM, H -> -H is a gauge transformation"). A candidate I downloaded (Haber-O'Neil arXiv:1011.6188) had no such sentence and was deleted. The closest primary statement is in BBP, which is on disk.

3a. BBP Section 3.1.2 "Z2 Topology", eq. (3.21), p. 27 (PDF p. 30) (`../r22/battye_brawn_pilaftsis_1106.3482.txt:1386-1406`; visually [V6]): the chart shows (V1, V2) <-U(1)_Y-> (-V1, -V2), with Z2 acting vertically (V1, V2) <-> (V1, -V2). **The overall sign flip of the Higgs VEV(s) is a U(1)_Y gauge move. Only the relative sign is the genuine Z2.**

3b. Same page (`../r22/battye_brawn_pilaftsis_1106.3482.txt:1407-1414`):
> "Observe that for Z2 -symmetric 2HDM scenarios with two non-zero VEVs v1,2 ≠ 0, we cannot move via a U(1)Y transformation from one vacuum configuration, e.g. (v1 , v2 ), to its Z2 -symmetric one, i.e. (v1 , −v2 ) or (−v1 , v2 ). However, if v1 or v2 were zero, then such a transformation would be possible, and the discrete vacua will be connected via a continuous U(1)Y gauge transformation. In the latter case, there are no topological defects, such as domain walls or superconducting condensates similar to the ones discussed by Hodges [51], ..."

(superscripts 0 on v dropped by pdftotext; see the PNG.)

Scope: this is the 2HDM with one VEV zero, which is the one-condensing-doublet case. It is the closest explicit literature statement: the vacua ±v of a single condensing doublet are joined by a continuous U(1)_Y gauge path, so no walls. It is **not** a verbatim "SM one-doublet" sentence.

## 4. Statement for the team (what the pins support, and what they do not)

Supported:
1. In G~ = SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1)_Y with Y_H = 1/2, the SU(2) centre element -1 acts as -1 on H, l_L, q_L and +1 on e_R, u_R, d_R, nu_R. On every SM field it coincides with the U(1)_Y rotation e^{6πiY} (Tong's ξ³ is trivial; pins 1b, 1c, plus z6check.py). The Z6 generated by ξ (pin 1b) acts trivially on all SM fields (pin 1c).
2. The two Higgs vacua ⟨H⟩ = +v and -v lie in one gauge orbit: -1 ∈ SU(2), or e^{2πiY} on H alone. So they are the same point of the vacuum manifold, which is S³ and connected (pins 2a-2c). With π0(M) = 1 there are no topological domain walls (pins 2d-2f). BBP state the same conclusion for a single condensing doublet (pin 3b).

NOT supported / must not be claimed:
- "H -> -H with all fermions held fixed is a gauge transformation." It is not. Any gauge element trivial on q_L and d_R is trivial on H, because the Yukawa q̄_L H d_R is gauge invariant. That map is not a symmetry of the Yukawa terms either. The gauge element that flips H also flips l_L and q_L (SU(2) centre), or rotates quark phases (e^{2πiY}). DERIVED; no source pinned for this sentence.
- "No domain walls of any kind." Only topological walls are excluded (A-V caveat, Section 2).
- Any equation number for Tong's Z6 generator. There is none.
