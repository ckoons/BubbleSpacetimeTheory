                                                   Abstract. Let (G, K) be an irreducible Hermitian symmetric pair of non-
                                                   compact type with G = SU (p, q), and let λ be an integral weight such that
                                                   the simple highest weight module L(λ) is a Harish-Chandra (g, K)-module.
                                                   We give a combinatorial algorithm for the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of L(λ).
                                                   This enables us to prove that the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of L(λ) decreases
                                                   as the integer hλ + ρ, β ∨ i increases, where ρ is the half sum of positive roots and
                                                   β is the maximal noncompact root. Finally by the combinatorial algorithm,
                                                   we obtain a description of the associated variety of L(λ).

    In 1978, Joseph [11] found that the GK-dimension of a highest weight sl(n)-
module Lw := L(−wρ − ρ) with w ∈ W can be computed by Robinson-Schensted
correspondence. This result suggests a combinatorial algorithm for highest weight
(g, K)-modules in the case G = SU (p, q). It is known that GKdim L(λ) for any
weight λ can be reduced to Lw for some w ∈ W by using Jantzen’s translation
functors [9], but we fail to find a concrete result in published literatures. In the
first part of this paper, we will formulate an explicit algorithm for GKdim L(λ) for
g = sl(n) and any weight λ ∈ h∗ . A main tool that we use is Lusztig’s a-function
on the Weyl group.
    The a-function is defined in a combinatorial way using the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis
of the Hecke algebra; see [13] or §3.2. There is a formula connecting the a-function
and the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension

                          GKdim Lw = |Φ+ | − a(w) for w ∈ W,

which is formulated in [14]. Thus computations of Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions
can be ultimately reduced to that of Lusztig’s a-functions. In the case g = sl(n),
the Weyl group is isomorphic to the symmetric group Sn in n letters, and the
a-functions on Sn can be easily determined by using some known properties about
two-side cells. Our results can be stated as follows.

We have the decomposition g = p− + k + p+ under the adjoint action of KC . From
Vogan [19], we know that the associated variety of any highest weight (g, K)-module
L(λ) is the closure of some KC orbit in p+ ∼ = (g/(k + p+ ))∗ , and closures of these
KC orbits form a linear chain of varieties:
(6.1)                {0} = Ō0 ⊆ Ō1 ⊆ ... ⊆ Ōr−1 ⊆ Ōr = p+ ,

where C is a constant only depending on G.
   In the case G = SU (p, q), we have hρ, β ∨ i = n − 1 and C = 1, and hence

summarize, we have the following theorem about Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions and
associated varieties of a highest weight (g, K)-modules in the case G = SU (p, q).
Theorem 6.4. Let G = SU (p, q). Let L(λ) be a highest weight (g, K)-module.
   (i) If λ is an integral weight, then
        (a) P (λ) is a Young tableau with at most two columns;
        (b) GKdim L(λ) = m(n − m) where m is the number of entries in the
            second column of P (λ);
        (c) The associated variety of L(λ) is Ōm .
  (ii) If λ is not integral, then
        (a) P (λ) consists of two Young tableaux with single column;
        (b) GKdim L(λ) = pq;
        (c) The associated variety of L(λ) is Ōr , r = min{p, q}.
