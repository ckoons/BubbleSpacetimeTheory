                                             ON THE ANNIHILATOR VARIETY OF A HIGHEST WEIGHT
                                                        HARISH-CHANDRA MODULE

                                                                       ZHANQIANG BAI AND JING JIANG*


                                                   Abstract. Let G be a Hermitian type Lie group with maximal compact sub-
                                                   group K. Let L(λ) be a highest weight Harish-Chandra module of G with the
                                                   infinitesimal character λ. By using some combinatorial algorithm, we obtain a




arXiv:2408.07951v1 [math.RT] 15 Aug 2024
                                                   description of the annihilator variety of L(λ). As an application, when L(λ) is
                                                   unitarizable, we prove that the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of L(λ) only depends
                                                   on the value of z = (λ, β ∨ ), where β is the highest root.




                                                                                 1. Introduction
                                              Let G be a simple Lie group with complexified Lie algebra g and K be a maximal
                                           compact subgroup with complexified Lie algebra k such that G/K is a Hermitian
                                           symmetric space. We have a Cartan decomposition g = k ⊕ p and a decomposi-
                                           tion p = p+ ⊕ p− into nonzero irreducible K-subrepresentations. Then we have a
                                           triangular decomposition g = k ⊕ p+ ⊕ p− and q = k ⊕ p+ is a maximal parabolic
                                           subalgebra of g. Let h be a Cartan subalgebra of g. Denote by Φ the root system
                                           associated with (g, h), fix a positive system Φ+ , let Cλ−ρ be a finite-dimensional
                                           irreducible k-module with highest weight λ − ρ ∈ h∗ , where ρ is half the sum of
                                           positive roots. It can also be viewed as a q-module with trivial p+ -action. The
                                           corresponding generalized Verma module N (λ) is defined by
                                                                            N (λ) := U (g) ⊗U (q) Cλ−ρ .
                                           The simple quotient of N (λ) is denoted by L(λ), which is a highest weight module
                                           with highest weight λ − ρ. From [EHW83], we call L(λ) a highest weight Harish-
                                           Chandra module. The classification of unitarizable highest weight Harish-Chandra
                                           modules is given in [EHW83] and [Jak83] independently.
                                              For a U (g)-module M , let I(M ) = Ann(M ) be its annihilator ideal in U (g) and
                                           J(M ) be the corresponding graded ideal in S(g) = gr(U (g)). The zero set of J(M )
                                           in the dual vector space g∗ of g is called the annihilator variety of M , which is
                                           also called the associated variety of I(M ). We usually denote it by V (Ann(M )).
                                           The study of associated varieties of primitive ideals (or annihilator varieties of high-
                                           est weight modules) is a very important problem since it is closely related with
                                           many research fields, such as representations of Weyl groups, Kazhdan-Lusztig cells
                                           and representations of Lie groups, see for example [BB82; BV82; Bar+23; GSK21].
                                           Borho-Brylinski [BB82] proved that the associated variety of a primitive ideal I with
                                           a fixed regular integral infinitesimal character is the Zariski closure of the nilpotent
                                           orbit in g∗ attached to I, via the Springer correspondence. Joseph [Jos85] extended
                                           this result to a primitive ideal with a general infinitesimal character.

   Recently Bai-Ma-Wang [BMW23] found some simple algorithms to characterize
the nilpotent orbit appearing in the annihilator variety of a highest weight module
for all classical type Lie algebras by using the Robinson-Schensted algorithm. In
this paper, our first goal is to give the explicit description of V (Ann(L(λ))) for all
highest weight Harish-Chandra modules of classical types by using the partition
algorithm in [BMW23]. See Theorem 3.3, Theorem 4.1, Theorem 4.3, Theorem 5.1
and Theorem 5.3.
   The Gelfand-Kirillov (GK) dimension is an important invariant to measure the
size of an infinite-dimensional U (g)-module M , see [Jos78] and [Vog78]. From
Joseph [Jos78], we know that dim V (Ann(L(λ))) = 2GKdimL(λ). When there is
only one nilpotent orbit with the dimension 2GKdimL(λ), we can determine this
nilpotent orbit appearing in V (Ann(L(λ))) by the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of
L(λ). In [BXX23], Bai-Xiao-Xie computed the Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions of high-
est weight Harish-Chandra modules of exceptional types. Then we can determine
V (Ann(L(λ))) by the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of L(λ) since there is only one
nilpotent orbit with the dimension 2GKdimL(λ) for these cases. See Theorem 6.1
and Theorem 6.2. Except for the cases of SU (p, q), Sp(n, R) and SO ∗ (2n), our
characterization of V (Ann(L(λ))) is given in a function of z = (λ, β ∨ ).
   Our second goal of this paper is to prove that when L(λ) is a unitary highest weight
module with highest weight λ − ρ = λ0 + zξ, where z = (λ, β ∨ ), (λ0 + ρ, β) = 0, ξ is
orthogonal to the root system Φ(k) and (ξ, β ∨ ) = 1, then GKdimL(λ) is independent
of the selection of λ0 . In other words, GKdim L(λ) only depends on the value of
z = (λ, β ∨ ). This result was firstly proved in [BH15] by using previous results in
[Jos92]. In our paper, we use our characterization of V (Ann(L(λ))) to give a new
proof for this result. See Theorem 7.2.
   The paper is organized as follows. In §2, we give some necessary preliminaries
about Gelfand-Kirillov dimension, associated variety, annihilator variety and parti-
tion algorithm for highest weigh modules. In §3-6, we give the characterization of
the annihilator variety V (Ann(L(λ))) for a highest weight Harish-Chandra module
of type An−1 , type Bn and Cn , type Dn , type E6 and E7 respectively. In §7, we
prove that when L(λ) is a unitary highest weight module, then GKdim L(λ) only
depends on the value of z = (λ, β ∨ ).

2.3. Annihilator variety. For a U (g)-module M , let I(M ) = Ann(M ) be its anni-
hilator ideal in U (g) and J(M ) be the corresponding graded ideal in S(g) = gr(U (g)).
The zero set of J(M ) in the dual vector space g∗ is called the annihilator variety
of M , and we denote it by V (Ann(M )). Let G be a connected semisimple finite
dimensional complex algebraic group with Lie algebra g and W be the Weyl group
of g. We use Lw to denote the simple highest weight g-module of highest weight
−wρ − ρ with w ∈ W . We denote Iw = Ann(Lw ), then by [BB85] V (Iw ) = Ow is
irreducible, where Ow is a nilpotent coadjoint orbit in g∗ .

Proposition 2.11 ([Jos85]). Let g be a reductive Lie algebra and I be a primitive
ideal in U (g).Then V (I) is the closure of a single nilpotent coadjoint orbit OI in
g∗ . In particular, for a highest weight module L(λ), we have V (Ann(L(λ))) =
OAnn(L(λ)) .

  From [Jos78, Prop. 2.7] and [Vog78], we have dim V (Iw ) = 2GKdim(Lw ). In
general, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.12. Suppose L(λ) is a highest weight Harish-Chandra module with
V (L(λ)) = Ok(λ) for some 0 ≤ k(λ) ≤ r. Then dim OAnn(L(λ)) = 2 dim Ok(λ) =
2 dim V (L(λ)).

  Proposition 2.5 indicates that if L(λ) is a unitary highest weight module, then
GKdim L(λ) only depends on the value z = (λ, β ∨ ). The proof of Proposition 2.5
used some results from Joseph [Jos92]. In this section, we will prove this result by
another method.

Lemma 7.1 ([Bai+24, Lem. 5.4]). Suppose µ = (t1 , · · · , tn , s1 , · · · , sm ) is a (n, m)-
dominant sequence with tn ≤ s1 . Then applying R-S algorithm to µ, we can get
a Young tableau P (µ) with two columns, and the number of boxes in the second
column is precisely the largest integer k for which we have
                 tn−k+1 ≤ s1 , tn−k+2 ≤ s2 , · · · , tn−1 ≤ sk−1 , tn ≤ sk .

   Now we recall two root systems Q(λ0 ) and R(λ0 ) given in [EHW83]. Their con-
structions are as follows. Let Φc (λ0 ) = {α ∈ Φ(k)|(λ0 , α) = 0}. Consider the subroot
system Ψ1 of Φ, which is generated by ±β and Φc (λ0 ). Let Q(λ0 ) be the simple
component of Ψ1 which contains −β. If Φ has two root lengths and there exist short
roots α′ ∈ Φ(k) which are not orthogonal to Q(λ0 ) and satisfy (λ0 , α′∨ ) = 1, then
let Ψ2 be the root system generated by ±β, Φc (λ0 ) and all such α. Let R(λ0 ) be
the simple component of Ψ2 which contains −β. If Φ has only one root length or no
such α exists, we let R(λ0 ) = Q(λ0 ).

Theorem 7.2. When L(λ) is a unitary highest weight module and z = zk =
(ρ, β ∨ ) − kc, we will have V (L(λ)) = Ok(λ) , where k(λ) = r if k ≥ r and k(λ) = k if
0 ≤ k ≤ r − 1.

   When L(λ) is a unitary highest weight module, we will have V (L(λ)) = Ok(λ)
with k(λ) given in Proposition 2.9. From [EHW83], we can write λ − ρ = λ0 + zξ,
where ξ is the fundamental weight perpendicular to the compact roots such that
(ξ, β ∨ ) = 1 and λ0 = (λ1 , λ2 , · · · , λn ) with (λ0 + ρ, β ∨ ) = 0.
                                             16
  Now z = z(λ) = (λ, β ∨ ) = zk = (ρ, β ∨ ) − kc, we want to prove that k(λ) = r if
k ≥ r and k(λ) = k if 0 ≤ k ≤ r − 1.

  In the following, we will give the proof of our Theorem 7.2 in a case-by-case way.
The idea of our proof is uniform. For G = SU (p, q), Sp(n, R) and SO∗ (2n), we use
Lemma 7.1 to prove our result. For other cases, we use our characterizations of
annihilator varieties of L(λ) since they are given in a distribution of the value of
z = z(λ) = (λ, β ∨ ).
