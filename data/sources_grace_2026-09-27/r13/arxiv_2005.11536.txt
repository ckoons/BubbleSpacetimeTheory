                                                  Abstract. In this paper, we present a uniform formula of Lusztig’s
                                                  a-functions on classical Weyl groups. Then we obtain an efficient al-
                                                  gorithm for the Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions of simple highest weight
                                                  modules of classical Lie algebras, whose highest weight is not necessarily
                                                  regular or integral. To deal with type D, we prove an interesting prop-
                                                  erty about domino tableaux associated with Weyl group elements by
                                                  introducing an invariant, called the hollow tableau. As an application,
                                                  the associated varieties of all the simple highest weight Harish-Chandra
                                                  modules are explicitly determined, including the exceptional cases.

                                                                           1. Introduction
                                          1.1. GK dimensions and associated varieties. Let g be a complex
                                          semisimple Lie algebra and U (g) be its enveloping algebra. The Gelfand-
                                          Kirillov (GK) dimension is an essential invariant to measure the size of an
                                          infinite-dimensional U (g)-module M [GK66]. The associated variety of M ,
                                          whose dimension is equal to the GK dimension of M , is another invari-
                                          ant to distinguish U (g)-modules [Ber71]. These two invariants are closely
                                          related and play significant roles in representation theory, see for example
                                          [Jos80a, Jos80b, Jos81, Tra01, LM15].
                                             The computations of these two invariants are generally complicated. The
                                          case of simple highest weight modules for complex simple Lie algebras has at-
                                          tracted considerable amount of attention (e.g., [Jos84, BB85, Tan88, Mel93,
                                          McG00, Wil15]), as well as the case of Harish-Chandra (HC) modules for
                                          real reductive groups (e.g., [Vog78, Vog91, Gar93, AV21]). The first main
                                          result of this paper is an efficient algorithm for GK dimensions of highest
                                          weight modules for classical Lie algebras. A formula of GK dimensions of
                                          regular integral highest weight modules for type A was obtained by Joseph
                                          [Jos78]. Based on the theory of Lusztig’s a-function [Lus77, Lus79, Lus82,
                                          Lus84, Lus85a, Lus85b], Bai-Xie were able to extend Joseph’s result to sin-
                                          gular and nonintegral cases for type A [BX19]. More efforts are needed for
                                          the other classical types. In this paper, formulas of GK dimensions for sim-
                                          ple highest weight modules are explicitly presented in the most generality

   The second main result of this paper is a full description of the associated
varieties of all the highest weight HC modules. Such a result was expected
for a long time. In 1992, Joseph [Jos92] found a recursive relation for asso-
ciated varieties of unitary highest weight modules in a case-by-case fashion.
After that, the GK dimensions of holomorphic representations were obtained
for Hermitian symmetric groups in the dual pair settings by Tan-Zhu [TZ96].
Nishiyama-Ochiai-Taniguchi [NOT01] and Enright-Willenbring [EW04] in-
dependently gave characterizations of the GK dimensions and associated
varieties for unitary highest weight HC modules appearing in the dual pair
settings. In recent years, a uniform formula for the GK dimensions and
associated varieties of unitary highest weight HC modules was discovered
by Bai-Hunziker [BH15]. Almost at the same time, Barchini-Zierau [BZ17]
and Zierau [Zie18] computed the characteristic cycles of highest weight HC
modules with regular integral infinitesimal characters, which can give some
information of the corresponding associated varieties. In this paper, we de-
termine associated varieties of the highest weight HC modules for all the
Hermitian types (Theorems 6.2 and 7.1), where the highest weights are not
necessarily regular or integral and the modules are not necessarily unitary.
Note that the case of type A was already known in [BX19].

1.2. Lusztig’s a-functions. There is a beautiful formula by Lusztig [Lus85b]
connecting the GK dimensions and the a-functions. Using this formula, one
can translate the computation of GK dimensions of simple highest weight
modules to the computation of Lusztig’s a-functions on the integral Weyl
subgroups [BX19]. Thus, for our purposes we need to find a fine formula of
Lusztig’s a-functions first.
   Fix a Cartan subalgebra h of g. Let Φ be the root system of (g, h) and
W be its Weyl group. The function a : W → N first appeared in Lusztig’s
works on representations of finite groups of Lie type and Weyl groups [Lus77,
Lus79, Lus82, Lus84]. It is originally defined as a function on the set of
irreducible representations of Weyl groups. In the study of cells of affine
Weyl groups, Lusztig [Lus85a, Lus85b] introduced the a-functions on Weyl
groups defined in terms of Kazhdan-Lusztig basis of Hecke algebras. He
found many interesting properties about a-functions and cells. In fact, the a-
functions on the set of simple modules and on the Weyl group are compatible
[Lus03, Prop. 20.6].
   It is well known that there is an algorithm of the a-functions for Weyl
groups of type A, using Robinson-Schensted insertion. This gives rise to a
combinatorial algorithm of GK dimensions of simple highest weight modules
of sl(n, C) [BX19, Thm. 4.6]. From this, we can determine the GK dimen-
sions and the associated varieties of highest weight HC modules for type A
(see [BX19] for more details). This paper is a sequel of [BX19], and we will
give an answer for other classical types.

   Note that the Weyl groups of type B and C are the same. For Weyl
groups of types B and D, Lusztig gave a formula for the a-functions on the
set of irreducible representations of the Weyl groups [Lus77, Lus79, Lus84].
Since the irreducible representations are parametrized by Lusztig’s symbols
[Lus77], we need a certain combinatorial correspondence between the ele-
ments of Weyl groups and the irreducible representations. This was realized
in the work [BV82] by Barbasch and Vogan. In other words, they gave a way
of associating an element of the Weyl group to a symbol. Combining these
results together, we get combinatorial algorithms for computing a-functions
on the Weyl groups of types B and D (Propositions 3.2 and 3.5). It is
quite surprising that we can simplify these algorithms and get a uniform
and efficient formula of a-functions on classical Weyl groups (Theorem 1.3).

1.5. Associated varieties of highest weight modules. Now we can
compute GK dimensions of the highest weight HC modules for all Hermit-
ian type Lie groups. For the classical types, we apply the above algorithm
for GK dimensions of simple highest weight modules. For the exceptional
types, we will need some results in [CIS88]. By Vogan [Vog81b] and Ya-
mashita [Yam01], the associated varieties of such modules form a linear
chain of closures of certain orbits, and hence they are determined by their
GK dimensions. Combining these results, we can explicitly describe the as-
sociated varieties for all the highest weight HC modules, see Theorems 6.2
and 7.1.
1.6. Organization. This paper is organized as follows. In §2, we prepare
some necessary preliminaries. In §3, the formulas of Lusztig’s a-functions on
classical Weyl groups are described. In §4, we prove a key result (Theorem
4.10) about the domino tableaux. Then the GK dimensions of all the simple
highest weight modules for classical Lie algebras are obtained in §5. In
§6 and §7, we pin down the associated varieties of the highest weight HC
modules for all Hermitian type Lie groups.

   In this section, we introduce a new invariant, the hollow tableau. It will

4.1. Domino insertion. A domino is a skew shape consisting of two adja-
cent boxes. A (standard) domino tableau D consists of a tiling of the Young
diagram by dominoes and a filling of each domino with a positive integer,
such that the numbers are strictly increasing along each row and column. If
D has shape p, we write p = sh(D). We say D is a skew domino tableau if
D = Y /Z for two domino tableaux Z ⊆ Y .

Definition 4.2 (Domino insertion algorithm). Let D be a domino tableau
with shape p and i be a positive integer which does not appear in D. Now
we define a new domino tableau E = D ← ci with c = ±1 as follows.
      (1) Let E≤i be the domino tableau obtained from D≤i−1 by adding a
          horizontal (resp. vertical) domino with value i in the first row (resp.
          column) when c = 1 (resp. c = −1).
      (2) For j > i, assume that we have obtained E≤j−1 with shape qj−1 . If D
          contains no domino labeled j, then set E≤j = E≤j−1 , otherwise E≤j
          is a domino tableau obtained from E≤j−1 by bumping the domino
          domj in D occupied by j in the following way.
            (i) If qj−1 ∩ domj = ∅, then E≤j = E≤j−1 ∪ domj .
           (ii) If qj−1 ∩ domj is precisely a box (k, l), then we add a domino
                containing j to E≤j−1 to obtain E≤j which has shape qj−1 ∪
                domj ∪(k + 1, l + 1).
          (iii) If qj−1 ∩ domj = domj and domj is horizontal (resp. vertical),
                then we bump the domino domj to the next row (resp. column),
                and set E≤j to be the union of E≤j−1 with a horizontal (resp.
                vertical) domino with value j adding in the next row (resp.
                column) of domj .
      (3) Repeat (2) until D≥j+1 is empty, then set E = E≤j .
