      ChPT is a low-energy effective field theory (EFT) of the strong interaction, constructed from
the spontaneously-broken chiral symmetry of QCD. Its Lagrangian consists of infinitely many
terms, arranged in an increasing power of a small chiral expansion parameter (will be discussed
below), such that to any given level of precision, only a finite number of terms in the Lagrangian
need to be included. Terms in the chiral Lagrangian are accompanied by parameters known as
low-energy constants (LECs); they are non-perturbative quantities which values are not constrained
by the chiral symmetry, and thus must be deduced either from experiments or computed with
first-principles methods such as lattice QCD.

6. Summary

    To briefly summarize this talk:

    • The first-row CKM unitarity, often simplified as the Cabibbo unitarity, offers precision
      tests of the SM and sensitive probes for BSM physics competitive to high-energy collider
      experiments. The test requires precise extractions of 𝑉𝑢𝑑 and 𝑉𝑢𝑠 from weak decays, and 𝐾ℓ3
      is the primary avenue for the latter.

    • A number of SM theory inputs are needed for the extraction of 𝑉𝑢𝑠 from 𝐾ℓ3 partial decay
      rate, including the process-independent electroweak RC, the 𝐾 − → 𝜋 0 form factor, the phase
                                                                                            𝐾ℓ .
      space integral, the isospin-breaking correction, and the long-distance EM correction 𝛿EM

    • The previous state-of-the-art calculation of 𝛿EM
                                                    𝐾ℓ was performed using ChPT and reached a

      10−3 precision; the main sources of uncertainties are the neglected higher-order terms in the
      chiral expansion and the poorly-known LECs.

    • We performed a re-analysis of the 𝐾ℓ3 EM correction by combining Sirlin’s representation
      and ChPT approaches. The use of kaon and pion form factors and the lattice results of 𝐾𝜋
      box diagram overcome the limitations in the pure ChPT treatment and improve the precision
      level to 10−4 .

    • Our work helps to consolidate the 𝑉𝑢𝑠 extraction, and sharpen the so-called “Cabibbo angle
      anomaly” since its first observation in 2018.
