1       Introduction

The decay K + → π + ν ν̄ is a Flavor Changing Neutral Current (FCNC) process. This decay
proceeds at the lowest order in the Standard Model (SM) through electroweak box and penguin
diagrams and is therefore highly suppressed by the Glashow-Iliopoulos-Maiani (GIM) mechanism
and the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix element governing t → d quark transitions.
Since the decay is dominated by the short-distance t-quark exchange and the hadronic matrix
element can be extracted from the precisely measured decay of K + → π 0 e+ ν 1,2,3 , the SM predicts
the branching ratio of K + → π + ν ν̄ with a precision better than 8 % 4,5,6 . The uncertainty is
dominated by the CKM parameters Vcb and γ, while the intrinsic theoretical uncertainty is
approximately 3 % 1 . The prediction of the branching ratio was B(K + → π + ν ν̄) = (8.4 ± 1.0) ×
10−11 , using CKM matrix elements extracted from tree-level decays b 7 . More recent works
predict (8.60 ± 0.42) × 10−11 , using only meson mixing to eliminate |Vcb | dependence 4 , and
(7.86 ± 0.61) × 10−11 , using a full CKM parameter fit 8 .
      The K + → π + ν ν̄ decay is among the most promising channels to search for physics beyond
the Standard Model (BSM). It probes new physics at mass scales up to O(100 TeV). Several
BSM scenarios predict significant deviations from the SM branching ratio, correlations with
other flavor observables, as well as correlations with the corresponding neutral mode KL →
π 0 ν ν̄ 9,10,11,12 . A model-independent relationship between the charged and neutral modes is
provided by the Grossman-Nir bound 13 : B(KL → π 0 ν ν̄) ≲ 4.3 · B(K + → π + ν ν̄). The direct
measurement of the neutral mode is conducted by the KOTO experiment at J-PARC, providing
the latest upper limit B(KL → π 0 ν ν̄) < 2.2 × 10−9 at 90 % CL 14 .
      The first K + → π + ν ν̄ candidate event was reported by the E787 and E949 experiments at
BNL, using the kaon decay-at-rest technique, with a measured 15 B(K + → π + ν ν̄) = 17.3+11.5−10.5 ×
10−11 . The NA62 experiment at CERN was designed to measure the K + → π + ν ν̄ decay
with a kaon decay-in-flight technique. It provided evidence at the 3.4σ level using its 2016–
2018 dataset and has achieved 5σ observation with the combined 2016–2022 dataset 16,17 . The
measured branching ratio was B(K + → π + ν ν̄) = 13.0+3.3 −3.0 × 10
                                                                    −11 , from an observation of 51

events with 18+3     −2 expected background events. The NA62 experiment continues to collect data
until 2026. The preliminary result of the K + → π + ν ν̄ measurement with the dataset collected
by the NA62 experiment in 2023–2024 are reported below.

2       The NA62 experiment

A detailed description of the NA62 beam line and detector can be found in Ref. 18 . Fig. 1
displays the setup upgraded from 2021 onwards. A 75 GeV momentum secondary beam, with
a 6 % K + component, is produced by directing the 400 GeV proton beam from CERN SPS
onto a beryllium target. The kaons are identified by a differential Cherenkov counter (KTAG).
A silicon pixel detector based beam spectrometer (GTK) provides tracking of the beam parti-
cles. After the final collimator (COL) and the last station of GTK, the beam enters a vacuum
tank, which includes the fiducial decay volume (FV). Downstream of it, the decay product π +
is measured with a magnetic spectrometer (STRAW) for tracking, a ring-imaging Cherenkov
counter (RICH) for particle identification, and two scintillator hodoscopes (CHOD) for timing
and triggering. Additional particle identification is provided by a set of calorimeters consisting
of a liquid krypton electromagnetic calorimeter (LKr), 2 hadronic iron/scintillator-strip sam-
pling calorimeters (MUV1,2), and an array of scintillator tiles (MUV3) behind an iron wall. To
reject any additional activity, comprehensive veto systems are employed. Upstream of the FV,
scintillator-based veto detectors VC, CHANTI, and ANTI0 are designed to reject events with
K + decays upstream of the fiducial volume, interactions between beam particles and materials,
and beam halo muons, respectively. A photon veto system is composed of 12 large-angle veto

    In 2021–2022 data-taking, NA62 was operating under its maximum design intensity. Studies

performance according to the studies. Since 2023, the radiator gas of KTAG was changed from
N2 to H2 , lowering the material budget from 3.9 % X0 to 0.7 % X0 20 .

combined RICH and calorimetric particle identification (PID) suppresses µ+ ⇒ π + misidentifi-
cation at O(107 ). The comprehensive photon veto system rejects π 0 at O(108 ). The excellent
performance of the NA62 detector provides the sensitivity to the ultra-rare K + → π + ν ν̄ signal.


3   Event selections

The analysis uses K + → π + π 0 as the normalization channel. The normalization sample is
selected from a dedicated trigger line (NORM) with a downscaling factor DNORM = 400 and
requires signatures consistent with a single π + track with in-time signal in KTAG. Offline the
downstream π + track is matched to an upstream K + candidate in space and time. A vertex
is defined as the mid-point of the Closest Distance of Approach (CDA) segment between the
two extrapolated tracks. Any upstream activity is rejected using the upstream veto detectors
described above, as well as information from GTK. A Boosted Decision Tree (BDT) classifier
based on the spatial information of the two tracks is used to further reject events with the π +
coming upstream of the FV. Finally, the full PID based on RICH and calorimeters is applied.
    The signal is selected from the signal trigger line (PNN) with no downscaling and applies
trigger level photon and multi-track vetos in addition to the NORM trigger. In offline selections,
in addition to the normalization selection, full photon veto and multi-track veto are applied.
    In the 2023–2024 dataset analysis, a transformer-based 4D GTK beam-tracking algorithm is
developed, reducing the probability that the true K + is not reconstructed in the GTK from 6 %
to 4 % with no significant loss of efficiency. Furthermore, a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)
based calorimetric PID is developed using information from the LKr and MUV1,2, significantly
improving the performance against µ+ ⇒ π + misidentification, especially when a photon cluster
overlaps with the µ+ track in LKr.
    In PNN trigger line, the LAV is used to apply a trigger-level photon veto at large angles.

Since 2023, this trigger condition was changed from vetoing the event with any signal in LAV
stations 2–11 within a 6 ns time window to vetoing events within 4 ns time window and only
using LAV stations downstream of the vertex between the π + track and the nominal beam

the number of events with the π + coming from beam-material interactions upstream of the FV.
To suppress these upstream background events, an offline veto using the first station of LAV
within 2 ns time window is applied. This offline veto provides a much stronger suppression of
these events than the previous trigger-level veto.

4   Signal sensitivity

The analysis exploits the kinematic variable squared missing mass, m2miss = (PK − Pπ )2 , where
PK and Pπ are the 4-momenta of the K + and π + candidates. In the m2miss distribution, signal
regions are defined to suppress the main kaon decay modes at O(104 ) 21,16 . The analysis is
performed in six categories of 5 GeV/c wide bins of π + momentum between 15–45 GeV/c. For
a specific π + momentum bin pi , the observed number of events in signal region (SR) is given by

7     Conclusion

The NA62 2023–2024 dataset doubled the effective sample size of the previous dataset while
significantly reducing the background in proportion, bringing a major boost in sensitivity. The
combined NA62 2016–2024 dataset provides a measured branching ratio B(K + → π + ν ν̄) =
(9.6+1.9
    −1.8 ) × 10
                −11 , achieving a precision of 20 %, excluding the background-only hypothesis with

a significance exceeding 6σ. The NA62 results are self-consistent and compatible with the results
from the BNL E787 and E949 experiments 15 , and are in good agreement with SM predictions.
The NA62 experiment continues to take data until 2026. Based on the number of days of
data-taking, the whole dataset is extrapolated to increase by about 50 %.
