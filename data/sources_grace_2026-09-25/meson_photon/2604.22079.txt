                                                    The Belle and Belle II experiments have accumulated a combined data set of 1.2 ab−1 of
                                                    e+ e− → B B̄ collisions at the Υ(4S) resonance. Owing to the clean event environment and the
                                                    well-constrained initial-state kinematics, these data are particularly well suited for studying
                                                    channels involving missing energy from neutrinos. This includes the reinterpretation of B + →
                                                    K + ν ν̄ and the first inclusive measurement of B → Xs ν ν̄ with the missing energy directly from
                                                    B decays, as well as searches involving missing energy from τ decays, such as B 0 → K ∗0 τ + τ − ,
                                                    B 0 → KS0 τ + τ − , and B + → K + τ + τ − .


                                          1    Introduction

                                          The quark transition b → s, mediated by a flavour-changing neutral current (FCNC), is a
                                          sensitive probe of physics beyond the Standard Model (SM). In the SM, FCNC processes occur
                                          only through loop diagrams such as the electroweak penguin amplitudes in Fig. 1a, leading to
                                          small branching fractions of O(10−7 )–O(10−5 ). Searches at Belle and Belle II aim to reveal
                                          possible New Physics (NP) effects via modified rates, either from new tree-level interactions
                                          (Fig. 1b) or reduced GIM suppression in loops (Fig. 1c).

                                              The Belle experiment 1 operated at the KEKB asymmetric e+ e− collider 2 in Tsukuba,
                                          Japan. Its successor, Belle II 3 , runs at SuperKEKB, designed to reach about forty times higher
                                          instantaneous luminosity 4 . (Super)KEKB collides e+ e− at the Υ(4S) resonance, producing
                                          almost exclusively B B̄ pairs in a low-background environment compared to hadron colliders.
                                          The data samples used here correspond to 711 fb−1 from Belle and 365 fb−1 from Belle II. Since
                                          Υ(4S) → B B̄ produces no additional particles, missing energy from neutrinos in the signal decay
                                          can be inferred from the reconstruction of the accompanying B meson (Btag ). The large solid-
                                          angle coverage and well-constrained initial kinematics of Belle and Belle II make it particularly
well suited for analyses with missing energy, using the two tagging methods described below.

Inclusive tagging: this method exploits the inclusive properties of the accompanying B decay
by exploiting distinct signal features with machine-learning. It offers high efficiency, but suffers
from large background and a strong reliance on simulation.

Exclusive tagging: This approach employs the machine-learning-based Full Event Interpreta-
tion (FEI) algorithm 5 to reconstruct the tag-side B meson using either hadronic or semileptonic
decay modes. By applying a stringent requirement on the FEI output probability, a high-purity
sample can be obtained.

      • Hadronic tagging fully reconstructs specific hadronic B decays. It strongly suppresses
        combinatorial background and provides tight kinematic constraints on Bsig via p⃗Bsig =
        −⃗pBtag in the Υ(4S) rest frame. However, the reconstruction efficiency is typically below
        1%.

      • Semileptonic tagging reconstructs B → Dℓν and B → D∗ ℓν decays, where the neutrino
        is treated as missing energy. The presence of a high-momentum lepton enables efficient
        identification, resulting in higher efficiency than hadronic tagging due to large semilep-
        tonic branching fractions. However, the undetected neutrino leads to reduced kinematic
        constraints and lower sample purity.

2     b → sℓ+ ℓ− (b → sτ + τ − ) transitions

In this report, we concentrate on final states containing τ -lepton pairs. The b → sτ + τ − pro-
cesses involve third-generation leptons, which makes them particularly sensitive to potential
new physics contributions 6 . Furthermore, the ability of Belle II to reconstruct final states with
undetectable neutrinos from τ decays provides a significant experimental advantage.

2.1    B 0 → K ∗0 τ + τ − search using hadronic B-tagging at Belle II
Signal candidates are categorized into ℓℓ, ℓπ, ππ, and ρX categories according to the τ decay
products (tτ ). The excellent photon efficiency and resolution enable a clean reconstruction of
π 0 mesons, allowing ρ+ → π + π 0 decays to be included as τ prong candidates.
    Among these, the ℓℓ category provides the highest sensitivity and purity due to the excel-
lent lepton identification at Belle II and the strong suppression of q q̄ background. The latter
originates from the fact that continuum q q̄ events hadronize into jet-like topologies dominated
by light hadrons, resulting in event shapes that differ significantly from the more spherical B B̄
topology. A boosted decision tree (BDT) is trained to further suppress background using vari-
ables such as residual calorimeter energy in the electromagnetic calorimeter (Eextra ), the K ∗0 tτ
invariant mass, q 2 , etc. A simultaneous fit to the BDT outputs (Fig. 2) shows no significant
signal. We obtain a limit of B UL > 1.8 × 10−3 at 90% confidence limit (CL), which improves
the Belle constraint by approximately a factor of two while using only half of its data.

2.3   B + → K + τ + τ − search using hadronic B-tagging at Belle and Belle II
On the signal side, only leptonic τ decays (τ → eν ν̄ and τ → µν ν̄) are considered, providing
a low-background environment. Instead of a multivariate approach, a cut-based strategy is
adopted: the dominant B → Dℓν background is suppressed by requiring the Kℓ invariant mass
to exceed the D-meson threshold, the lepton momentum, and the q 2 .
     The signal yield is extracted from the distribution of Eextra , using a Poisson event-counting

photons in the calorimeter and backrgound distributes far from 0. The background expectation
is validated with sideband data. No significant excess is observed, and an upper limit of B(B + →
K + τ + τ − ) < 5.6 × 10−4 at 90% CL is set (the upper limit has been updated compared to the

3     b → sν ν̄ transitions

3.1    B + → K + ν ν̄ reinterpretation

We recently determined of the B(B + → K + ν ν̄) decay at Belle II under the assumption of SM
kinematics, providing the first evidence fo this decay using combined inclusive and hadronic B-
tagging method 12 . In this analysis, backgrounds are suppressed using two consecutive classifiers:
BDT1 and BDT2 . The signal yield is extracted using a two-dimensional binning scheme in
the transformed BDT2 output, ηBDT2 , and the reconstructed momentum-transfer squared, qrec      2 ,

which is divided into 4 × 3 bins.
    The reinterpretation strategy is straightforward 13 . We start with simulated SM signal events
and construct a model-agnostic number density, n0 (x), where x denotes the fit observables,
namely ηBDT2 and qrec 2 as shown in Eq. 1. The expected event yield in a given point x is

obtained from the selection efficiency and the model prediction. For a new model, we reweight
the SM events in each q 2 bin by the ratio of the new-model prediction to the SM one, σσ10 , which
provides the predicted distribution for that model.

    The Weak Effective Theory (WET) provides a unified framework for studying possible NP
effects, as it describes contributions from both the SM and NP through effective operators. In
this formalism, vector, scalar, and tensor Wilson coefficients (C) contribute to the differential
branching fraction of the B + → K + ν ν̄ decay as predicted by the WET is given by 14 . We
extract the marginal posterior distributions for the unconstrained combinations CV L + CV R ,
CSL + CSR , and CT , which are treated as the parameters of interest together with the relevant
hadronic form factors. The posterior maximum is located at (11.3, 0.0, 8.2) in this parameter
space. As shown in Fig. 5, our work obtains that an enhanced vector contribution, together
with a non-zero tensor one, provide a better description of data.

5   Conclusion

The Belle II detector, with its large solid-angle coverage and well-constrained initial-state kine-
matics, provides an excellent environment for studying B decays with missing energy using
various tagging techniques. Current results demonstrate that Belle II can already achieve com-
petitive—and in some cases world-leading—precision with relatively modest data samples.
    We present a model-agnostic reinterpretation of the B + → K + ν ν̄ result 12 within the WET
framework, providing the first credible intervals for b → s Wilson coefficients from Belle II data.
By releasing the corresponding likelihood 16 , this work enables rigorous tests of new-physics
scenarios and establishes a framework for future Belle II reinterpretations.
    In addition, using the same dataset and hadronic B-tagging, we study the FCNC processes
B → Xs ν ν̄ and B 0 → K ∗0 τ + τ − . We set 90% CL upper limits of 3.2 × 10−4 and 1.8 × 10−3 ,
respectively, with the former representing the most stringent constraint to date.
