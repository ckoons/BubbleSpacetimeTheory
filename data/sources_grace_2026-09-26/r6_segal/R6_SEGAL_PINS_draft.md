# R6 — Segal chronometric cosmology: claims and observational verdicts (PIN DRAFT)

Compiled 2026-09-26 (Grace lane, r6). Every pin below is a verbatim quote from a file in this directory, cited as `file:line`.
Conventions:
- `*_ADSscan.txt` = `pdftotext -layout` of the NASA ADS full-text scan (articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/<bibcode>). The text layer is ADS's OCR, so OCR errors are kept exactly as they appear (for example "chronométrie" for "chronometric", "tan2" for "tan²", "3n/2" for "3π/2", "oo" for "∞"). The PDF image is the authority for any quote that matters.
- `*_arXiv*.txt` = `pdftotext -layout` of the arXiv PDF (the preprint version, not the journal version).
- `pubmed_segal_pnas_abstracts.txt` = abstracts pulled from the NCBI E-utilities PubMed XML (saved alongside). This is the author's own abstract text, so it counts as PRIMARY, but ABSTRACT ONLY. The PNAS full texts (PMC scanned PDFs) could not be fetched: PMC returned a reCAPTCHA, Europe PMC and pnas.org returned Cloudflare challenges, and Wayback had no snapshots. **Full-text PNAS pins are PIN OWED.**
- PRIMARY = author's own text, full paper. PRIMARY-ABS = author's own abstract only. SECONDARY = someone else describing it. INFERENCE = my reasoning connecting a paper that does not name Segal to Segal's model. **An INFERENCE is not a verdict on Segal from the paper.**

## B. What Segal claimed (PRIMARY unless marked)

**B1. Spacetime = R¹×S³ (Einstein universe), conformally equivalent to the universal cover of compactified Minkowski space; two times, two energies.** Source: Segal & Zhou 1995, ApJS 100, 307, ADS scan, PRIMARY full text.
- `segal_zhou1995_apjs100_307_ADSscan.txt:19-20`: "M is conformally equivalent to the Einstein universe E = Æ1 X S3, (where R1 denotes the time axis and S3 the space consisting of a three-dimensional sphere)." ["Æ1" is OCR for R¹.]
- `:20-24`: "Temporal evolution in M is equivalent to that in E; i.e., simple translation along the time axis R1. This is materially inequivalent to time evolution in Mo. The “Einstein” and “Minkowski” energies in M and Mo (i.e., conjugate quantities to the respective times) are correspondingly materially different on a cosmic timescale, although microscopically they differ by unobservably little."
- `:24-27`: "Chronometrie cosmology (CC) proposes that the Einstein energy is the driving energy of the universe, while the Minkowski energy is the locally observed energy, as required for coincidence with the local frequency of oscillation. The redshift is correspondingly the excess of the Einstein over the Minkowski energy."
- `:27-29`: "CC is devoid of adjustable cosmological parameters, luminosity, or density evolution, implies conservation of the Einstein (but not Minkowski) energy, and predicts that remnant radiation will be in an isotropic Planck-law state."
- `:89-92` (mechanism): "The generator of temporal evolution in this local Minkowski space corresponds to the usual relativistic energy that is measured by extant local apparatus but it is fundamentally inequivalent to the generator of temporal evolution in the global Einstein universe. The latter generator necessarily exceeds the former, not by theoretical fiat but in an intrinsic mathematical way, giving rise to a spectral shift to the red"
- `:234-235` (metric convention): "the metric c2 dt2 — R2 ds2, where c and R are positive constants, t is the time coordinate, and ds is the element of arc length on S'3 in radians."
- `:176-181` (Segal's speculative extension, relevant to any "second-time energy = gravity" idea): "The excess E - Eqoï the Einstein energy E over the Minkowski energy EQ vanishes to second order in the distance ... 3. E- E0 transforms identically to the Newtonian gravitational potential under scaling and Euclidean transformations. One is thereby led to the proposal that the diffuse energy E- E0 may primarily represent what is otherwise familiar as gravitational"

**B2. The redshift–distance law, verbatim, with its convention.**
- Segal & Zhou 1995, `segal_zhou1995_apjs100_307_ADSscan.txt:30-31` (PRIMARY): "The redshift-distance law in CC, which takes the form z = tan2 (p/2), where p is the distance in radians on S3, is derived rigorously for photons of localized spatial support." [OCR: "tan2 (p/2)" = tan²(ρ/2), where ρ is the distance in radians on S³.]
- Segal 1976 PNAS 73, 669 abstract, `pubmed_segal_pnas_abstracts.txt:7` (PRIMARY-ABS): "...the relation z = tan(2)(r/2R) (where R denotes the radius of the universe)..." [PubMed renders tan² as "tan(2)".]
- Segal 1993 PNAS 90, 11114 abstract, `pubmed_segal_pnas_abstracts.txt:11` (PRIMARY-ABS): "The chronometric redshift-distance relation z = tan 2(1/2rho), where rho is the distance in radians in the Einstein metric, is derived by an elementary geometric analysis ... The differential dTt of Einstein time evolution Tt through time t, as applied to the local Minkowski coordinates x, takes the form sec2(1/2t). At the point of observation t = rho, implying that for a sufficiently localized source, observed wave lengths are a factor of sec2(1/2rho) greater than the corresponding emitted wave lengths."
- Segal & Nicoll 1986 ApJ 300, 224, `segal_nicoll1986_apj300_224_ADSscan.txt:108-118` (PRIMARY): units "c = h = R = 1, where R is the “radius of the universe” S3 ... called a radius or “ rad.”"; line 113: "dV(r) = 4n x sin2 rdr , L(r) oc (sin r)~2 ." [OCR: dV = 4π sin²r dr, L(r) ∝ (sin r)⁻²]; line 118: "z = tan2 (r/2)".
- **Convention summary:** z = tan²(ρ/2), with ρ = r/R the geodesic distance on S³ in radians. For small ρ this gives z ≈ ρ²/4, a quadratic law. With 1+z = sec²(ρ/2), the wavelength ratio is sec²(ρ/2) (1993 abstract). Wright 1987 `wright1987_apj313_551_ADSscan.txt:27-30` writes the same law as "z = tan2 (6/2)" [OCR of θ/2] and calls it "the redshift follows the square of the distance for small distances".
- **NOT pinned:** Segal's first 1972 statement of the law (the request named "Covariant chronogeometry and extreme distances", PNAS 1972). The 1972 paper was not located. The "Covariant chronogeometry and extreme distances" title found is the 1981 PNAS paper (78, 5261), whose full text is blocked. The 1975 abstract (`pubmed_segal_pnas_abstracts.txt:3`) says "the cosmological theory proposed by Segal in 1972". **The 1972 source is PIN OWED.**

**B3. Zero free parameters; Segal's claimed observational success.** PRIMARY-ABS.
- 1975 PNAS 72, 2473, `pubmed_segal_pnas_abstracts.txt:3`: "It implies definite relations between directly observed quantities ... which are entirely free of adjustable parameters, such as the q(0) and Lambda of general relativistic cosmologies. Rigorous statistical analysis of large ( greater, similar100) or systematically defined samples of galaxies or quasars accepts these relations at substantial probability levels."
- Nicoll & Segal 1978 PNAS 75, 535, `pubmed_segal_pnas_abstracts.txt:19`: "The chronometric theory, although devoid of free parameters ... provides an excellent fit to the observed relation. The expansion theory does not provide an empirically meaningful fit."

**B4. Segal's CMB claim.** Segal & Zhou 1995, `segal_zhou1995_apjs100_307_ADSscan.txt:1140-1146` (PRIMARY): "A free photon will be infinitely redshifted at the antipode in S3 to its point P of emission, but on returning to P it will be in its original state, as a consequence of the periodicity of free photon wave functions in E. The Planck theory ( or the maximization of the entropy subject to energy conservation) implies that the remnant radiation in the universe will be in a black-body state, irrespective of how optically thin cosmic space may be. ... CC thus implies, without any ad hoc scenario, the existence of an isotropic cosmic background radiation."

**N1 — why rows 5–8 bear on Segal (my inference, not a pinned verdict).** Wright states the chronometric photometric relation D_L = sqrt(1+z)·D_A, against (1+z)²·D_A in "all respectable cosmological models" (`wright_segal_errors.txt:51-53`). This is consistent with Segal & Nicoll's own L(r) ∝ (sin r)⁻² on S³ (`segal_nicoll1986_apj300_224_ADSscan.txt:113`) plus an energy-only (1+z) loss. On a static R×S³ with energy-only redshift, there is no (1+z) stretch of arrival rates (no time dilation). Surface brightness would then dim as (1+z)⁻¹, the tired-light exponent n=1. Lubin & Sandage measure n≈2.3–3.6. I have NOT found a primary Segal text stating the chronometric prediction for SN light-curve durations or for surface brightness. **Before rows 5–8 can be called a verdict on chronometric cosmology, the chronometric time-dilation prediction must be pinned from Segal. That pin is owed.** The Segal texts in this directory were checked by grep for "dilation", "surface brightness" and "Tolman": the only hits are the Hubble & Tolman 1935 citation.
