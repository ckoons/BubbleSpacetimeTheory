                                                                                            ABSTRACT
                                            The Palomar Bright Quasar Survey and other complete samples derived by Schmidt and Green from
                                         quasar surveys are used for comparative cosmological studies and for predictions of quasar counts. Systematic
                                         self-tests and mutual cross-tests of the chronométrie and Friedman cosmologies are made. Nonparametric esti-
                                         mates of the normalized luminosity function are made that involve no assumption as to the homogeneity of
                                         the spatial distribution of quasars and are statistically optimal in this respect. The homogeneity question is
                                         studied independently by V/Vm tests.
                                            The chronométrie cosmology is found to fit the observations quite satisfactorily without either luminosity
                                         or density evolution, and without the use of any adjustable parameters such as q0 or A. In addition, its predic-
                                         tions regarding the results of analyses predicated on a nonevolutionary Friedman cosmology are similar to the
                                         deviations actually found in such analyses, from which the hypotheses of evolution have derived.
                                            The number of quasars per unit volume, out to a designated limiting absolute magnitude, is estimated, and
                                         is combined with the convolution of the normalized luminosity function and the chronométrie volume rela-
                                         tion, constrained appropriately in redshift and magnitude, to obtain estimates of the expected numbers of
                                         quasars in the individual samples treated by Schmidt and Green. These theoretical estimates are in good
                                         agreement with the observed counts.

      mologies 1 and 2 under the physical assumption of NU, or
      spatial homogeneity (more exactly, spatial homogeneity in the
      radial direction; the question of the isotropy of the quasar
      distribution is not directly treated here, but isotropy is theo-
      retically implied by radial spatial homogeneity relative to
      every point of observation). It is of course no novelty, by virtue
      of the work of Schmidt and others, that a nonevolutionary
      Friedman cosmology is inconsistent with NU. However, the
      extent to which the departures of this cosmology from the
      implications of NU are predictable by the chronométrie cos-
      mology in conjunction with NU are relevant to the cross-
      testing of the latter cosmology, and to the evaluation of the
      hypothesis of number evolution.
