Related 2024–2026 lattice work with no new binding number:
- Parrott et al., PoS LATTICE2024 (2025) 084 (2409.11477). Proceedings of the same group's analysis in #8. Journal ref as cited at 2603.12418.txt:1369–1370.
- Vujmilovic, Collins, Leskovec, Prelovsek 2025/26 (2510.17549, v4 29 Apr 2026, no jref on arXiv). First lattice calculation of the T_bb electromagnetic form factors. It finds a compact [bb] 3̄ diquark plus a [ūd̄] antidiquark, and a charge radius "significantly smaller than the combined charge radii of B and B*" (2510.17549.txt:16–18). It calls T_bb "as-yet-experimentally-undiscovered" (2510.17549.txt:46–47).

Two corrections to the brief:
- The brief labels 2404.03588 (Alexandrou et al.) a "scattering-amplitude pole" result. It is not. Its headline number comes from chiral and continuum extrapolation of ground-state energies. The authors state they did not use Lüscher amplitudes because of left-hand-cut concerns, and that trial K-matrix fits moved the pole by ≲0.1 MeV (2404.03588.txt:954–959). The pole-based determinations are #3 (ERE, 2019) and #9 (continuum-extrapolated amplitude, 2025).
- #10 revises #3. The same Meinel data, reanalysed with symmetric local-only correlation matrices, gives −74 rather than −128 (2603.12418.txt:21–23).

Spread across the 10 rows: −189 (2017) down to −74/−76 (2026). The more recent calculations (#5–#10) fall between −76 and −116, with the HAL QCD coupled-channel value at −83. Every group finds the state below threshold, and every group that says so explicitly calls it strong- and EM-stable.

## C. T_bc (b c ubar dbar, I=0): lattice status is contested

- Padmanath, Radhakrishnan, Mathur 2024 (2307.14128; PRL 132, 201902). J^P=1+: binding −43(+6 −7)(+14 −24) MeV relative to D B̄* (2307.14128.txt:34–35; :350). Scattering length a0 = +0.57(+4 −5)(17) fm, "strong enough to host a real bound state Tbc" (2307.14128.txt:31–34).
- Status among lattice groups, in the words of Alexandrou et al. 2024: "Independent groups carrying out full lattice-QCD simulations have arrived at different conclusions. The existence of a strong-interaction-stable b̄c̄ud tetraquark with I(J^P)=0(1+) was initially reported in Ref. [45] but later revoked in Ref. [46]. In Ref. [42], we did not find evidence for QCD-stable b̄c̄ud bound states, but could not rule out shallow bound states. Other authors found an indication for a b̄c̄ud bound state …" (2404.03588.txt:60–64).
- Colquhoun et al. 2024 expect "at-most-weak-binding in the 1+ udb̄c̄ channel" (2407.08816.txt:1086).

## D. Heavy-quark-symmetry and phenomenology anchors (2017)

- Eichten & Quigg 2017 (1707.09575; PRL 119, 202002). Predicts {bb}[ūd̄] 1+ at m = 10 482 MeV, Q = −121 MeV relative to B⁻B̄*⁰ at 10 603 MeV. Predicts {bb}[q̄s̄] 1+ at Q = −48 MeV (Table II, 1707.09575.txt:202–203; text :129–132). Abstract: "double-beauty states composed of bbūd̄, bbūs̄, and bbd̄s̄ will be stable against strong decays, whereas the double-charm states … mixed beauty+charm states bcq̄kq̄l … will dissociate into pairs of heavy-light mesons" (1707.09575.txt:11–14). Their [bc][ūd̄] 0+ has Q = +83 and {bc}[ūd̄] 1+ has Q = +82 (1707.09575.txt:192, :198), so they predict T_bc unbound.
- Karliner & Rosner 2017 (1707.07666; PRL 119, 202001). T(bbūd̄) 1+ "at 10,389 ± 12 MeV, 215 MeV below the B⁻B̄*⁰ threshold and 170 MeV below threshold for decay to B⁻B̄⁰γ … therefore stable under strong and electromagnetic (EM) interactions and can only decay weakly, the first exotic hadron with such a property" (1707.07666.txt:31–36). T(bcūd̄) 0+ at 7134 ± 13 MeV, 11 MeV below B̄⁰D⁰; "Our precision is not sufficient to determine whether bcūd̄ is actually above or below the threshold" (1707.07666.txt:40–42).

## E. Lifetime / weak-decay expectation

- Karliner & Rosner 2017 give a crude free-b-decay estimate. It uses |Vcb| = 0.04, a factor 2 for two b quarks, and a final state at M(B̄)+M(D) = 7144.5 MeV. Result: Γ = 17.9 × 10⁻¹³ GeV, "leading to a predicted lifetime τ(bbūd̄) = 367 fs" (1707.07666.txt:225–245).
- Colquhoun et al. 2024: "can decay only via the weak interaction. It is thus expected to be narrow, long-lived, and have a large number of exclusive decay modes, each with a small branching fraction, making experimental detection at current facilities extremely challenging" (2407.08816.txt:1138–1141).

## F. Experimental status: not observed; no dedicated published search found

- LHCb T_cc⁺ observation, Nature Physics (2022) (2109.01038). Motivation: "It is expected that the b quark is heavy enough to sustain the existence of a stable bbud state with its binding energy of about 200 MeV" (2109.01038.txt:65–68). Conclusion: "The observation of this ccud tetraquark candidate close to the D*⁺D⁰ threshold provides strong support for the theory approaches and models that predict the existence of a bbud tetraquark stable with respect to the strong and electromagnetic interactions" (2109.01038.txt:286–290).
- LHCb T_cc⁺ study, Nature Comm. 13, 3351 (2022) (2109.01056). Same framing: "stable … with a mass of about 200 MeV below the BB* mass threshold" (2109.01056.txt:106–108).
- LHCb Upgrade II physics case (1808.08865; CERN-LHCC-2018-027): "Prompt production at LHC remains the best hope for unambiguously establishing the existence of stable, weakly decaying bbud tetraquark predicted by both lattice QCD and phenomenological models … However, the inclusive reconstruction efficiencies for such states are tiny due to the small branching fractions of B and D mesons decays to low multiplicity final states" (1808.08865.txt:6248–6253; text spans a page break). Example channel X_bbud → B⁻ D⁺ π⁻ (Fig. 9.2, 1808.08865.txt:6160–6170).
- Most recent primary statements (2026):
  - Dai, Jia, Nefediev, Nieves, Shen, Zhang, "Exotic hadrons associated with b-quark" (2603.09315 v2, 18 Jun 2026). The authors include Belle II and LHCb physicists. On the T_cc partners T_bb and T_bc: "it has not yet been observed at Belle II, while its partner states can not be accessed at this experiment at all. In principle, LHCb could search for the particularly intriguing Tbc state …; however, no experimental information on this state is currently available" (2603.09315.txt:222–228).
  - Vujmilovic et al. (v4 Apr 2026) call T_bb "as-yet-experimentally-undiscovered" (2510.17549.txt:46–47).
  - The Belle/Belle II spectroscopy review (2606.23170, Jun 2026) has no hit for T_bb or bbūd̄ (grep).
- **Search status:** I found no published LHCb, CMS or Belle II search for T_bb. Web search found only phenomenology papers on search strategy, for example Ali, Qin, Wang, PLB 785, 605 (2018), arXiv:1806.09288, as cited at 2503.09760.txt:1225–1227. Those were not downloaded. That "no search exists" is a negative from search, not a pin; PIN OWED if a positive statement is needed (e.g. an LHCb exotic-hadron review or the PDG).
