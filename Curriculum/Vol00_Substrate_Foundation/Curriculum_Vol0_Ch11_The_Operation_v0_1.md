## 11.1 The one act (for a bright high-schooler)

Everything in this volume exists to support **one thing that happens**: a **commitment**.

To commit is to do two things at once — one act, two stages:

- **WRITE** — lay down a single *yes/no* record on the boundary that you *cannot take back*. "This happened." The fact that it can't be un-done is the arrow of time.
- **ENCODE** — the interior answers by wrapping that record in the smallest self-checking structure, so if the record is later corrupted, the corruption can be *noticed and located*. The record carries its own damage report.

**Write the bit; check the bit.** That is the whole operation. Quantum mechanics is what it looks like as it happens; spacetime is the stage it happens on; the particles are the alphabet it is written in.

## 11.2 The operation as one operator (linear algebra on D_IV⁵)

Formally, a commitment is a single **graded operator** on the spin-factor structure of D_IV⁵:

> **C = P_record ⊕ P_encode**

a rank-1 projection onto the *arrow line* (the irreversible binary write, eigenvalues {0,1}) ⊕ the projection onto **V₁₂** (the confined 3-dimensional encode). This is the geometry's *own* Peirce decomposition of a primitive idempotent — not a picture laid over the geometry, but the geometry read one way. **[Tier: Derived — verified.]**

**Neither stage is removable** (this is why it is *minimal*): drop the encode → the record can be silently corrupted; drop the write → there is nothing to encode.

**One honest correction, kept in the text:** the encode is a **detection/erasure** structure (minimum distance 2), *not* an error-correcting code. So the record is **self-validating** — it flags its own damage — but *not* "unforgeable" (a public code forges trivially; the tamper-resistance is the irreversibility of the write, not the code). Earlier drafts said "error-correction" and "unforgeable"; both are corrected here.
