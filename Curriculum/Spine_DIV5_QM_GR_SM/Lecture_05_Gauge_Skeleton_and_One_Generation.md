# Lecture 5 — The Gauge Skeleton and One Generation

## The question

Why these forces, and why do the particles carry exactly these charges?

## For the reader in a hurry

The shape's symmetry group has a skeleton — a pattern of commuting rotations — and the Standard Model's gauge group fits onto it in one way. A single generation of matter is the smallest object the shape's spin structure allows, sixteen real components, and once you fix one observed charge every other charge in the generation follows from a theorem. What does *not* follow is the strong force itself: the shape supplies a slot for three colours and no group to rotate them (Lecture 2). We import that, and we say so.

## What this lecture will derive (from the register — prose to be written)

1. The skeleton: which subgroups of $\mathrm{SO}(5,2)$ carry the electroweak algebra, and why electric charge lives in the Cartan of $\mathrm{SO}(5)$ and not on the central circle (K1687 — a correction of the program's own earlier identification, and the fifth victim of one mislabel).
2. One generation: the 16-real spinor; the four-input hypercharge theorem with its one observational input stated inside it; why every hypercharge in the generation is then fixed.
3. Chirality: a single-chirality positive-energy spectrum from the odd embedding dimension.
4. The conserved charges: Noether's from the group, three more topological.
5. What is *kinematic* about the strong sector: (A1) — no two-row-sector $K$-type has a Šilov boundary value — a theorem about which states can reach the boundary, stated without the word "colour." And what is imported: the $SU(3)$ dynamics, the confining potential, the string tension. We used to write "confinement (A) derived." We do not.

## Tier line

Derived: 1–4 and (A1). Imported: SU(3) dynamics (#108). Not claimed: SU(3) from $N_c$; confinement derived; grand unification (forbidden — Lecture 10).

## What would make this lecture wrong

A fifth force, or a fermion outside the 16-real spinor's charge assignments in a single generation, would falsify item 2. Proton decay or a right-handed $W$ would falsify the skeleton (they are on the forbidden list). These are live laboratory statements.
