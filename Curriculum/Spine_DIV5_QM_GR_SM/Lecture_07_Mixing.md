# Lecture 7 — Mixing

## The question

Why do quarks of different generations mix the way they do — and what exactly did the geometry predict before anyone looked?

## For the reader in a hurry

When a quark changes flavour it can land in any of three generations, and the odds fall off steeply: the first-to-third jump is much rarer than the second-to-third. We asked the shape two things. First: how big is the first mixing angle? It said $1/\sqrt{20}$, and it said so on 2026-07-29 before we compared, and the measured value agrees to a third of a percent. Second: how much rarer is the far jump than the near one? It said *exactly one power rarer*, and that is a statement with no knob in it — you cannot tune an order. What the shape did *not* tell us is the size of the far jump itself; we tried five candidate formulas, sealed them in an envelope, opened it, and all five missed. We publish that with the prediction.
