# Lecture 10 — The Method, and How to Kill It

## The question

How do we know what we know — and what would make us stop?

## For the reader in a hurry

Most of this course has been about what we found. This lecture is about how we kept ourselves honest while finding it, because with a shape this rich the danger is not that you find nothing; it is that you find everything. Our rules are simple. Every claim carries a word that says how much to believe it. Every number that could have been tuned is computed with the target hidden and the protocol written down and hashed first. Every failure is published in the same voice as every success. And there is a short list of experimental results — no grand unification, no proton decay, no right-handed weak bosons, no magnetic monopoles, no sterile neutrinos, no supersymmetric partners — any one of which, confirmed, ends the program. We would rather you read that list first.

## What this lecture will state (prose to be written)

1. The four tier words and how they are used exactly; why "partially derived" is allowed only in explicit-split form; why "identified" is a candidate and never evidence.
2. Forcing and evidence: the instrument validated must be the instrument used; a null with a free sampling range is not a null; "smallest of N" presupposes an enumerable family and N is reported; count over ranking; a control that passes can be the wrong control.
3. Pre-registration as we practise it: the hash before the number; landings and tolerances committed; half-blind; the sealed-envelope negatives of Lectures 7 and 8 as worked examples.
4. The audit chain: how a K-audit, a Cal section, and a registry row relate; what "banked" means and what it does not; the recursive part — the checkers check the checkers, and the record shows it (seven upstream catches in one packaging week, two against the gate-writer and the checker).
5. What went wrong, told plainly: the scorecard that said "derived" twelve days after the registry said "identified"; the genus that was mislabelled for four months; the colour clause in a row registered fifteen days before the ruling that struck it and never swept; the three λ's in one chapter. And the instruments built after each.
6. **How to kill it.** The forbidden list, with the experiment that would fire each. The live positive falsifiers: first-row unitarity (Lecture 7); the $|V_{ub}|/|V_{cb}|$ order; Cal's $\varepsilon = 0$; a forced $p \notin (0,2)$. The doors: the Bergman-to-Hardy link, the mechanism for the colour identification, a non-measure weight for the mass tower — each named with the kind of idea that opens it.
7. How to check any of this without trusting us: `python3 play/verify_bst.py`; the registry; the toys; the audits; this curriculum's own staleness stamp.
