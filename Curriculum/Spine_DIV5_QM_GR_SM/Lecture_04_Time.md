# Lecture 4 — Time

## The question

What is time, in a geometry that has none built in — and why does it run one way?

## For the reader in a hurry

Our shape has a circle at its centre — the $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ in its stabiliser $K = \mathrm{SO}(5)\times\mathrm{SO}(2)$. Nothing in the shape moves; but if you ask what "one step" means for a record on the boundary, the answer is one turn of that circle, and the direction of the turn is fixed by a sign that the shape's spectrum forces. Time is the count of those turns. It has a smallest step — $N_{\max}$ Compton periods of the electron, about a sixth of an attosecond — and it cannot be run backwards, not because the geometry forbids it but because the dynamics on it is contractive one way and not the other.

## What this lecture will derive (from the register — prose to be written)

1. The generator: the geometry-forced linear conformal Hamiltonian $J$ and its two Wick faces, $e^{i\tau J}$ (unitary evolution, Lecture 3 rule 3) and $e^{-\tau J}$ (the contractive commit, rule 6). One operator; time is its parameter.
2. The arrow: positivity of the spectrum makes the contractive face a semigroup — it has no inverse. This is *irr_arrow*, the geometric arrow. The thermodynamic cost of unwriting an amplified record is a second theorem (the companion paper) and is not confused with it.
3. Why the arrow is dynamical and not geometric: a CPT-mirror of the substrate is impossible (the positive-time ontology); the geometry has no arrow — the dynamics on it does.
4. The tick: $\tau_0 = N_{\max}\,\hbar/(m_e c^2) = a_0/c$. Stated in T1136's own convention with the 2π pinned in the sentence: a push is 137 *radians*, about 21.8 writes. (One of us wrote "137 turns = 1.1 as" in September; that was a convention slip, corrected the same week.)
5. What the clock does *not* carry: a laboratory prediction of $\alpha$ drift (withdrawn, Lecture 8) and an initialisation vector that survives the reset between cycles (floored: the survivor is a distribution on the winding count, four to twelve bits, and nothing lawful consults it).

## What would make this lecture wrong

A physical process that reverses a committed record at zero cost — an inverse to the contractive face — would falsify the arrow as derived. A measured tick different from $a_0/c$ in the program's own convention would falsify T1136. Neither is a near-term laboratory test and we do not dress them as one.
